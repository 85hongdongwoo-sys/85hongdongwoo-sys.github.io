The Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme (still widely called the First Home Guarantee) lets eligible first home buyers purchase with a 5% deposit and pay no lenders mortgage insurance. Since 1 October 2025 there are no income caps and no limit on places, and price caps were raised — to $1.5 million in Sydney and $950,000 in Melbourne, with Darwin lifted to $750,000 from 1 July 2026.

What changed — and what is the rule now?

The Home Guarantee Scheme, run by Housing Australia, used to ration places and cap applicants’ income at $125,000 (singles) and $200,000 (couples). From 1 October 2025 the government brought forward its expansion: the guarantee is now open to every eligible first home buyer, with no income test and unlimited places. The Regional First Home Buyer Guarantee closed to new applications on the same date, and its role was folded into the main scheme. In July 2026 the cap for Darwin rose from $600,000 to $750,000 and four more NSW regional markets (Central Coast, Coffs Harbour–Grafton, Mid North Coast and Richmond–Tweed) joined the $1.5 million cap.

How the guarantee works

Normally a lender charges LMI above 80% LVR. Under the scheme the government guarantees the slice of your loan between your deposit and 20% of the value — so a participating lender treats a 95% loan as if it were 80% and charges no LMI. You still borrow (and repay) the full 95%; the guarantee only replaces the insurance.

Eligibility checklist (2026)

  • Australian citizen or permanent resident, aged 18 or over; one or two applicants (partners, friends or family).
  • First home buyer, or no interest in Australian property in the previous 10 years.
  • Deposit of at least 5% (2% for single parents under the single-parent stream) and less than 20%.
  • You must live in the home: move in within six months and stay while the guarantee applies.
  • Owner-occupied, principal-and-interest loan of up to 30 years with a participating lender.
  • Price — and the lender’s valuation — at or below the cap for the postcode.

Worked example: an $800,000 home

With the scheme, a buyer needs a $40,000 deposit plus purchase costs and borrows $760,000. At 6.49% over 30 years that is $4,799 a month ($2,214 a fortnight). Without the scheme, the same 95% loan would attract indicative LMI of about $34,983 (one lender’s May 2026 table) — added to the loan, that would cost another $221 a month for 30 years. Compared with saving a 20% deposit ($160,000), the trade-off is a bigger loan: $152,769 more interest over 30 years than an 80% loan at the same rate.

Watch-outs

Bank assessment still applies. The guarantee removes LMI; it does not raise your borrowing power. The lender still tests repayments at the loan rate plus APRA’s 3-point buffer (9.49% today).
Thin equity. With 5% equity, a small fall in values can leave you owing more than the home is worth, which makes refinancing harder.
Stamp duty is separate. Combine the scheme with your state’s first-home concession — see stamp duty by state.

FAQ

Is there still an income cap on the First Home Guarantee?

No. For applications from 1 October 2025 there are no income caps under the 5% Deposit Scheme, and the number of places is unlimited.

What are the 5% Deposit Scheme price caps in 2026?

Capital city and listed regional centre caps are NSW $1,500,000, VIC $950,000, QLD $1,000,000, WA $850,000, SA $900,000, TAS $700,000, ACT $1,000,000 and NT $750,000 (Darwin, from 1 July 2026). Rest-of-state caps are lower.

Do I pay LMI with a 5% deposit under the scheme?

No. The government guarantee covers the part of the loan above 80% of the value, so participating lenders do not charge lenders mortgage insurance.

What are repayments on an $800,000 home with a 5% deposit?

You would borrow $760,000; at the 6.49% average variable rate over 30 years that is about $4,799 a month.

Can I use the scheme for an investment property?

No. You must live in the property as your home and take a principal-and-interest owner-occupier loan.
