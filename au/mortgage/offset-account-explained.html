How an offset account works

An offset is an ordinary transaction or savings account linked to your mortgage. Each day the lender subtracts the account balance from the loan balance before calculating interest. Your required repayment does not fall; instead, less of it goes to interest and more to principal, so the loan shrinks faster. Your money stays fully accessible.

Offset vs high-interest savings

Offset “earns” your loan rate — 6.49% — and it is not taxable income, because you are saving interest, not earning it. A savings account paying 4.5% leaves a taxpayer on the 30% bracket (plus 2% Medicare) with about 3.06%. To match an offset, a savings account would need to pay around 9.54% before tax. That is why households who keep an emergency fund often hold it in an offset.

Offset vs redraw

Redraw lets you pull back extra repayments you have made. It saves the same interest, but the money legally becomes part of the loan: lenders can limit or freeze redraw, it may carry fees, and — importantly for future investors — funds redrawn for investment can muddy the tax deductibility of interest if you later rent the home out. An offset keeps savings separate.

Is the offset worth the fee?

Offset accounts usually come with a “package” loan that costs around $300–$400 a year or carries a slightly higher rate. A $395 annual fee is worth it if your average offset balance saves more than that — at 6.49% the break-even is an average balance of about $6,086. A loan priced 0.10 points higher on $600,000 costs about $600 a year, which needs a balance of roughly $9,245 to recover.

Tips

Have your pay deposited into the offset and pay bills by credit card (paid in full) so cash stays in the offset as long as possible.
Use multiple offset sub-accounts if your lender allows, to keep bills, holidays and emergency money separate.
Check fixed-rate limits. Many fixed loans offer partial or no offset.

FAQ

How does a mortgage offset account work?

The balance of the linked account is subtracted from your loan balance before interest is calculated each day. You pay less interest, your repayment stays the same, and the extra goes to principal.

How much does $50,000 in an offset save?

On a $600,000, 30-year loan at 6.49%, a constant $50,000 offset balance saves about $233,655 of interest and pays the loan off 5 years 1 month sooner.

Is an offset account better than a savings account?

For most taxpayers, yes. Offset savings equal your loan rate (6.49%) and are tax-free, whereas savings interest is taxed at your marginal rate.

What is the difference between offset and redraw?

Both save interest. Offset money stays in a separate account you own; redraw money is extra repayments inside the loan that the lender can restrict, and using it can complicate tax if the property later becomes an investment.

Do fixed-rate loans have offset accounts?

Some do, often partial (e.g. 40%) or capped. Most full 100% offsets are on variable loans.
