How interest-only works

On an interest-only (IO) loan you pay just the interest charged each month for an agreed period — usually up to five years for owner-occupiers and up to five to ten for investors. The balance does not fall. When the IO period ends the loan converts to principal and interest (P&I) over the remaining term, which is shorter, so repayments rise sharply.

When interest-only can make sense

  • Investors who want to maximise tax-deductible debt on an investment property while paying down their non-deductible home loan.
  • Temporary cash-flow squeezes — parental leave, a career break, or bridging between selling and buying.
  • Construction loans, where IO applies during the build.

The risks

Payment shock. The jump from $3,245 to $4,047 is 25% — if rates have also risen by then, it is larger.
No equity build-up. If values fall, you have only your deposit as a buffer.
Harder to roll over. Lenders reassess you (at the rate + 3-point buffer) before extending an IO period, and many will not.
Not allowed under the 5% Deposit Scheme, which requires P&I owner-occupier loans.

A middle option

If you want flexibility without paying more interest overall, take a P&I loan with an offset account: you can build savings in the offset in tight years and still keep the balance falling.

FAQ

What is the difference between interest-only and principal and interest?

Interest-only repayments cover just the interest, so the loan balance does not fall. Principal-and-interest repayments cover interest plus part of the loan, so the balance falls to zero by the end of the term.

How much is interest-only on $600,000?

At 6.49% it is $3,245 a month, compared with $3,788 on a 30-year principal-and-interest loan.

What happens when my interest-only period ends?

The loan reverts to principal and interest over the remaining term. On $600,000 at 6.49%, after five years IO the repayment rises to about $4,047 a month over the remaining 25 years.

Are interest-only rates higher?

Usually. RBA lending-rate data in August 2026 showed investor interest-only loans priced about 0.2 percentage points above principal-and-interest loans.

Can first home buyers get interest-only loans?

Some lenders allow it, but the 5% Deposit Scheme requires principal-and-interest repayments, and lenders assess IO applications more strictly.

  • 5% Deposit Scheme (First Home Guarantee): Housing Australia rules from 1 October 2025 — unlimited places, no income caps; Darwin cap raised to $750,000 from 1 July 2026.
  • Repayments use standard amortisation (monthly, fortnightly or weekly compounding to match the payment) with the rate held constant for the whole term; real loans calculate interest daily and rates change.
