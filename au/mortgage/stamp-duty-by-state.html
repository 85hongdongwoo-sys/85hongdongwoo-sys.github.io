Stamp duty (transfer duty) is a state tax on buying property, and it is usually the largest upfront cost after the deposit. Each state sets its own rates and first-home concessions. In 2026, WA raised its first-home thresholds to $600,000/$800,000, Tasmania ended its established-home exemption, and the ACT moved to remove duty for eligible first home buyers. Always confirm with the state revenue office before you sign.

How stamp duty works

Duty is charged on the dutiable value — generally the higher of the price and market value — on a sliding scale, so the average rate rises with the price. It is paid within weeks of settlement (or contract, in some states) and cannot normally be added to the loan unless the lender agrees to a higher LVR. That is why duty often decides whether a buyer can reach 20% equity.

For a sense of scale, Victoria’s general rate on a $750,000 home is $40,070 ($2,870 plus 6% of the value above $130,000, SRO Victoria scale) — more than 5% of the price, or almost a quarter of a 20% deposit. A first home buyer in Victoria paying $600,000 pays nothing.

2026 changes worth knowing

Western Australia

From 7 May 2026, WA lifted the first home owner rate of duty: no duty up to $600,000 (previously $500,000) and a concession up to $800,000 (previously $700,000). Vacant land thresholds rose to $450,000/$550,000 (WA Government media statement).

Tasmania

The 100% exemption for first home buyers of established homes up to $750,000 ended on 30 June 2026; settlement, not contract date, decided eligibility. Buyers now pay the standard scale unless another concession applies.

ACT

The ACT has been replacing stamp duty with general rates for over a decade. From 1 July 2026 eligible first home buyers are reported to pay no duty; check income and eligibility rules with the ACT Revenue Office.

Queensland

Since 1 May 2025, first home buyers of a new home (or vacant land to build on) pay no transfer duty at any price. Established homes keep the first-home concession up to $800,000.

How stamp duty affects your loan

Every dollar of duty comes out of savings that would otherwise go to the deposit. Take a $750,000 purchase: if duty consumes $25,000 of your savings, the loan rises by the same amount — about $158 a month at 6.49%, or, worse, it can tip the LVR above 80% and trigger LMI. Combining a state concession with the 5% Deposit Scheme is how most first home buyers keep upfront costs manageable.

Other upfront costs

  • Transfer registration and mortgage registration fees (a few hundred dollars each, set by the state).
  • Conveyancing or solicitor fees, typically $1,000–$2,500.
  • Building and pest inspection, strata report for units.
  • Loan establishment or valuation fees, and LMI if your LVR is above 80%.
  • Foreign purchasers pay an additional surcharge duty in every state.

FAQ

How much is stamp duty in Australia?

It depends on the state and price. As an example, Victoria’s general duty on a $750,000 home is $40,070, while an eligible first home buyer in Victoria pays nothing up to $600,000. Use the official state revenue office calculator for exact figures.

Which states have no stamp duty for first home buyers?

Every state has some relief except Tasmania (for established homes since 1 July 2026) and the NT. NSW exempts first homes up to $800,000, Victoria up to $600,000, Queensland up to $700,000 for established homes and with no cap for new homes, WA up to $600,000, SA for new homes, and the ACT reportedly for eligible first home buyers from 1 July 2026.

Did WA change first home buyer stamp duty in 2026?

Yes. From 7 May 2026 WA first home buyers pay no duty up to $600,000 and a concessional rate up to $800,000.

Can stamp duty be added to my home loan?

Usually it must come from savings. Some lenders will lend extra if your LVR allows, but that can push you above 80% LVR and trigger LMI.

Is the Tasmanian first home buyer stamp duty exemption still available?

No. The exemption for established homes up to $750,000 ended for settlements after 30 June 2026.
