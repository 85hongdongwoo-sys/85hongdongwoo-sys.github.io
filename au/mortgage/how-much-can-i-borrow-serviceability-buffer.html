Australian banks must check that you could still repay your loan if the rate were 3 percentage points higher. With the average variable rate at 6.49%, that means an assessment rate of about 9.49%. If your budget can spare $5,000 a month for repayments, the buffer cuts the maximum 30-year loan from about $791,900 to $595,100. From February 2026 APRA also limits loans at six or more times income.

What is the serviceability buffer?

The buffer is set by APRA, the prudential regulator, and banks apply it as a minimum. APRA raised it from 2.5 to 3 percentage points in October 2021 and has kept it there since — most recently confirming it in its May 2026 review, noting that households remain highly indebted and cash-flow pressure has grown with higher rates. The idea is simple: a borrower who could just afford today’s repayment would be in trouble after a few rate rises — exactly what happened in 2026.

What banks count

  • Income: base salary in full; overtime, bonuses, commissions and rental income are often shaded to 50–80%.
  • Living expenses: your declared spending or the Household Expenditure Measure (HEM) benchmark, whichever is higher.
  • Other debts: car loans, HECS-HELP repayments, Buy Now Pay Later and — crucially — credit-card limits (usually 3–3.8% of the limit a month), even if the balance is zero.
  • Existing mortgages: also assessed at a buffered rate.

APRA’s debt-to-income (DTI) limit from 2026

From 1 February 2026, each bank may make no more than 20% of its new owner-occupier lending (and separately 20% of investor lending) to borrowers whose total debt is six or more times their gross income. Loans to buy or build new homes are exempt. It is a portfolio “speed limit”, not a hard cap on each borrower, but it means borrowing above 6× income has become harder to get.

Five ways to borrow more (safely)

Cut card limits you do not use — a $10,000 limit can reduce borrowing power by tens of thousands.
Clear small debts such as car loans or BNPL before applying.
Apply jointly if you are buying with a partner — two tax-free thresholds raise combined take-home pay.
Choose a 30-year term for assessment; you can still repay faster.
Use the 5% Deposit Scheme to avoid LMI, though it does not change the buffer test.

FAQ

What is the APRA serviceability buffer in 2026?

Three percentage points above the loan rate. APRA kept it at 3 points in its May 2026 review, so a 6.49% variable loan is assessed at about 9.49%.

How much can I borrow on $100,000 a year?

It depends on your expenses, debts and household size. As a reference, six times income ($600,000) is the threshold for APRA’s high debt-to-income limit, and many single applicants with no other debts are assessed somewhat below that at current rates.

What is the debt-to-income limit?

From 1 February 2026 banks may make no more than 20% of new owner-occupier loans (and separately investor loans) at a debt-to-income ratio of six or more. Loans for new homes are exempt.

Why does my bank assess me at a higher rate than I will pay?

APRA requires lenders to check you could keep paying if rates rose by 3 percentage points. It reduces the risk of borrowers falling behind when rates rise.

Do credit card limits reduce borrowing power?

Yes. Lenders count a monthly commitment of roughly 3% or more of the card limit, even if you pay it off in full each month.
