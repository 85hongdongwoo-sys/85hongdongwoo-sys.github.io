What $1m costs at November 2026 rates

The Reserve Bank has lifted the cash rate four times in 2026, from 3.60% to 4.60%, most recently on 29 September; the big four passed that rise on in full from 9 October. Canstar estimated the average owner-occupier variable rate at 6.49% once lenders caught up, although sharper lenders still advertise from 5.89% and the average across every advertised product is higher at 6.75%. On $1,000,000, each 0.25-point step moves the 30-year repayment by about $165 a month, and the gap between the cheapest and the average rate is worth $389 a month.

Sydney and inner-Melbourne territory. A $1m loan usually means a $1,250,000 purchase with 20% down — well above the average new loan of $731,000. Lenders will look closely at your debt-to-income ratio: $1,000,000 at APRA’s DTI threshold of 6 implies at least $166,667 of gross household income before a loan stops counting as “high DTI”, and banks may only write 20% of new owner-occupier loans above that line.

25 vs 30 years: total interest on $1,000,000

Shortening the term is the most powerful way to cut interest — if the budget allows. At 6.49% a 30-year term costs $432 a month less than 25 years, but total interest climbs from $1,023,747 to $1,273,078 — an extra $249,331. A 20-year term would cost $704 a month more than 25 years and save another $235,784.

Principal & interest vs interest-only on $1m

Interest-only (IO) periods of up to five years are mainly used by investors and by owner-occupiers managing a temporary squeeze, such as parental leave. At 6.49% the IO repayment on $1,000,000 is $5,408 a month, $906 less than principal and interest. The catch: after five years you still owe all $1,000,000, and the loan has only 25 years left to clear it, so the repayment jumps to $6,746 — $1,337 a month more than during the IO period, and $432 more than if you had paid P&I from day one. Over the life of the loan you pay about $75,169 extra interest, before any IO rate premium (RBA data show investor interest-only loans priced roughly 0.2 points above P&I in August 2026).

Monthly vs fortnightly vs weekly repayments on $1,000,000

Most Australian lenders let you choose how often you pay. If you choose a true fortnightly repayment the lender works out $2,913 — the 30-year loan simply split into 26 smaller payments a year — and it saves only a little interest. The trick is paying half the monthly amount, $3,157, every fortnight: 26 halves equal 13 monthly payments a year, so the loan is gone in about 24 years 2 months instead of 30 years and you save about $292,775 of interest. Paying a quarter of the monthly amount ($1,579) weekly works almost the same: about 24 years 1 month and $294,010 saved.

How an offset account changes a $1m loan

An offset account is a transaction account linked to the loan: the balance is subtracted before interest is charged, so $50,000 of savings against $1,000,000 means you pay interest on $950,000 — while the repayment stays at $6,314, and the extra goes straight off the principal. Because the saving equals your loan rate and is not taxed, it is worth more than a savings account paying 6.49% before tax to anyone who pays income tax.

What income do you need for a $1,000,000 mortgage?

Australian lenders do not use a fixed income multiple. They test whether you could still pay at the assessment rate — the loan rate plus APRA’s 3-point serviceability buffer, 9.49% here — after your declared living expenses (or the Household Expenditure Measure, if higher), other debts and credit-card limits. At 9.49% the 30-year repayment on $1,000,000 would be $8,401 a month, $2,087 more than you would actually pay today.

  • 30% rule of thumb: keeping the real repayment ($6,314) under 30% of gross pay needs about $252,600 a year — a single earner on that salary takes home about $13,999 a month in 2026-27, so the repayment is 45% of take-home. A couple on $126,300 each takes home $15,892 together (40%).
  • APRA debt-to-income limit: since 1 February 2026 banks may write only 20% of new owner-occupier loans at a DTI of 6 or more. $1,000,000 ÷ 6 = $166,700 of gross income before this loan alone becomes “high DTI”.

Deposit, LMI and the 5% Deposit Scheme

With a 20% deposit — $250,000 on a $1,250,000 home — no lenders mortgage insurance applies. Borrow more than 80% of the value and the lender passes on an LMI premium, which is usually added to the loan. Tips to cut the cost of a $1m home loan

Haggle with your existing lender. Lenders often price new customers below existing ones. On $1m, getting from 6.49% to 5.99% saves about $325 a month — ask for a rate review before refinancing, then compare with the 5.89% lowest variable rate on the market.
Pay fortnightly, not “fortnightly equivalent”. Paying half the monthly amount every fortnight makes 26 half-payments a year — one extra month annually. On this loan that clears it 5 years 10 months sooner and saves about $292,775 interest at 6.49%.
Park savings in an offset. Every dollar in an offset account saves interest at your loan rate, tax-free. $50,000 sitting in an offset against $1m saves about $255,820 of interest over the loan and finishes it 3 years 4 months early.
Build a buffer before the next rise. The RBA meets again at 2:30pm AEDT on Tuesday 3 November 2026. Paying the repayment you would owe at 6.99% — $6,646 instead of $6,314 — builds a redraw buffer now and means two more rises would not change your budget.
Keep the term at 25 years if you can. A 30-year term lowers the monthly repayment by $432 but adds $249,331 of interest. You can take 30 years for flexibility and still pay the 25-year amount voluntarily.

FAQ

What are the repayments on a $1,000,000 home loan?

At the 6.49% average owner-occupier variable rate (Canstar, after the September 2026 RBA rise) a $1,000,000 principal-and-interest loan costs about $6,314 a month over 30 years or $6,746 over 25 years. Fortnightly over 30 years it is $2,913; weekly $1,456.

How much income do I need for a $1m home loan?

As a rule of thumb, keeping the $6,314 monthly repayment to 30% of gross income needs about $252,600 a year. Banks assess at 9.49% (the rate plus APRA’s 3-point buffer), where the repayment would be $8,401 a month, and subtract your living costs and other debts. To stay below a debt-to-income ratio of 6 you would need at least $166,700 of gross income if this is your only debt.

How much interest will I pay on $1,000,000?

About $1,273,078 over 30 years or $1,023,747 over 25 years at 6.49%, if the rate never changed. Paying half the monthly repayment every fortnight cuts the 30-year interest by about $292,775.

What are interest-only repayments on $1,000,000?

$5,408 a month at 6.49%. If you take 5 years interest-only on a 30-year loan, repayments then jump to $6,746 for the remaining 25 years, and total interest rises to about $1,348,247 versus $1,273,078 on principal and interest. Lenders usually charge a higher rate for interest-only.

How much is LMI on a $1m loan?

Using one lender’s indicative premium table (May 2026), LMI on $1,000,000 is roughly $14,070 at 85% LVR, $25,160 at 90% and $46,030 at 95%. There is no LMI with a 20% deposit or under the 5% Deposit Scheme.

Can I use the 5% Deposit Scheme for a $1m loan?

A $1,000,000 loan with a 5% deposit buys about $1,052,632, which is within the First Home Guarantee price cap in: NSW (Sydney + Newcastle, Lake Macquarie, Illawarra, Central Coast, Coffs Harbour–Grafton, Mid North Coast, Richmond–Tweed). Under the scheme there is no LMI, no income cap and unlimited places (from 1 October 2025).

Assumptions & sources

  • RBA cash rate target 4.60%: raised 0.25 points on 29 September 2026 (the fourth rise of 2026, after February, March and May). The Monetary Policy Board announces its next decision at 2:30pm AEDT on Tuesday 3 November 2026; most bank economists expected another 0.25-point rise.
  • Average owner-occupier variable rate (P&I) 6.49%: Canstar estimate once the September rise was passed on (29 September 2026), in line with RBA Table F6 — new owner-occupier variable loans averaged 6.23% in August 2026, before the rise. Canstar’s average advertised rate across its whole database was 6.75% (13 October 2026) and the lowest variable rate was 5.89% (20 October 2026).
  • Fixed rates: big-four 1–2 year fixed rates ranged from 6.49% (ANZ) to 6.81% (NAB 2-year) in October 2026; the lowest fixed rate on Canstar was 5.79% (1-year).
  • APRA serviceability buffer: 3 percentage points above the loan rate (kept unchanged in APRA’s May 2026 review). From 1 February 2026 no more than 20% of a bank’s new owner-occupier lending (and separately investor lending) may be at a debt-to-income ratio of 6 or more.
  • LMI: indicative full-doc premiums from one lender’s table published by Home Loan Experts (updated 18 May 2026); insurers price individually and state stamp duty on the premium may apply (none in NSW).
  • 5% Deposit Scheme (First Home Guarantee): Housing Australia rules from 1 October 2025 — unlimited places, no income caps; Darwin cap raised to $750,000 from 1 July 2026.
  • Repayments use standard amortisation (monthly, fortnightly or weekly compounding to match the payment) with the rate held constant for the whole term; real loans calculate interest daily and rates change.
