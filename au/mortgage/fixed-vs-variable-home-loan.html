Where rates stand (early November 2026)

The Reserve Bank lifted the cash rate in February, March, May and September 2026, taking it from 3.60% to 4.60%. Lenders passed the September rise on in full from 9 October. Canstar estimated the average owner-occupier variable rate at 6.49%, with the cheapest lenders from 5.89%. On the fixed side, every big-four lender raised fixed rates in October: ANZ’s 1- and 2-year rates of 6.49% were the lowest of the four, while NAB’s 2-year rate reached 6.81%. A handful of smaller lenders still offered fixed rates below 6% (the lowest on Canstar was 5.79% for one year). The RBA’s next decision is at 2:30pm AEDT on Tuesday 3 November 2026.

What you give up when you fix

  • Break costs: leave a fixed loan early (sell, refinance) and you may pay an economic break cost if rates have fallen since you fixed — sometimes thousands of dollars.
  • Extra repayment caps: many fixed loans allow only $10,000–$30,000 of extra repayments a year.
  • Limited offset: few fixed loans offer a full 100% offset.
  • Revert rate: when the fixed term ends the loan rolls to the lender’s standard variable rate, often well above its best new-customer rate — diarise the expiry date.

Split loans: the middle path

A split loan fixes part of the balance and leaves the rest variable. A 50/50 split on $600,000 gives payment certainty on half while keeping an offset and unlimited extra repayments on the other half. Many borrowers fix the amount they could not afford to see rise, and keep variable the amount they plan to pay down quickly.

Who should fix?

Fixing suits households with little buffer, a tight budget (for example on one income during parental leave), or who simply value certainty. Variable suits borrowers who make extra repayments, use an offset, may sell within the fixed term, or think rates are near their peak. Neither is a bet you can reliably win: fixed rates already price in the rate rises markets expect.

Whichever you choose, remember that the bank assesses your application at the higher of the loan rate plus 3 points — 9.49% on the average variable rate — so your borrowing power is similar either way.

FAQ

Should I fix my home loan in November 2026?

It depends on your buffer and plans. Big-four 1–2 year fixed rates (6.49%–6.81%) are close to or above the 6.49% average variable rate, so fixing mainly buys certainty against further RBA rises. This is general information, not advice.

What is the difference between fixed and variable home loans?

A fixed rate stays the same for an agreed period (usually 1–5 years); a variable rate moves with the lender’s pricing, usually following RBA cash-rate changes. Variable loans normally allow unlimited extra repayments and a full offset; fixed loans often do not.

What are break costs on a fixed loan?

If you repay or refinance a fixed loan early and wholesale rates have fallen since you fixed, the lender can charge an economic break cost to cover its loss. It can be large on big loans with long fixed terms remaining.

Can I split my home loan between fixed and variable?

Yes. Most lenders allow a split loan, fixing part of the balance and leaving the rest variable with offset and extra-repayment features.

What happens when my fixed rate ends?

The loan reverts to the lender’s standard variable rate, which is often higher than its new-customer rates. Ask for a rate review or compare refinancing offers before expiry.
