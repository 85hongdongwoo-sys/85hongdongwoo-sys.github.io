Fortnightly vs monthly mortgage repayments

Why “half the monthly payment every fortnight” saves years — and why the bank’s fortnightly figure barely does.

Paying fortnightly only saves serious money if you pay half your monthly repayment every fortnight. That makes 26 half-payments — 13 months’ worth — a year. On a $600,000 loan at 6.49% over 30 years, it turns $3,788 a month into $1,894 a fortnight, clears the loan in about 24 years 2 months and saves around $175,665 of interest. The lender’s own “fortnightly” figure ($1,748) saves only $643.

Two very different “fortnightly” repayments

True fortnightly is what a lender calculates when you ask to pay every two weeks: it re-amortises the same loan over 30 years with 26 payments a year. For $600,000 at 6.49% that is $1,748 a fortnight — $45,440 a year versus $45,462 on monthly repayments. Interest is charged slightly earlier off a lower balance, so it saves a little: $643 over the loan, and only under a month sooner.

Half-monthly fortnightly is when you set the fortnightly payment to exactly half the monthly figure, $1,894. Because a year has 26 fortnights rather than 24 half-months, you pay $49,250 a year — one extra monthly repayment of $3,788 — and every extra dollar comes straight off the principal. That is where the big saving comes from.

Weekly repayments

Paying a quarter of the monthly figure every week ($947) adds up to 13 months a year in the same way, and finishes in about 24 years 1 month — a hair quicker than half-monthly fortnightly because each payment lands sooner. The lender-calculated weekly amount ($874) behaves like true fortnightly: small savings only.

Is it the frequency or the extra money?

Almost all of the benefit is the extra repayment, not the timing. You would get nearly the same result by paying monthly and adding $316 a month (one-twelfth of a repayment). Fortnightly simply makes it painless if you are paid fortnightly, because the money leaves your account before you notice it. Australian lenders calculate interest daily and charge it monthly, so timing on its own changes little.

Checklist before switching

Ask for the half-monthly amount explicitly. Many banking apps default to the lower true-fortnightly figure.
Fixed loans may cap extra repayments (often $10,000–$30,000 a year). Variable loans usually allow unlimited extra repayments.
Keep the redraw or offset. Extra repayments can usually be redrawn if needed — check fees and minimums.

FAQ

Do fortnightly mortgage repayments save money?

Yes, if you pay half the monthly repayment every fortnight. On $600,000 at 6.49% over 30 years that saves about $175,665 and cuts the loan to roughly 24 years 2 months. The lender’s true-fortnightly amount saves only about $643.

Why do fortnightly repayments pay off a loan faster?

There are 26 fortnights in a year, so 26 half-monthly payments equal 13 monthly repayments. The extra month each year goes straight to the principal.

What is the fortnightly repayment on a $600,000 loan?

Half the monthly repayment is $1,894 a fortnight at 6.49% over 30 years. A lender-calculated true fortnightly repayment would be $1,748.

Are weekly repayments better than fortnightly?

Only marginally. A quarter of the monthly repayment each week finishes slightly sooner than half each fortnight, because money arrives earlier, but the difference is small compared with the extra 13th payment both methods create.
