Hourly to annual salary in Australia: $20 an hour by pay period

To convert an hourly rate into an annual salary in Australia, multiply it by your weekly hours and by 52. The National Employment Standards set a maximum of 38 ordinary hours a week for full-time staff, so $20 × 38 × 52 = $39,520. A day is 7.6 hours; paid annual leave and public holidays are already inside the 52 weeks for permanent employees. Most Australians are paid fortnightly, so that column is the one to check against your payslip.

How $20 an hour is taxed in 2026-27

From 1 July 2026 the first resident tax rate fell from 16% to 15% (rates: nil to $18,200; 15% to $45,000; 30% to $135,000; 37% to $190,000; 45% above). On $39,520 that means $21,320 at 15% — $3,198 before offsets. The low income tax offset then removes $599, leaving $2,599. The 2% Medicare levy adds $790. Compared with 2025-26 rates, the 15% cut puts $213 a year ($8 a fortnight) back in your pocket. Your marginal rate on the next dollar is 22% including Medicare.

You pay no income tax until yearly income passes $18,200 — at $20 that is 17.5 hours a week. You would need more than 60 hours a week to reach the $69,528 HELP threshold. The 37% bracket ($135,000+) is out of reach at any realistic number of hours.

Is $20 an hour legal in Australia? For an adult employee, generally no. From 1 July 2026 the national minimum wage is $26.44 an hour ($1,004.90 for a 38-hour week), and casuals covered by it must get at least $33.05 including the 25% casual loading. $20 is $6.44 an hour short — about $12,725 a year on full-time hours. Lower rates can be lawful for junior employees (under 21), apprentices, trainees and workers on the supported wage system, and some modern awards set different junior percentages. If you are 21 or older and not in one of those groups, check your award with the Fair Work Ombudsman’s Pay Calculator: underpayments can be recovered.

HELP / HECS repayments on $20 an hour

From the 2026-27 income year, compulsory HELP repayments use a marginal system: nothing up to $69,528 of repayment income, then 15c for each dollar above it, $9,028 plus 17c per dollar above $129,717, and 10% of total income above $186,050 (ATO). At 38 hours your $39,520 is below the threshold, so a HELP debt is not repaid through payroll — but indexation still applies each 1 June. Repayment income also adds reportable fringe benefits, reportable super contributions and net investment losses, so salary sacrifice does not dodge HELP.

Casual loading: is $25 really more?

A casual employee gets a 25% loading instead of paid annual leave (4 weeks), personal/carer’s leave (10 days) and notice. At $20 base that means $25 an hour. Work 38 hours for a full 52 weeks and the casual earns $49,400 — $9,880 more than a permanent — but take the same 6 weeks off unpaid and the casual year is $43,700, still $4,180 ahead of the permanent’s $39,520. Super is 12% of ordinary pay for both.

$20 an hour vs the minimum wage and the ABS median

Tips to keep more of $20 an hour

Check the award, not just the minimum. Use the Fair Work Ombudsman’s Pay and Conditions Tool (PACT) to find your award classification. Most award rates rose 4.75% from 1 July 2026, and penalty rates apply for weekends, evenings and public holidays in many awards.
Claim the tax-free threshold once. Tick “yes” to the $18,200 tax-free threshold on your TFN declaration for your main job only. With two jobs, claiming it on both usually leaves a tax bill at the end of the year.
Watch your super land — Payday Super. From 1 July 2026 employers must pay the 12% super guarantee at the same time as wages and it must reach your fund within 7 business days. At $20 on 38 hours that is about $182 a fortnight — check your fund app matches your payslip.
Casual for 6 months? Ask about conversion. Casual employees can notify their employer that they want to become permanent after 6 months (12 months with a small business) if their work has been regular. You give up the 25% loading but gain paid leave and notice.
Overtime and penalty rates. Each extra ordinary hour at $20 adds about $15.60 after tax and Medicare. Award overtime is often paid at 150% or 200%, so check your payslip shows the right multiplier.

FAQ

$20 an hour is how much a year?

At 38 hours a week for 52 weeks (1,976 hours), $20 an hour is $39,520 a year before tax. At 30 hours it is $31,200 and at 20 hours $20,800.

How much is $20 an hour after tax in Australia?

In 2026-27 a full-time $20 an hour job leaves $36,131 a year after $2,599 income tax and a $790 Medicare levy — $1,390 a fortnight, $695 a week or about $18.28 an hour. Your employer also pays $4,742 super.

How much is $20 an hour per fortnight after tax?

About $1,390 a fortnight on 38 hours a week. Gross fortnightly pay is $1,520.

Is $20 an hour above the minimum wage in Australia?

No. From 1 July 2026 the national minimum wage for adults is $26.44 an hour ($1,004.90 a week). $20 can only be lawful for junior employees, apprentices, trainees or supported-wage workers.

What is $20 an hour as a casual?

With the 25% casual loading, a $20 base rate becomes $25 an hour. Full time that is $49,400 a year and $43,331 after tax, but casuals get no paid annual or personal leave.

How much HELP do I repay on $20 an hour?

Nothing on 38 hours a week, because $39,520 is below the 2026-27 HELP repayment threshold of $69,528.
