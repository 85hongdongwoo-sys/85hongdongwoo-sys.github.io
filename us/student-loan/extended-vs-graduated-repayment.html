Extended vs Graduated Repayment Plans

Two older fixed-schedule plans let you lower your payment without income paperwork. They remain available for loans made before July 2026 but are closed to new loans. Here is what each costs.

Short answer

The graduated plan keeps a 10-year payoff but starts with lower payments that rise about every two years. The extended plan stretches repayment up to 25 years with fixed or graduated payments, and requires more than $30,000 in Direct Loans. Both lower early payments at the cost of more interest, neither offers forgiveness, and they’re available only for loans disbursed before July 1, 2026.

Under federal rules a graduated payment can’t be less than the monthly interest or more than three times any other payment. The exact schedule comes from your servicer; our figures use an illustrative five-step model.

Who should consider them

  • Graduated: new graduates who expect steady raises and want to be debt-free in 10 years.
  • Extended: borrowers with large balances who need a lower fixed payment and don’t want to recertify income every year.
  • Neither: public-service workers (they don’t count for PSLF) and low-income borrowers (RAP or IBR is usually cheaper).
New loans change everything: taking out or consolidating a loan on or after July 1, 2026 limits you to the Tiered Standard plan or RAP.

The graduated plan costs only modestly more than the standard plan because it still ends in 10 years. The extended plan costs far more because interest runs for 25 years. If you need a lower payment mainly because your income is low right now, RAP or IBR may lower it further while counting toward PSLF — and you can move back to a fixed plan later if your income rises, as long as all your loans predate July 2026.

Switching between plans is done through your servicer or StudentAid.gov. Before you switch, ask whether any unpaid interest would be capitalized and how the change affects your forgiveness count.

Frequently asked questions

What is the extended repayment plan?

A plan for borrowers with more than $30,000 in Direct Loans that spreads repayment over up to 25 years with fixed or graduated payments. It is available only for loans disbursed before July 1, 2026.

How does graduated repayment work?

Payments start lower and increase about every two years, with the loan paid off within 10 years. No payment can be less than the monthly interest or more than three times any other payment.

Do extended or graduated plans qualify for PSLF?

No. Only income-driven plans (such as RAP or IBR) and the 10-year standard plan produce qualifying payments.

Can new borrowers choose the extended or graduated plan?

No. Loans first disbursed on or after July 1, 2026 can only use the Tiered Standard plan or RAP.
