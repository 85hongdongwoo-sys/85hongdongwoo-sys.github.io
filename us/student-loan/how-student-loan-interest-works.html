How Student Loan Interest Works

Interest on federal student loans is simple, daily and fixed — but it starts earlier than many borrowers expect. Here is the formula, worked examples at 2026-27 rates, and how to keep interest from growing your balance.

Short answer

Federal student loans charge simple daily interest: each day, your principal balance is multiplied by your fixed annual rate divided by the number of days in the year. On $5,500 at 6.52% that is about $0.98 a day. Interest on unsubsidized loans accrues from the day the money is disbursed — including while you’re in school — while the government pays the interest on subsidized loans during school, the six-month grace period and deferment.

The student loan interest formula

Federal servicers use a daily “interest rate factor”: the annual rate divided by the number of days in the year. The interest added between payments is:

Interest = principal balance × (annual rate ÷ days in year) × days since last payment

Example: $5,500 unsubsidized loan at 6.52%. Daily interest ≈ $5,500 × 0.0652 ÷ 365 = $0.98, or about $29.47 in a 30-day month. Because the interest is simple, it is charged on principal only — interest does not earn interest unless it is capitalized (added to principal).

Subsidized vs unsubsidized: interest during school

Undergraduates with financial need can get Direct Subsidized Loans; everyone else (and all graduate students) gets unsubsidized loans. The difference shows up before your first payment:

LoanIn school (4 yrs) + 6-mo graceWho pays it
$5,500 subsidized at 6.52%$0 to youU.S. Department of Education
$5,500 unsubsidized at 6.52%$1,614You (accrues while in school)

Paying the interest while in school — even $30–$50 a month — stops it from being capitalized later. Capitalization now happens in fewer situations than before 2023 (for example it can still follow some deferments), but whenever it happens your future interest is charged on a bigger principal.

Why early payments are mostly interest

On a $30,000 loan at 6.52% over 10 years the payment is $340.95. In year one, $1,891 of the $4,091 you pay is interest; by year ten only $141 is. Total interest: $10,914.

That is why extra payments early in the loan save the most: every extra dollar of principal stops accruing interest for all the remaining years.

How payments are applied

  • Payments go first to any outstanding fees, then to accrued interest, then to principal.
  • Paying before the due date reduces the days of interest charged that cycle.
  • If you have several loans, ask the servicer to put extra payments on the highest-rate loan (usually PLUS or graduate loans).
  • Enrolling in auto-debit typically earns a 0.25% rate reduction on federal loans.
  • On RAP, interest your payment doesn’t cover is waived — it neither accrues nor capitalizes.

Frequently asked questions

Is student loan interest daily or monthly?

Federal student loan interest accrues daily as simple interest on the principal balance and is billed monthly.

Does interest accrue while I’m in school?

On unsubsidized and PLUS loans, yes, from the day each loan is disbursed. On subsidized loans, the government pays the interest while you’re in school at least half time, during the six-month grace period and during deferment.

What is capitalized interest?

Capitalization adds unpaid interest to your principal, so you then pay interest on it. It happens in fewer situations since 2023 rule changes, and on RAP unpaid interest is waived instead.

How much interest accrues on $10,000 a month?

About $54.33 at the 6.52% undergraduate rate, $67.25 at 8.07% and $75.58 at the 9.07% PLUS rate.

Do extra payments reduce interest?

Yes. Any extra amount that goes to principal stops accruing interest immediately, which shortens the loan and cuts total interest.
