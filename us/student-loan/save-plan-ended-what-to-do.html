The SAVE Plan Has Ended: What to Do Now

Millions of borrowers sat in a SAVE forbearance while the plan was fought in court. SAVE is now gone. Here is the timeline, your options, and what each choice means for your payment.

Short answer

SAVE no longer exists. After years of litigation, the plan ended in court in March 2026 and the Education Department stopped enrolling borrowers. Former SAVE borrowers must choose another plan — RAP, IBR or a standard plan — within 90 days of their servicer’s notice. If you don’t choose, the servicer moves you to a plan it selects, which may have a much higher payment.

SAVE plan timeline

Dates per BestColleges (updated June 2026) and Tate Law (updated October 2026). Exact dates in your notice may differ — the 90 days run from the date your servicer contacts you.

Your options after SAVE

  • RAP — 1%–10% of AGI, $10 minimum, interest waived, forgiveness after 30 years. Counts for PSLF.
  • IBR — 10% of income above 150% of the poverty line (15% for pre-2014 loans), capped at the standard payment, forgiveness after 20/25 years. Available if all your loans predate July 1, 2026.
  • Standard plan — fixed payments with no forgiveness. Good if your income is high relative to your balance.
  • PAYE or ICR — closing; both end by July 1, 2028, so few borrowers should choose them now.

Reports differ on the default: BestColleges says borrowers who miss the deadline go to the Tiered Standard plan or another plan the servicer specifies; Tate Law says borrowers with only pre-July-2026 loans may land on the 10-year Standard plan. Either way, the payment is likely higher than an income-driven option.

Checklist for former SAVE borrowers

  • Log in to StudentAid.gov and your servicer account; make sure your email and address are current so you don’t miss the notice.
  • Use the official loan simulator to compare RAP and IBR with your latest tax return.
  • If you are pursuing PSLF, pick the lowest-payment income-driven plan and look at the PSLF buyback for SAVE forbearance months.
  • Consolidating or taking a new loan after July 1, 2026 removes IBR as an option for the new loan — think before you consolidate.
  • Set up auto-debit as soon as the first bill arrives.

Frequently asked questions

Is the SAVE plan over?

Yes. SAVE ended in March 2026 after court rulings, and the Education Department no longer enrolls borrowers in it.

How long do SAVE borrowers have to pick a new plan?

90 days from the date their servicer notifies them. Servicers began sending notices around July 1, 2026.

What happens if I don’t choose a plan after SAVE?

Your servicer places you in a plan — reportedly the Tiered Standard or Standard plan — which usually has a higher payment than an income-driven plan.

Is RAP more expensive than SAVE?

For most borrowers, yes. For example, a single borrower with $40,000 AGI paid about $50 a month on SAVE but pays $100 on RAP.

Can former SAVE borrowers choose IBR?

Yes, if all their loans were disbursed before July 1, 2026. IBR no longer requires a partial financial hardship.
