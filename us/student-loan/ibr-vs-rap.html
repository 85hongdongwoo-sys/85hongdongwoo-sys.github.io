IBR vs RAP: Which Plan Costs Less?

If all your federal loans were made before July 1, 2026 you can still choose Income-Based Repayment. Here is how its payments and forgiveness timeline compare with the new Repayment Assistance Plan.

Short answer

IBR usually wins at low incomes and for large balances: it charges 10% of income above 150% of the poverty line (15% for pre-July-2014 borrowers), can be $0, is capped at the 10-year standard payment and forgives the rest after 20 years (25 for older borrowers). RAP often wins in a middle income band: its 1%–10% of total AGI can come in below IBR’s 10% of discretionary income, and its interest waiver and $50 principal match keep the balance from growing — but forgiveness takes 30 years.

For a single borrower the winner flips back and forth. At the lowest incomes IBR is cheaper — at $25,000 it is $9 vs $42 on RAP — because the poverty-line deduction shields the first $23,940. Between roughly $30,000 and $80,000, RAP’s lower bracket percentages often make it the cheaper plan, though bracket jumps can flip individual incomes (at $45,000: $150 vs $176). Above that, RAP’s rate climbs to 10% of total AGI and IBR wins again, by a wide margin once IBR’s cap at the standard payment ($730 here) kicks in. For a family of three, IBR’s larger poverty-line deduction wins at the bottom, the two plans run close in the middle, and IBR’s cap wins at the top — check the table at your own income.

Which plan should you choose?

  • Pursuing PSLF? Pick whichever gives the lower payment — both count, and the rest is forgiven tax-free after 120 payments.
  • Low income, large balance, no PSLF: IBR’s lower payments and 20-year forgiveness usually cost less in total, even after the tax on forgiveness.
  • Moderate income: compare both — bracket jumps mean either plan can be lower, and RAP’s interest waiver matters if your payment is below the monthly interest.
  • High income relative to debt: IBR’s cap at the standard payment makes it cheaper than RAP’s uncapped 10%; a standard plan or refinancing may be cheaper still.
  • New loans after July 1, 2026: IBR is no longer available for them — your choices are RAP or the Tiered Standard plan.

Frequently asked questions

Is IBR still available in 2026?

Yes, for borrowers whose loans were all disbursed before July 1, 2026. Loans made on or after that date can only use RAP or the Tiered Standard plan.

Which is cheaper, IBR or RAP?

It depends on income and family size. IBR is usually cheaper for low-income single borrowers and very high earners (because of its cap); RAP is often cheaper in a middle-income band (roughly $30,000–$80,000 for a single borrower), though it varies by bracket.

How long until forgiveness on IBR vs RAP?

IBR forgives after 20 years for borrowers who first borrowed after July 1, 2014 (25 years for earlier borrowers). RAP forgives after 360 qualifying payments, or 30 years.

Do I still need a partial financial hardship for IBR?

No. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act removed the partial financial hardship requirement to enroll in IBR.

Do IBR and RAP count for PSLF?

Yes. Payments under both plans count toward the 120 qualifying payments for Public Service Loan Forgiveness.
