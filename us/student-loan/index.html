What changed in 2026

From July 1, 2026, new federal student loans can only be repaid on the Tiered Standard plan (10–25 years by balance) or the income-driven Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP). SAVE has ended, PAYE and ICR are being phased out by July 1, 2028, Grad PLUS is closed to new borrowers, and graduate borrowing is capped at $100,000 ($200,000 for professional programs).

Frequently asked questions

What are federal student loan interest rates for 2026-27?

6.52% for undergraduate Direct Loans, 8.07% for graduate and professional Direct Unsubsidized Loans and 9.07% for PLUS loans first disbursed July 1, 2026–June 30, 2027 (FSA, June 4, 2026). Rates are fixed for the life of each loan.

What is the Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP)?

RAP is the income-driven plan created by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and available from July 1, 2026. Payments are 1% to 10% of adjusted gross income (minimum $10 a month), minus $50 a month per dependent; unpaid interest is waived and remaining balances are forgiven after 360 qualifying payments.

Is the SAVE plan still available?

No. SAVE has ended and borrowers who were enrolled in it must choose another plan (RAP, IBR or a standard plan) after their servicer notifies them.

What is the monthly payment on $30,000 in student loans?

$340.95 a month over 10 years at 6.52%, or $261.66 on the 15-year Tiered Standard plan for new loans.

How much can graduate students borrow after July 1, 2026?

New graduate borrowers can take up to $20,500 a year ($100,000 total); professional students up to $50,000 a year ($200,000 total). Grad PLUS is closed to new borrowers and there is a $257,500 lifetime cap.
