How Much Student Loan Debt Is Too Much?

There is no legal limit on what is “too much,” but two simple rules help you judge whether a balance fits your expected income — before you borrow or while you choose a repayment plan.

Short answer

A common guideline is to keep student loan payments at or below 10% of gross income, which works out to an income of about 120 × your monthly 10-year payment. A second rule: don’t borrow more in total than you expect to earn in your first year after graduation. On $30,000 at 6.52% the 10% rule needs about $41,000 of income.

Balances up to $57,500 use the undergraduate rate; larger balances the graduate rate. If your income is below these levels, an income-driven plan like RAP keeps your payment proportional to income instead.

Rule 2: total debt ≤ first-year salary

If your total student debt is less than your expected starting salary, the 10-year standard plan is usually manageable at roughly 10%–15% of gross pay. Debt of 1.5–2× salary typically requires an income-driven plan, a longer term, or PSLF to stay affordable. These are rules of thumb, not lender requirements — but lenders do count your student loan payment in your debt-to-income ratio for mortgages and car loans.

At $45,000 the standard payment eats 15% of gross pay — well over the guideline — while RAP keeps it near 4%. By $80,000 the standard plan fits the 10% rule and finishing in 10 years saves the most interest. That is the typical path: income-driven early, faster payoff later.

Remember that gross income overstates what you can spend. After federal and state income tax and FICA, a 10%-of-gross payment is often 13%–15% of take-home pay, so check your actual paycheck before committing to an aggressive payoff plan.

What if you are already over the line?

  • Enroll in RAP (or IBR for older loans) so the payment tracks income.
  • Check PSLF if you work for government or a nonprofit.
  • Target extra payments at the highest-rate loans once income rises.
  • Avoid new debt — car loans and credit cards compete for the same income.

Frequently asked questions

How much student loan debt is too much?

As a rule of thumb, debt is high if the 10-year payment exceeds 10% of your gross income or total debt exceeds your expected first-year salary.

What salary do I need for $50,000 in student loans?

About $68,000 a year to keep the 10-year payment at 10% of gross income at 6.52%, or $73,000 at the 8.07% graduate rate.

What is the 10% rule for student loans?

Keep total monthly student loan payments at or below 10% of gross monthly income.

Do student loans affect getting a mortgage?

Yes. Lenders include your student loan payment in your debt-to-income ratio, so a lower payment (for example on RAP) can affect how much you qualify for.
