Should You Refinance Student Loans?

A lower rate is tempting, especially on 8%–9% graduate and PLUS loans. But refinancing a federal loan is a one-way door. Here is how to weigh the savings against the protections you give up.

Short answer

Refinancing replaces your loans with a new private loan, ideally at a lower rate. On $80,000 at 8.07%, every percentage point lower saves several thousand dollars over 10 years. But refinanced federal loans permanently lose RAP and IBR, PSLF, federal deferment and forbearance, and federal discharge options. Refinance only if your income is secure, you won’t use income-driven repayment or forgiveness, and the rate cut is meaningful.

A half-point cut on a small balance saves a few hundred dollars — rarely worth giving up federal protections. A two-point cut on a large graduate or PLUS balance saves five figures, which is where refinancing gets serious for high earners. Shorter private terms (5 or 7 years) save more, but only if you can afford the larger payment.

What you give up when you refinance federal loans

  • Income-driven repayment — RAP and IBR payments that adjust if your income falls.
  • Forgiveness — PSLF (tax-free after 120 payments) and IDR forgiveness.
  • Federal deferment and forbearance for unemployment and economic hardship.
  • Federal discharges — for example death and total and permanent disability discharge on federal terms.
  • Future federal relief — any program Congress or the Department creates applies only to federal loans.
No way back: a private loan can’t be converted to a federal loan later.

When refinancing makes sense

  • You already have private loans — refinancing them costs nothing in federal benefits.
  • Your income is high and stable, your emergency fund is solid, and you plan to pay off within 5–10 years.
  • Your federal loans carry high rates (Grad PLUS or 9.07% PLUS) and the private offer is at least a full point lower.
  • You don’t work, and won’t work, for a government or nonprofit employer.

When to keep federal loans

  • You are pursuing PSLF or could in the future.
  • Your payment on RAP or IBR is much lower than a standard payment.
  • Your job or income is uncertain.
  • The rate difference is small — under about half a point.

A middle path: refinance only part of your debt (for example, high-rate PLUS loans) and keep the rest federal.

Frequently asked questions

Is refinancing student loans a good idea in 2026?

It can be for borrowers with private loans or high-rate federal loans who have stable, high incomes and no plans to use RAP, IBR or PSLF. For most others, the federal protections are worth more than a modest rate cut.

What do I lose if I refinance federal student loans?

Access to RAP and IBR, PSLF and IDR forgiveness, federal deferment and forbearance, and federal discharge programs. These can’t be restored later.

How much could I save by refinancing $80,000?

At 6.00% instead of 8.07% over 10 years, about $10,250 in interest — illustrative only; actual private rates depend on your credit.

Can I refinance only some of my student loans?

Yes. Many borrowers refinance private or high-rate loans and keep lower-rate federal loans for the safety net.
