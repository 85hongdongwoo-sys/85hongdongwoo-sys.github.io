Quick answer

On the classic 10-year standard plan, $40,000 in student loans at the 2026-27 undergraduate rate of 6.52% costs $454.60 a month ($14,552 total interest). New loans first disbursed after July 1, 2026 use the Tiered Standard plan, which sets a 15-year term for this balance: $348.88 a month. On the income-driven Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP) the payment ignores the balance — at $50,000 AGI with no dependents it starts at $167 a month.

Which repayment plans can you use for $40k?

Which of these plans you can use depends on when your loans were disbursed, not on the balance. If every loan you have was first disbursed before July 1, 2026, you keep access to the old 10-year standard plan, the 25-year extended plan (available with more than $30,000 in Direct Loans) and the graduated plan, plus IBR and RAP. If you take a new loan — or consolidate — on or after July 1, 2026, your choices shrink to the Tiered Standard plan and RAP. The tier is set by your total Direct Loan principal when you enter repayment, so $40,000 lands in the 15-year band ($25,000–$49,999).

  • Loans all disbursed before July 1, 2026: 10-year standard, graduated, extended, IBR, RAP (PAYE and ICR are being phased out by July 1, 2028; SAVE has ended).
  • Any loan disbursed or consolidated on/after July 1, 2026: Tiered Standard (15 years for $40,000) or RAP only.
  • Parent PLUS loans: not eligible for RAP.

RAP payment on $40,000 student loans by income

Because RAP charges a percentage of adjusted gross income, two borrowers who both owe $40,000 can have wildly different payments. At $50,000 of AGI the payment is 4% of income, or $167 a month; each dependent knocks off $50. RAP only becomes more expensive than the 10-year standard payment once AGI reaches about $78,000 (single, no dependents). In our projection for a single borrower starting at $50,000 (income +3% a year), the balance is gone in about 17 yr 9 mo, with $70,442 paid in total, $590 of interest waived and $2,293 of government principal matching.

Total interest on $40,000: 10 vs 15 vs 20 vs 25 years

Longer terms lower the bill but raise the price. Moving $40,000 from 10 to 15 years at 6.52% cuts the payment by $106 a month but adds $8,247 of interest. At 25 years, interest reaches $41,175 — 103% of the original balance.

How much income do you need for $40,000 in student loans?

A popular rule of thumb is to keep student loan payments at or below 10% of gross income. For $40,000, that means earning about $55,000 a year to carry the 10-year standard payment, $42,000 for the 15-year tiered payment and $32,000 for the extended plan. A related guideline — don’t borrow more in total than you expect to earn in your first year — points to a starting salary of at least $40,000. If your income is below this today, an income-driven plan can bridge the gap until your salary catches up.

Paying off $40,000 early: extra payments

Paying more than the minimum is the surest way to cut the cost. Adding $100 a month to the 10-year payment on $40,000 at 6.52% clears the debt in 7 yr 8 mo and saves $3,645 of interest; adding $250 finishes in 5 yr 9 mo and saves $6,593. Ask your servicer to apply extra money to the highest-rate loan first and to keep your due date — otherwise the extra can be treated as an early payment of next month’s bill.

Refinancing $40,000 in student loans: is a lower rate worth it?

Private lenders may offer a lower fixed rate to borrowers with strong credit and income, but refinancing federal loans turns them into private loans permanently. You give up RAP and IBR, Public Service Loan Forgiveness, federal deferment and forbearance, and federal discharge protections. The table shows what a lower rate would save on $40,000; the rates are illustrative, not current offers — compare quotes yourself.

Student loan interest deduction on $40,000

In the first year of the 10-year plan you would pay about $2,521 of interest on $40,000, so you could deduct $2,500 (the $2,500 maximum) if your 2026 modified AGI is under $85,000 (single) or $175,000 (married filing jointly). At a 22% federal bracket that is worth roughly $550. The deduction phases out completely at $100,000 / $205,000 and is not available if you file separately.

FAQ: $40,000 student loan payments

What is the monthly payment on $40,000 in student loans?

About $454.60 a month on a 10-year standard plan at the 6.52% 2026-27 undergraduate rate. At 6.52% it is $454.60, at 8.07% $486.79 and at the 9.07% PLUS rate $508.22. On the new 15-year Tiered Standard plan it is $348.88.

How long does it take to pay off $40,000 in student loans?

Ten years on the standard plan, 15 years on the Tiered Standard plan for new loans, or up to 25 years on the extended plan. Paying an extra $250 a month at 6.52% cuts it to about 5 yr 9 mo.

What would my RAP payment be on $40,000 of student loans?

RAP depends on income, not the balance: 1% to 10% of AGI divided by 12, minus $50 per dependent, with a $10 minimum. At $40,000 AGI it is $100 a month; at $65,000 it is $325. The balance only decides how long you pay.

How much do I need to earn to afford $40,000 in student loans?

Using the 10%-of-gross-income rule, about $55,000 a year for the 10-year standard payment of $455, or $42,000 for the 15-year tiered payment.

How much interest will I pay on $40,000 in student loans?

At 6.52%: $14,552 over 10 years, $22,799 over 15 years, $31,688 over 20 years and $41,175 over 25 years.
