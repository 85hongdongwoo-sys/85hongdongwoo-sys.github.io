Short answer

On a $45,000 salary a single filer with a standard W-4 should expect a federal refund of about $0 — withholding is designed to match the $3,220 of tax. The big refunds come from what your W-4 doesn’t know about: about $3,254 for a head of household with one child, $8,801 for a one-income married couple with two kids, and $227–$1,884 from the new overtime, tips and senior deductions.

Example: Luis, $45,000

Luis is an hourly warehouse associate. Base pay works out to about $18.91 an hour; on top of 2,080 regular hours he worked 200 overtime hours at time-and-a-half, for $5,672 of overtime pay inside the $45,000 total. Withholding with a “Single” W-4: $3,220.

  1. Wages $45,000 − deductions (standard $16,100, overtime $1,891) = taxable income $27,009.
  2. Tax from the single brackets: 10% × $12,400 = $1,240; 12% × $14,609 = $1,753 → $2,993.
  3. Refund = withheld $3,220 − tax after credits $2,993 = $227.

Only the “half” in time-and-a-half counts: 200 hours × $9.45 = $1,891. The other $3,782 is ordinary pay. Employers must report qualified overtime separately (on the 2026 W-2, Box 12 code TT) — if yours lumps it together, ask payroll for the figure before you file.

How the new OBBBA deductions can make a $45,000 refund bigger in 2027

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act added four deductions for 2025–2028 that you claim on the new Schedule 1-A whether or not you itemize. None of them is built into the default withholding tables, so if you qualify and didn’t update your W-4, the saving lands in your refund. At $45,000, you are in the 12% bracket, so every $1,000 of extra deduction cuts your tax by about $120.

  • Overtime: 200 hours of time-and-a-half adds a $1,891 deduction → about $227 back. How it works.
  • Tips: if $15,700 of the pay were qualified tips, the refund is about $1,884. Details.
  • Age 65+: the extra $2,050 standard deduction plus the new senior deduction ($6,000 at this income) → about $966. Senior deduction guide.
  • New car loan: $2,500 of interest on a new U.S.-assembled vehicle → about $300. Car loan rules.

These deductions lower taxable income but not AGI, so they don’t change EITC or Child Tax Credit eligibility. Social Security and Medicare still apply to tips and overtime.

Why your refund could still come out different

  • Two jobs. Earn the same $45,000 from two $22,500 jobs without checking Step 2 on either W-4 and the result flips: about $1,280 withheld against $3,220 of tax — you’d owe roughly $1,940.
  • A raise mid-year. Withholding is figured per paycheck on an annualized basis, so a raise is handled almost perfectly. It rarely moves the refund more than a few dollars.
  • Pre-tax 401(k) or HSA contributions. They lower both the tax and the withholding, so they do not create a refund on their own — they just shrink both bars in the chart.
  • Side income. 1099 gig money, interest or a stock sale has no withholding by default. A few thousand dollars of it can erase a $3,254 refund.
  • Bonus pay. In our scenario, $5,000 of the $45,000 arrives as a bonus withheld at the flat 22% rate. Because your bracket is 12%, that over-withholds and adds about $500 to the refund.

At this income the Earned Income Tax Credit still pays something: $1,054 for a head of household with one child and $4,401 for a married couple with two kids. Claiming it (or the refundable part of the Child Tax Credit) means the IRS can’t release your refund before mid-February — the PATH Act hold.

Tax refund on $45,000: FAQ

How much federal tax is withheld from a $45,000 salary?

With a 2026 W-4 that only has the filing status checked, payroll withholds about $3,220 a year as a single filer ($124 per biweekly check), $2,148 as head of household and $1,280 as married filing jointly (one earner). Social Security and Medicare (7.65%) come on top and are never refunded.

Will I get a tax refund on $45,000 if I'm single with no kids?

Probably a small one or none. Our estimate is ≈ $0: the IRS withholding tables subtract the same $16,100 standard deduction your 1040 uses, so tax withheld and tax owed ($3,220) match. Refunds for single filers usually come from bonuses withheld at 22%, the new tips or overtime deductions, or credits.

How much is the refund for a family of four on $45,000?

For a married couple with two kids under 17 and one $45,000 W-2 job, we estimate about $8,801 if the W-4 did not list the children in Step 3. Most of that is the $2,200-per-child Child Tax Credit plus $4,401 of EITC. If the kids were entered in Step 3, the refund drops to about $7,521 because the credit was already paid out in bigger paychecks.

How much does no tax on overtime save on a $45,000 salary?

If $45,000 includes 200 hours of time-and-a-half overtime, the deductible premium is about $1,891 and the tax saving is about $227. Because withholding doesn't know about the deduction unless you add it to W-4 Step 4(b), that saving shows up as a refund in 2027.

Is this the same as my state tax refund?

No. These figures are federal only. Your state return is separate; on $45,000 a single filer owes roughly $783 in California, $1,647 in Georgia and $0 in Texas. Whether you get a state refund depends on your state withholding.
