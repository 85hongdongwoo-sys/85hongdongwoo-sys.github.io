Standard deduction 2026: $16,100 / $32,200 / $24,150

The single biggest number on most tax returns — and the reason withholding tables usually land close to the right answer.

Short answer

For 2026 the standard deduction is $16,100 for single filers and married filing separately, $32,200 for married filing jointly and $24,150 for head of household (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32). People 65 or older or blind add $2,050 (unmarried) or $1,650 per spouse, and the new $6,000 senior deduction comes on top for 2025–2028.

How it shapes your refund

Your employer’s withholding tables already assume the standard deduction. IRS Publication 15-T (2026) subtracts $8,600 ($12,900 married) from annual wages and starts the 10% band at $7,500 single, $19,300 married or $15,550 head of household — which adds up exactly to $16,100, $32,200 and $24,150. That’s why a single person with one job and nothing else tends to get a refund close to zero.

Refunds come from what the tables don’t assume: credits, the new Schedule 1-A deductions, a better filing status than the W-4 says, or itemized deductions above the standard amount.

Standard deduction or itemize?

Itemize only if your deductions beat the standard one. A single homeowner with mortgage interest of $9,000, state & local taxes (cap $40,400) of $6,500, charity of $1,500 has $17,000 of itemized deductions — more than $16,100, so itemizing wins by $900.

The higher SALT cap — $40,400 for 2026, phasing down by 30% of MAGI above $505,000 to a $10,000 floor — means more people in high-tax states will itemize again, especially married homeowners. Two things you get either way: the four Schedule 1-A deductions (tips, overtime, senior, car-loan interest) and, new for 2026, a charitable deduction of up to $1,000 ($2,000 joint) for cash gifts if you don’t itemize. secondary sources

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard deduction for 2026?

$16,100 for single and married filing separately, $32,200 for married filing jointly and $24,150 for head of household.

What is the extra standard deduction for seniors in 2026?

$2,050 for unmarried filers 65 or older (or blind) and $1,650 per qualifying married spouse. The separate $6,000 senior deduction is added on top for 2025–2028.

What was the standard deduction in 2025?

$15,750 single, $31,500 married filing jointly and $23,625 head of household, after the One Big Beautiful Bill Act raised the original 2025 amounts.

Can I take the standard deduction and still deduct tips or overtime?

Yes. The tips, overtime, senior and car-loan interest deductions are claimed on Schedule 1-A in addition to the standard deduction.

Should I itemize in 2026?

Only if mortgage interest, state and local taxes (up to the $40,400 cap), charity and other itemized deductions add up to more than your standard deduction.
