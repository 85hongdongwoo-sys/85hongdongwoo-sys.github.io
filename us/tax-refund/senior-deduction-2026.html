The $6,000 senior deduction for 2026, explained

It’s often called “no tax on Social Security” — it isn’t, exactly. It’s a bonus deduction for anyone 65 or older, working or retired.

Short answer

If you’re 65 or older by December 31, 2026, you can deduct an extra $6,000 ($12,000 if both spouses qualify) on top of the standard deduction and the existing age-65 add-on, whether you itemize or not. It phases out by 6% of MAGI above $75,000 ($150,000 joint). A single 65+ filer with low income can deduct $24,150 in total; a couple both 65+, $47,500.

Three layers of deductions for seniors

For 2026 the stack is: the regular standard deduction ($16,100 single, $32,200 joint); the long-standing additional amount for age 65 or blindness ($2,050 unmarried, $1,650 per married spouse); and the new senior deduction of $6,000 per qualifying person from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, available for 2025 through 2028. Itemizers get the $6,000 too.

Requirements: you must turn 65 by the end of the tax year (born before January 2, 1962, for 2026), include a valid SSN, and file jointly if married.

Example: a retired couple with a pension and IRA withdrawals

Linda and George, both 68, have $62,000 of taxable pension and IRA income (they’re delaying Social Security until 70, so there are no benefits to tax yet). They had $4,100 withheld from the pension and IRA payments — a W-4P set years ago, before the senior deduction existed.

Without the new deduction, their taxable income would be $26,500 and tax about $2,684. The $12,000 senior deduction drops taxable income to $14,500 and tax to $1,450, so their refund is about $2,650.

That example shows why many retirees will see larger refunds in 2027: pension and IRA withholding rarely gets updated. If you’d rather keep the money monthly, submit a new Form W-4P (pensions) or W-4R (IRA withdrawals) to the payer.

How the 6% phase-out works

Ray, a single 67-year-old earning $75,000 in wages, gets the full $6,000; his estimated refund with a standard W-4 is $1,771. Tom, 66, earns $110,000: 6% of the $35,000 above $75,000 is $2,100, so his deduction is $3,900 and his refund about $1,309. Remember that MAGI includes taxable Social Security, pension and IRA income — not just wages. See the worked pages for $75,000 and $110,000.

Does this mean no tax on Social Security?

Not literally. The rules for taxing Social Security benefits (up to 85% taxable above certain income levels) didn’t change. But by lowering taxable income, the deduction means many seniors whose income is mostly Social Security owe little or no federal income tax. A deduction is also worth the most to people in the 12%–24% brackets — about $720 to $1,440 per person — and nothing to someone whose income already falls below the standard deduction.

Frequently asked questions

Who qualifies for the $6,000 senior deduction?

Anyone who is 65 or older by December 31 of the tax year (born before January 2, 1962, for 2026), has a valid SSN and, if married, files jointly. It applies for tax years 2025 through 2028.

Is the senior deduction in addition to the standard deduction?

Yes. It stacks on the regular standard deduction and the extra $2,050 (single) or $1,650 (per married spouse) for age 65, and itemizers can claim it too.

What is the income limit for the senior deduction?

It’s reduced by 6% of modified AGI over $75,000 ($150,000 joint). A single filer loses it completely at $175,000.

Does the senior deduction eliminate tax on Social Security?

No. The rules for taxing benefits are unchanged, but the larger deduction means many retirees owe less or no federal income tax.

Do I need to do anything to get it?

Claim it on Schedule 1-A with your return. To get the benefit during the year instead, update Form W-4, W-4P or W-4R.
