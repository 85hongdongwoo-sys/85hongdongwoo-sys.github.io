When will I get my tax refund in 2027?

A realistic refund calendar for 2026 returns: the 21-day rule, the PATH Act hold for EITC and child-credit refunds, and what slows things down.

Short answer

If you e-file and choose direct deposit, the IRS issues most refunds in 21 days or less after accepting the return. If you claim the Earned Income Tax Credit or the Additional Child Tax Credit, federal law (the PATH Act) bars the IRS from releasing the refund before mid-February, so early filers typically see the money in late February to early March 2027. Paper returns take six weeks or more.

The refund calendar for 2026 returns

The IRS hasn’t announced the first day of the 2027 filing season yet (as of November 3, 2026). For context, the 2026 season opened on January 26, 2026, and the agency usually picks the last week or so of January. Tax software will happily take your return earlier and hold it until e-filing opens.

Why EITC and child-credit refunds wait until mid-February

The Protecting Americans from Tax Hikes (PATH) Act of 2015 requires the IRS to hold the entire refund — not just the credit portion — on any return that claims the Earned Income Tax Credit or the Additional Child Tax Credit until mid-February. The pause gives the IRS time to match the wages on your return against the W-2s employers file, which is the main defense against identity-theft refund fraud.

Take Tasha, a head of household earning $50,000 with one child. Our $50,000 estimate shows a refund of about $2,455, including $0 of refundable ACTC. Because of that ACTC, filing on opening day doesn’t help much: her refund still can’t leave before mid-February. A single coworker with no kids who files the same day could see a (small) refund roughly three weeks after acceptance.

In 2026 the IRS said Where’s My Refund would show projected deposit dates for most early EITC/ACTC filers by February 21, with most of those refunds landing around March 2. Expect a similar pattern in 2027.

How long each filing method takes

Direct deposit is now practically required. Under Executive Order 14247, the IRS generally stopped issuing paper refund checks to individuals after September 30, 2025. If you file without bank details, the IRS sends a CP53E letter asking for an account within 30 days; with no response, a paper check is issued only after about six weeks. You can split a refund into up to three accounts with Form 8888, and many prepaid debit cards and mobile-banking apps accept direct deposits too.

What delays a refund

  • Errors and mismatches. A wrong Social Security number for a child, wages that don’t match the W-2, or a math slip on the new Schedule 1-A deductions sends the return to manual review.
  • Identity verification. If the IRS mails a 5071C or similar letter, the refund waits until you verify — usually online.
  • Offsets. Past-due child support, state tax debts and some federal debts are taken out first by the Treasury Offset Program; you receive a notice explaining the reduction.
  • Amended returns. A Form 1040-X runs on a separate, much slower track — think months, not weeks.
  • Filing on paper. Paper returns must be keyed in by hand; six weeks is the IRS minimum guidance.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a tax refund in 2027?

Most e-filed returns with direct deposit are refunded within 21 days of acceptance. Returns claiming the EITC or Additional Child Tax Credit can’t be refunded before mid-February, so early filers usually get them in late February or early March. Paper returns take six weeks or more.

When does the IRS start accepting 2026 tax returns?

The IRS had not announced the 2027 opening date as of November 3, 2026. The 2026 filing season opened January 26, 2026, and the start is usually in the second half of January.

Why is my refund held until February?

If your return claims the Earned Income Tax Credit or the Additional Child Tax Credit, the PATH Act requires the IRS to hold the whole refund until mid-February so it can verify wages against employer W-2s.

Can I still get a paper refund check?

Generally no. The IRS phased out paper refund checks for individuals after September 30, 2025, apart from limited exceptions. Without bank details you’ll get a CP53E notice, and a paper check is issued only after about six weeks with no response.

Does filing early get my refund faster?

Yes for most filers, because returns are processed roughly in the order received. For EITC/ACTC claimants, filing early only helps up to the mid-February PATH release.
