Average tax refund: $3,273 — and what to expect in 2027

The IRS publishes refund statistics every week of filing season. Here is what the 2026 numbers say — and what they don’t.

Short answer

Through May 1, 2026, the IRS paid 97.9 million refunds averaging $3,273, up 11.1% from $2,947 at the same point in 2025. The jump came largely from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act applying retroactively to 2025 while paychecks hadn’t caught up. For 2026 returns filed in 2027 there is no official forecast yet; your own refund depends on your withholding, not the national average.

What the IRS data shows

The IRS had received 143.7 million individual returns by May 1, 2026, slightly fewer than a year earlier, but it issued 6.5% more refunds — 97.9 million — and each one was bigger on average. Total refund dollars therefore rose by roughly 18%.

Why so much? The 2025 tax law was signed on July 4, 2025 but applied to the entire 2025 year: a higher standard deduction ($15,750 single), a bigger Child Tax Credit ($2,200), and the new deductions for tips, overtime, seniors and car-loan interest. Payroll tables for 2025 had been set before the law passed, so most workers were over-withheld — and got the difference back in spring 2026.

Is the average refund a good benchmark?

Not really. The “average” mixes a retiree getting $300 back with a family of five collecting several thousand dollars of refundable credits. It’s a mean, pulled up by large EITC and child-credit refunds, and it only counts people who got a refund — about a third of filers owe or break even.

A single worker whose W-4 matches reality should get back close to nothing — that’s the system working. Families with children and workers claiming the new deductions are the ones who end up above the average.

What to expect for 2026 returns (filed in 2027)

Two forces pull in opposite directions:

  • Smaller: the 2026 withholding tables (IRS Publication 15-T 2026) already include the new $16,100 standard deduction and brackets, so the retroactive catch-up that inflated 2025 refunds doesn’t repeat. Workers who updated their W-4 for tips or overtime have been getting the benefit in each paycheck.
  • Bigger: 2026 is the first year employers report qualified tips and overtime on the W-2 (Box 12 codes TP and TT), which makes the deductions easier to claim — and many people never touched their W-4. Non-itemizers can also deduct up to $1,000 ($2,000 joint) of cash charitable gifts starting in 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average tax refund in 2026?

The IRS reported an average refund of $3,273 through May 1, 2026, up 11.1% from $2,947 at the same point in 2025. The average direct-deposit refund was $3,268.

Why were tax refunds bigger in 2026?

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act applied retroactively to all of 2025, with a higher standard deduction, a $2,200 Child Tax Credit and new deductions for tips, overtime, seniors and car-loan interest, while 2025 withholding hadn’t been adjusted.

Will tax refunds be bigger in 2027?

There’s no official forecast. The 2026 withholding tables already reflect the new standard deduction and brackets, so the one-time catch-up won’t repeat, but people who qualify for the new deductions and didn’t update their W-4 can still see larger refunds.

Is a big refund good?

It means you over-paid during the year and gave the government an interest-free loan. Some people like the forced savings; others prefer bigger paychecks by adjusting their W-4.
