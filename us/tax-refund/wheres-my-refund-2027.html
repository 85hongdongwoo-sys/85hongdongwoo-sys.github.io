Where’s my refund? Tracking your 2027 tax refund

How the IRS refund tracker works, what each status means, and the point at which waiting longer stops being normal.

Short answer

Use the IRS Where’s My Refund? tool on IRS.gov or the IRS2Go app. You need your SSN or ITIN, filing status and the exact refund amount. It shows status about 24 hours after an e-filed return is accepted (about four weeks after mailing a paper return), updates once a day, usually overnight, and moves through three stages: Received → Approved → Sent.

What you need before you check

The refund amount must match the whole-dollar figure on your 1040 — the “amount you want refunded to you” line, not the total overpayment if you applied part of it to next year. Married couples filing jointly can use either spouse’s SSN as long as it’s the one listed first on the return.

Checking more than once a day doesn’t help; the tool refreshes overnight. The same information appears in your IRS Online Account, which also shows the transcript and any notices.

What the three statuses mean

Return Received means the IRS has the return and is processing it. A return that claims the EITC or Additional Child Tax Credit sits here until at least mid-February because of the PATH Act — that is normal, not a problem.

Refund Approved comes with a projected deposit date. If the approved amount differs from what you filed, the IRS changed something (a math correction or an offset) and a letter explaining why follows by mail.

Refund Sent means the Treasury released the money. Direct deposits usually post within a few days; your bank may hold it briefly. Since the IRS phased out paper refund checks after September 30, 2025, almost everyone will see a deposit rather than a mailed check.

When should you worry?

Here is a simple rule of thumb, based on IRS guidance:

SituationNormal waitContact the IRS if…
E-filed, direct deposit, no EITC/ACTCup to 21 daysmore than 21 days since acceptance and the tool tells you to call
E-filed with EITC or ACTCuntil early March for January filersno deposit date by early March
Paper return6+ weeksmore than 6 weeks since mailing
CP53E letter received30 days to replyyou replied with bank info and nothing has changed after ~2 weeks

A message about “Tax Topic 152” is generic and simply means the return is still in processing. A message asking you to verify your identity is different — respond right away using the letter or the IRS identity-verification page, because nothing moves until you do.

If the deposited amount is lower than expected, compare it to our estimator and wait for the IRS notice; offsets for past-due child support or state tax debts are the usual reason. See also why is my tax refund smaller.

Example: tracking two different returns

Jordan (single, $55,000, no kids) e-files on February 9, 2027. The return is accepted that evening; on February 10 the tracker shows “Received”, by about February 20 “Approved” with a deposit date, and whatever small refund he has (his withholding matched his tax almost exactly) lands by about March 2.

Marcus and Elena (married, $70,000, two kids) file on January 28. Their refund of about $4,400 includes $0 of EITC and $360 of refundable child credit, so the status stays at “Received” until the PATH Act release in mid-February. Based on the 2026 pattern, a deposit date would appear around February 21 and the money around early March — even though they filed almost two weeks earlier than Jordan.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check my tax refund status?

Use Where’s My Refund? on IRS.gov or the IRS2Go app with your SSN or ITIN, filing status and exact refund amount. Your IRS Online Account shows the same status.

How often does Where’s My Refund update?

Once a day, usually overnight. Checking several times a day won’t show anything new.

Why does Where’s My Refund still say “Received”?

Processing isn’t finished. Returns claiming the EITC or Additional Child Tax Credit stay at Received until at least mid-February under the PATH Act. Otherwise, most e-filed returns move to Approved within three weeks.

My refund was approved for less than I filed for — why?

The IRS corrected something on the return or applied part of the refund to a debt such as past-due child support or state taxes. A letter explaining the change is mailed separately.

When should I call the IRS about my refund?

Only if it has been more than 21 days since your e-filed return was accepted (6 weeks for paper), or if Where’s My Refund tells you to contact the IRS.
