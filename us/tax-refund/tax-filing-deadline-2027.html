Short answer

The deadline to file a 2026 federal return and pay any tax owed is Thursday, April 15, 2027. Filing Form 4868 by that date gives you until Friday, October 15, 2027 to file — but not more time to pay. If you’re due a refund, there’s no penalty for filing late; you have until April 15, 2030 to claim it.

Key dates for your 2026 return

April 15, 2027 falls on a Thursday and isn’t near a Washington, D.C. holiday, so expect the deadline to stay put. Residents of federally declared disaster areas often receive automatic extensions — the IRS lists them by state on its disaster-relief page. Americans living abroad get an automatic two-month extension to June 15, 2027 (interest still runs on unpaid tax from April 15).

State deadlines usually mirror the federal date, but not always; check your state revenue department.

Late filing vs. late paying: the costs are very different

There are two separate penalties. Failure to file is 5% of the unpaid tax for each month or part of a month the return is late, up to 25%. Failure to pay is 0.5% a month, also capped at 25%. When both apply in the same month, the filing penalty is reduced by the payment penalty, so the combined charge is 5% a month. Interest is added on top at a rate the IRS resets each quarter.

The lesson: file by April 15 even if you can’t pay, then set up a payment plan on IRS.gov. If a return is more than 60 days late, a minimum failure-to-file penalty applies (the IRS adjusts it for inflation each year).

If you’re getting a refund, the deadline is softer

Penalties are calculated on unpaid tax. If your withholding covered everything and you’re owed money back, a late return costs you nothing except time — the IRS simply holds your money longer. You generally have three years from the original due date to claim a refund, so a 2026 refund can be claimed until April 15, 2030. After that, the money goes to the Treasury for good.

That matters for people who aren’t required to file at all. A part-time worker who earned $14,000 is below the $16,100 single filing threshold, but if federal tax was withheld — or if they qualify for the Earned Income Tax Credit — the only way to get that money is to file. Should you file early?

For refund-seekers, yes: returns are processed roughly first-in, first-out, and filing early also beats identity thieves who file fake returns using stolen SSNs. The main reason to wait is a missing form — a corrected W-2, a late 1099-B from a brokerage, or the new statements showing qualified tips and overtime (Box 12 codes TP and TT on 2026 W-2s). Filing before you have them often ends in an amended return, which is slow.

Frequently asked questions

When are 2026 taxes due?

Thursday, April 15, 2027 is the deadline to file 2026 federal returns and pay any balance due.

What is the tax extension deadline in 2027?

Friday, October 15, 2027, if you file Form 4868 (or make an extension payment) by April 15, 2027. The extension is for filing only; tax owed is still due April 15.

Is there a penalty for filing late if I get a refund?

No. Late-filing and late-payment penalties are based on unpaid tax, so if you’re owed a refund there is no penalty. You have until April 15, 2030 to claim a 2026 refund.

What happens if I can’t pay by April 15, 2027?

File on time anyway to avoid the 5%-per-month failure-to-file penalty, pay what you can, and request a payment plan. The failure-to-pay penalty is only 0.5% a month plus interest.

When is the last 2026 estimated tax payment due?

Friday, January 15, 2027, for the fourth quarter of 2026.
