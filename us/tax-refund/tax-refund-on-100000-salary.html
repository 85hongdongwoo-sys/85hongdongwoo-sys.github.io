Short answer

On a $100,000 salary a single filer with a standard W-4 should expect a federal refund of about $0 — withholding is designed to match the $13,170 of tax. The big refunds come from what your W-4 doesn’t know about: about $2,200 for a head of household with one child, $4,400 for a one-income married couple with two kids, and $924–$5,500 from the new overtime, tips and senior deductions.

Example: Ben, $100,000

Ben is a single filer who works two jobs, each paying $50,000. Both employers got a W-4 with only “Single” checked, so each one withheld as if $50,000 were the whole story — $3,820 apiece, $7,640 in total.

  1. Wages $100,000 − deductions (standard $16,100) = taxable income $83,900.
  2. Tax from the single brackets: 10% × $12,400 = $1,240; 12% × $38,000 = $4,560; 22% × $33,500 = $7,370 → $13,170.
  3. Refund = withheld $7,640 − tax after credits $13,170 = owe $5,530.

The trouble: each payroll applied the full $16,100 standard deduction and the low 10% and 12% brackets. On the 1040 they count once. Tax on $100,000 is $13,170, so Ben ends up owing about $5,530. Checking the Step 2 box on both W-4s (or using the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator) fixes this.

How the new OBBBA deductions can make a $100,000 refund bigger in 2027

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act added four deductions for 2025–2028 that you claim on the new Schedule 1-A whether or not you itemize. None of them is built into the default withholding tables, so if you qualify and didn’t update your W-4, the saving lands in your refund. At $100,000, your top dollars are taxed at 22%, so each $1,000 of new deduction is worth about $220 off your bill.

  • Overtime: 200 hours of time-and-a-half adds a $4,202 deduction → about $924 back. One caveat: at $100,000 many people are salaried and exempt from the Fair Labor Standards Act, so they earn no FLSA overtime at all — the deduction only covers overtime the law requires. How it works.
  • Tips: if $25,000 of the pay were qualified tips, the refund is about $5,500. ($25,000 of tips on a $100,000 income is less common, but tipped hairstylists, bartenders at busy venues and some delivery drivers do get there.) Details.
  • Age 65+: the extra $2,050 standard deduction plus the new senior deduction ($4,500 at this income, already reduced by the 6% phase-out) → about $1,441. Senior deduction guide.
  • New car loan: $2,500 of interest on a new U.S.-assembled vehicle → about $550. Car loan rules.

These deductions lower taxable income but not AGI, so they don’t change EITC or Child Tax Credit eligibility. Social Security and Medicare still apply to tips and overtime.

Why your refund could still come out different

  • Bonus pay. In our scenario, $5,000 of the $100,000 arrives as a bonus withheld at the flat 22% rate. 22% matches your bracket, so it nets out to zero.
  • A raise mid-year. Withholding is figured per paycheck on an annualized basis, so a raise is handled almost perfectly. It rarely moves the refund more than a few dollars.
  • Pre-tax 401(k) or HSA contributions. They lower both the tax and the withholding, so they do not create a refund on their own — they just shrink both bars in the chart.
  • Side income. 1099 gig money, interest or a stock sale has no withholding by default. A few thousand dollars of it can erase a $2,200 refund.

At $100,000 you are past the EITC income limits for every family size, so family refunds here come almost entirely from the $2,200 Child Tax Credit.

Tax refund on $100,000: FAQ

How much federal tax is withheld from a $100,000 salary?

With a 2026 W-4 that only has the filing status checked, payroll withholds about $13,170 a year as a single filer ($507 per biweekly check), $9,588 as head of household and $7,640 as married filing jointly (one earner). Social Security and Medicare (7.65%) come on top and are never refunded.

Will I get a tax refund on $100,000 if I'm single with no kids?

Probably a small one or none. Our estimate is ≈ $0: the IRS withholding tables subtract the same $16,100 standard deduction your 1040 uses, so tax withheld and tax owed ($13,170) match. Refunds for single filers usually come from bonuses withheld at 22%, the new tips or overtime deductions, or credits.

How much is the refund for a family of four on $100,000?

For a married couple with two kids under 17 and one $100,000 W-2 job, we estimate about $4,400 if the W-4 did not list the children in Step 3. Most of that is the $2,200-per-child Child Tax Credit. If the kids were entered in Step 3, the refund drops to about ≈ $0 because the credit was already paid out in bigger paychecks.

How much does no tax on overtime save on a $100,000 salary?

If $100,000 includes 200 hours of time-and-a-half overtime, the deductible premium is about $4,202 and the tax saving is about $924. Because withholding doesn't know about the deduction unless you add it to W-4 Step 4(b), that saving shows up as a refund in 2027.

Is this the same as my state tax refund?

No. These figures are federal only. Your state return is separate; on $100,000 a single filer owes roughly $5,055 in California, $4,391 in Georgia and $0 in Texas. Whether you get a state refund depends on your state withholding.
