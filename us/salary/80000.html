How a $80,000 salary is taxed in 2026

Federal income tax. The 2026 standard deduction for a single filer is $16,100 (set by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and indexed in IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32), so $63,900 of the $80,000 is taxable. The first $12,400 of taxable income is taxed at 10%, income up to $50,400 at 12%, up to $105,700 at 22%, and your top dollars sit in the 22% bracket. That adds up to $8,770 — an effective rate of 11.0% even though the marginal rate is 22%.

Social Security and Medicare (FICA). Social Security takes 6.2% of every dollar ($4,960) because the salary is below the 2026 wage base of $184,500. Medicare adds 1.45% ($1,160). Together FICA costs $6,120.

State income tax. Where you live changes take-home by up to $5,427 a year at this salary. Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming do not tax wages. Among states that do, North Dakota, Arizona and Ohio take the least, while Delaware, Hawaii and Oregon take the most. California takes $3,195 and New York $3,723 (before NYC tax), compared with $0 in Texas.

A raise from here. Moving from $80,000 to $85,000 adds about $3,518 of take-home in a no-tax state — you keep roughly 70 cents of each extra dollar before state tax.

FAQ

How much is $80,000 a year after taxes?

For a single filer in 2026, $80,000 a year comes to about $65,110 after federal income tax ($8,770) and Social Security and Medicare ($6,120) in a state with no income tax such as Texas or Florida. Across all 50 states and DC the take-home ranges from $59,683 in Oregon to $65,110, with a median of $62,298.

80k a year is how much an hour?

$80,000 a year is $38.46 an hour before taxes, assuming 40 hours a week for 52 weeks (2,080 hours). After federal tax and FICA that is about $31.30 an hour in a no-income-tax state, or $29.95 an hour in the median state.

How much is $80,000 a year per month after taxes?

About $5,426 a month in a state without income tax, $5,192 a month in the median state, and $4,974 a month in Oregon. Gross monthly pay is $6,667.

What federal tax bracket is $80,000 in?

With the 2026 standard deduction of $16,100, a single filer earning $80,000 has $63,900 of taxable income, which puts the top dollars in the 22% bracket. The effective federal income tax rate is only 11.0% because lower brackets are taxed at 10% and 12% first.

Which state lets you keep the most of a $80,000 salary?

The nine states without a wage income tax (Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming) leave the most: $65,110. Among states with an income tax, North Dakota ($64,809) and Arizona ($63,504) are the lightest at this salary, while Oregon takes the most ($5,427 in state tax).
