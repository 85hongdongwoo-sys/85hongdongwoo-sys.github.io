How Alaska compares

Alaska is one of nine states with no tax on wages (Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming). At $100,000 a single filer here keeps $79,180, which is $5,055 more than in California and $4,860 more than in New York (before NYC tax). Keep in mind that states without an income tax often lean harder on sales and property taxes.

Federal taxes are the same in every state: the 2026 standard deduction is $16,100, brackets run from 10% to 37%, Social Security is 6.2% up to $184,500 and Medicare 1.45% (plus 0.9% above $200,000).

FAQ

How much is $75,000 after taxes in Alaska?

About $61,593 a year ($5,133 a month) for a single filer in 2026. Alaska has no state income tax on wages, so only federal income tax ($7,670) and Social Security and Medicare ($5,738) are withheld.

Does Alaska have a state income tax?

No state income tax on wages. Take-home pay in Alaska is therefore the same as federal-only take-home, which is the highest of all 50 states and DC at every salary.

How much is $100,000 after taxes in Alaska?

About $79,180 a year, or $6,598 a month and $3,045 every two weeks, for a single filer using the 2026 standard deduction.

Are there other payroll or local taxes in Alaska?

No local income taxes on wages are included in these figures.
