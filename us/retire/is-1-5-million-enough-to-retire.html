Enough for many

Using the 4% rule, $1.5 million supports about $60,000 a year ($5,000 a month) in inflation-adjusted withdrawals for roughly 30 years. Add the average Social Security retirement benefit of $2,071 a month and a single retiree has about $84,852 a year — 138% of the $61,432 that the average 65+ household spends (BLS). For a typical retiree that covers average spending; whether it is enough depends on your lifestyle, health costs and retirement age.

How much income does $1.5 million provide in retirement?

The 4% rule comes from William Bengen’s 1994 research: withdraw 4% of your savings in year one, then raise the dollar amount with inflation each year, and historically a balanced portfolio lasted at least 30 years. Morningstar’s latest research puts the starting safe rate at 3.9% for people retiring in 2026 (30 years, 90% probability of success). Is $1.5 million enough to retire at 55, 62 or 67?

Where $1.5 million fits

$1.5 million at 4% supports about $60,000 a year, nearly matching the average 65+ household’s total spending before Social Security is even counted. That is Fidelity’s 10× target for a $150,000 earner. The biggest questions at this level are taxes — withdrawals from traditional 401(k)s are taxed as ordinary income — and how to keep a balanced allocation for a retirement that could last 30 years or more.

What decides whether it is enough for you

  • Spending, not savings, is the key number. Track the last 12 months; most retirees spend 70%–80% of pre-retirement income.
  • Housing. A paid-off home can cut required income by a third; rent rises with inflation.
  • Taxes. Traditional 401(k)/IRA withdrawals are taxable income; Roth withdrawals generally are not.
  • Healthcare. Before 65 you need private coverage; after 65 Medicare premiums and out-of-pocket costs remain.
  • Other income. A pension, part-time work or a spouse’s benefit changes the picture dramatically.

FAQ: Is $1.5 million enough to retire?

Is $1.5 million enough to retire?

Under the 4% rule $1.5 million provides about $60,000 a year. With the average Social Security benefit ($2,071 a month) that is about $84,852 a year, compared with average 65+ household spending of $61,432. Whether that is enough depends on your expenses, housing and retirement age.

How much monthly income will $1.5 million generate?

About $5,000 a month at a 4% withdrawal rate, or $4,875 at Morningstar’s 3.9% rate, before taxes.

How long will $1.5 million last in retirement?

Withdrawing $75,000 a year (5%), rising 2.5% a year for inflation, at a steady 5% return, lasts about 27 years. At 4% it lasts about 38 years.

Can I retire at 55 with $1.5 million?

If $1.5 million must last 40 years (to age 95), the maximum inflation-rising income at a 5% return is about $57,734 a year before Social Security — and that would spend the balance to zero, so a lower amount is safer.

How much do I need to save each month to reach $1.5 million?

Starting at 35 with nothing, about $1,099 a month at a 7% return to reach $1.5 million by 67, or $1,623 at 5%.
