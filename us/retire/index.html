Quick answer

A common benchmark from Fidelity is to have 1× your salary saved by 30, 3× by 40, 6× by 50, 8× by 60 and 10× by 67. Real balances are lower: Vanguard’s How America Saves 2026 puts the median 401(k) at $44,115 and the average at $167,970. Frequently asked questions

How much should I have in my 401(k) at 30?

Fidelity suggests about 1× your salary in retirement savings by 30. The median Vanguard participant aged 25–34 has $18,732, so most people are below this benchmark.

How much should I have saved for retirement at 40?

About 3× your salary by Fidelity’s guideline — $225,000 on a $75,000 income. The median 401(k) for ages 35–44 is $46,919 (Vanguard 2026).

How much should I have saved by 50?

About 6× salary by 50, according to Fidelity. From 50 you can add an $8,000 catch-up to the $24,500 401(k) limit in 2026.

Is $1 million enough to retire?

Under the 4% rule $1 million produces about $40,000 a year. With the average Social Security benefit ($2,071 a month in January 2026) a single retiree would have about $64,850 a year, slightly above average 65+ household spending of $61,432.

What is the average 401(k) balance?

Vanguard’s How America Saves 2026 reports an average balance of $167,970 and a median of $44,115 at the end of 2025.

Sources & method

  • Salary multiples: Fidelity Viewpoints, “How much do I need to retire?” — 1× salary by 30, 3× by 40, 6× by 50, 8× by 60, 10× by 67 (assumes saving 15% a year from age 25, retiring at 67, about 45% income replacement from savings). Values between these ages are our straight-line interpolation.
  • 401(k) balances by age: Vanguard, How America Saves 2026 (participant balances at Dec 31, 2025; average $167,970, median $44,115).
  • Retirement-account balances by age of family head: Federal Reserve, Survey of Consumer Finances 2022 (families that hold retirement accounts).
  • 2026 contribution limits: IRS news release IR-2025-111 and Notice 2025-67.
  • Social Security: SSA estimated average retired-worker benefit, $2,071/month (January 2026). Spending: BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey 2024, households 65+ ($61,432/yr). Withdrawal rate: Morningstar, The State of Retirement Income (3.9% for 2026 retirees).
  • Projections use monthly contributions and monthly compounding at the stated annual return; dollars are nominal (not inflation-adjusted). All projections are estimates.
