Retirement savings by age: the benchmark at 55

The most widely quoted rule of thumb comes from Fidelity: save 1× your salary by 30, 3× by 40, 6× by 50, 8× by 60 and 10× by 67. It assumes you save 15% of income every year from age 25 (employer match included), invest more than half in stocks, retire at 67 and need savings to replace about 45% of pre-retirement income, with Social Security covering much of the rest.

Average and median 401(k) balance at 55

Vanguard’s How America Saves 2026 covers about five million plan participants. For the 55–64 age band, the median balance is $107,269 and the average is $305,006 — the average is 2.8 times the median because a minority of large accounts pulls it up. The median is the better “typical person” comparison; the average tells you what a diligent long-term saver can reach.

Employer matching and profit-sharing contributions do not count against the $24,500 employee limit. Very few people hit these ceilings — Vanguard reports that only about 14% of participants contributed the maximum in 2025 — so for most, the practical target is a savings rate (12%–15% of pay including the match) rather than the legal cap. From 2026, catch-up contributions must be Roth if your prior-year FICA wages from that employer exceeded $150,000.

Catching up to 10× by 67: for a $75,000 earner, reaching $750,000 at 7% takes about $3,387 a month starting from nothing, or $2,296 a month starting from the $107,269 median — 37% of pay. At 5% returns it rises to $2,853 a month.

The rule of 55

Age 55 brings a lesser-known exception: if you leave your job in or after the calendar year you turn 55, you can withdraw from that employer’s 401(k) without the 10% early-withdrawal penalty (income tax still applies). It does not apply to IRAs or to old employers’ plans, so think twice before rolling everything into an IRA if early retirement is possible. On the Fidelity line, 55 is about 7× salary.

The cost of waiting 3 years

Investing $500 a month from 55 at a 7% return produces about $110,732 by 67. Waiting until 58 leaves about $74,146 — $36,587 less, even though you would only have contributed $18,000 less.

How to catch up on retirement savings at 55

  1. Use the 2026 catch-up: $8,000 in a 401(k) plus $1,100 in an IRA.
  2. Estimate retirement spending with real numbers from the last 12 months.
  3. Build a 1–3 year cash/bond buffer as retirement approaches.
  4. Know the rule of 55 before rolling a 401(k) into an IRA.
  5. Run Social Security claiming scenarios for 62, 67 and 70.

FAQ: Retirement savings at 55

How much should I have in my 401(k) at 55?

Fidelity’s guideline suggests about 7× your salary saved for retirement at 55 (interpolated between its milestones), for example $420,000 on a $60,000 salary or $700,000 on $100,000. Treat it as a rough benchmark, not a requirement.

What is the average 401(k) balance at age 55?

Vanguard’s How America Saves 2026 reports an average of $305,006 and a median of $107,269 for participants aged 55–64.

How much can I contribute to a 401(k) at 55 in 2026?

$32,500 in employee contributions, including a $8,000 catch-up, plus $8,600 to an IRA.

Am I behind on retirement savings at 55?

If you are below the median of $107,269 for ages 55–64 you are behind most savers your age, but 12 years of contributions can change a lot. Saving $500 a month at 7% for 12 years grows to about $110,732.

Is the Fidelity savings-by-age rule realistic?

It describes someone who saved 15% from age 25 with typical market returns. Most people start later, so the median saver is below it; the rule is most useful as a direction and to show how a higher savings rate closes the gap.
