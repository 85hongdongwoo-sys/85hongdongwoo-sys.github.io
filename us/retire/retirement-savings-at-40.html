Retirement savings by age: the benchmark at 40

The most widely quoted rule of thumb comes from Fidelity: save 1× your salary by 30, 3× by 40, 6× by 50, 8× by 60 and 10× by 67. It assumes you save 15% of income every year from age 25 (employer match included), invest more than half in stocks, retire at 67 and need savings to replace about 45% of pre-retirement income, with Social Security covering much of the rest.

Average and median 401(k) balance at 40

Vanguard’s How America Saves 2026 covers about five million plan participants. For the 35–44 age band, the median balance is $46,919 and the average is $120,742 — the average is 2.6 times the median because a minority of large accounts pulls it up. The median is the better “typical person” comparison; the average tells you what a diligent long-term saver can reach.

Catching up to 10× by 67: for a $75,000 earner, reaching $750,000 at 7% takes about $813 a month starting from nothing, or $497 a month starting from the $46,919 median — 8% of pay. At 5% returns it rises to $857 a month.

3× salary by 40

Fidelity’s second named milestone: about three times your salary by 40 — $225,000 on a $75,000 income. The median 35–44 Vanguard participant has $46,919, so most people are below this line; it describes someone who started at 25 and never stopped. At 40 you still have 27 working years to 67, which is plenty of time to close a gap if you raise your savings rate now. Higher earners should note the 2026 Roth IRA income phase-out ($153,000–$168,000 single, $242,000–$252,000 joint); above it, a “backdoor” Roth may be an option.

The cost of waiting 5 years

Investing $500 a month from 40 at a 7% return produces about $461,066 by 67. Waiting until 45 leaves about $303,353 — $157,713 less, even though you would only have contributed $30,000 less.

How to catch up on retirement savings at 40

  1. Check your Social Security estimate at ssa.gov to size your gap.
  2. Pay off credit-card and other high-interest debt before 50 to free cash for catch-ups.
  3. Review fees: switching from a 1% to a 0.1% fund can add tens of thousands by 67.
  4. Rebalance once a year rather than reacting to markets.
  5. Consider a Roth IRA (or backdoor Roth) for tax diversification.

Not financial advice. Figures are general educational estimates based on public data and fixed return assumptions (nominal, before fees and taxes); real returns vary and can be negative. Consider a fee-only fiduciary adviser for decisions about your own situation.

FAQ: Retirement savings at 40

How much should I have in my 401(k) at 40?

Fidelity’s guideline suggests about 3× your salary saved for retirement at 40, for example $180,000 on a $60,000 salary or $300,000 on $100,000. Treat it as a rough benchmark, not a requirement.

What is the average 401(k) balance at age 40?

Vanguard’s How America Saves 2026 reports an average of $120,742 and a median of $46,919 for participants aged 35–44.

How much can I contribute to a 401(k) at 40 in 2026?

$24,500 in employee contributions, plus $7,500 to an IRA.

Am I behind on retirement savings at 40?

If you are below the median of $46,919 for ages 35–44 you are behind most savers your age, but 27 years of contributions can change a lot. Saving $500 a month at 7% for 27 years grows to about $461,066.

Is the Fidelity savings-by-age rule realistic?

It describes someone who saved 15% from age 25 with typical market returns. Most people start later, so the median saver is below it; the rule is most useful as a direction and to show how a higher savings rate closes the gap.
