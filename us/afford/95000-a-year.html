How much house can I afford on $95k a year?

With a $95,000 salary, the 28/36 rule caps your monthly housing payment (PITI) at about $2,217. At the 7.24% average 30-year rate (Freddie Mac, October 29, 2026) that buys a home of roughly $330,000 with 20% down, or $264,000 with a 3.5% FHA down payment.

You can afford a house of about $330,000 on $95,000 a year if you put 20% down and have no other debts — about 3.5× your salary. That keeps principal, interest, property tax and insurance at $2,217 a month, 28% of your $7,917 gross monthly pay. With less down, PMI and a larger loan shrink the price: $286,000 at 10% down, $270,000 at 5% and $264,000 with FHA. That is 21% below the U.S. median existing-home price of $417,500 (NAR, September 2026).

The 28/36 rule on $95k: your maximum monthly mortgage payment

Ask a loan officer how much house you can afford and the first thing they calculate is debt-to-income. Under the 28/36 rule, no more than 28% of gross monthly pay should go to housing (PITI: principal, interest, taxes, insurance) and no more than 36% to housing plus all other debts. Both are measured before taxes, which is why the numbers can feel generous.

On $95,000 a year, gross pay is $7,917 a month, so the 28/36 rule gives:

What about 43%? It was the hard debt-to-income cap for a Qualified Mortgage until the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau replaced it with a price-based test in 2021 (mandatory since October 2022). Today 43% is still FHA's standard back-end ratio and a widely used benchmark, and Fannie Mae's automated underwriting can approve up to 50% with strong credit and reserves. If a lender approved you at the 43% line with no other debts, your housing payment could reach $3,404 — enough for about $507,000 with 20% down — but that leaves far less for everything else.

At this income you can afford roughly a median-priced American home or a little less, as long as you keep other debts small. The 28/36 rule leaves $633 a month for car, student-loan and card payments before your house budget starts to shrink.

In the first year about $2,559 of your payments would pay down principal; the rest is interest.

How car payments and student loans change what you can afford

The 36% back-end limit only bites once your other debts exceed $633 a month (8% of gross pay). Below that, the 28% front-end limit is the binding constraint and a small car payment doesn't change your price. Above it, every dollar of debt payment is a dollar less for housing.

There's a reason many planners prefer a take-home version of the rule: $2,217 is 28% of gross but 35% of take-home pay here. Add utilities and maintenance (often 1%–2% of the home's value a year, or $412 a month at 1.5%) and housing can quietly crowd out savings.

Rent or buy on $95k?

The 30% rent rule would let you rent up to about $2,375 a month on $95,000 a year (see how much rent you can afford on $95k). Owning the $330,000 home costs $2,217 in PITI plus roughly $412 a month for maintenance — about $2,629 in all — and ties up $75,860 in down payment and closing costs.

Buying tends to win the longer you stay: in year one only about $2,559 of your payments builds equity, and selling costs (often 6%–8% of the price) can wipe out early gains. If you might move within about five years, renting and investing the down payment is often the cheaper path.

Tips to afford more house on $95,000 a year

  • Treat the 28% number as a ceiling; many buyers are more comfortable at 25% of gross.
  • Shop property taxes: the same price can cost hundreds more a month in Texas, Illinois or New Jersey than in lower-tax states.
  • Keep 3–6 months of expenses in reserve after closing — lenders like reserves, and so will you when the water heater fails.
  • Consider buying discount points only if you expect to keep the loan longer than the break-even period.
  • Compare a 15-year loan if the payment fits: rates run lower and you build equity much faster.

FAQ: how much house can I afford on $95k?

How much house can I afford on $95k a year?

About $330,000 with 20% down and no other debts, using the 28/36 rule and the 7.24% average 30-year rate (Freddie Mac, October 29, 2026). That keeps your PITI payment at $2,217 a month. With 10% down it's about $286,000, with 5% down $270,000, and with a 3.5% FHA loan $264,000. These are estimates; lenders decide.

What is the 28/36 rule on $95,000?

Housing costs (principal, interest, property tax, insurance) should be at most 28% of gross monthly income — $2,217 on $95,000 — and all debt payments including housing at most 36%, or $2,850. That leaves $633 a month for car, student-loan and card payments before your home budget shrinks.

What mortgage payment can I afford on $95k?

A total housing payment (PITI) of about $2,217 a month under the 28% rule. Of that, roughly $1,798 would be principal and interest on a $263,860 loan at 7.24%, with about $418 for property tax and insurance. Some lenders approve up to 43% total DTI ($3,404) or more.

How much do I need for a down payment on a house on $95k?

For the $330,000 home, 20% down is $65,965; for the smaller $264,000 home an FHA loan needs $9,233 (3.5%). Add roughly 2%–5% of the price for closing costs. Saving 15% of take-home pay, that's about 1.9 years for the 5%-down option.

Can I buy a house in Los Angeles on $95k?

A median-priced home there is a stretch. The approximate Q2 2026 median single-family price in Los Angeles is $879,900 (NAR), and buying it with 20% down under the 28% rule needs about $253,000 a year. Condos, smaller homes or outer suburbs are more realistic.

Is it better to use the 28/36 rule or the 43% limit?

Use 28/36 as a comfortable budget and 43% as an upper limit. A 43% debt-to-income ratio would allow $3,404 a month for housing and debts on $95k, but it leaves much less for savings, repairs and emergencies.
