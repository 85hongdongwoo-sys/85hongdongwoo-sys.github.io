How much house can I afford on $40k a year?

With a $40,000 salary, the 28/36 rule caps your monthly housing payment (PITI) at about $933. At the 7.24% average 30-year rate (Freddie Mac, October 29, 2026) that buys a home of roughly $139,000 with 20% down, or $111,000 with a 3.5% FHA down payment.

You can afford a house of about $139,000 on $40,000 a year if you put 20% down and have no other debts — about 3.5× your salary. That keeps principal, interest, property tax and insurance at $933 a month, 28% of your $3,333 gross monthly pay. The 28/36 rule on $40k: your maximum monthly mortgage payment

The 28/36 rule is the classic lender test. The "28" is the front-end ratio: your total housing payment — principal, interest, property taxes and homeowners insurance (PITI), plus PMI and any HOA dues — should be no more than 28% of gross monthly income. The "36" is the back-end ratio: housing plus every other monthly debt payment (car loans, student loans, minimum card payments) should stay under 36%.

On $40,000 a year, gross pay is $3,333 a month, so the 28/36 rule gives:

What about 43%? It was the hard debt-to-income cap for a Qualified Mortgage until the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau replaced it with a price-based test in 2021 (mandatory since October 2022). Today 43% is still FHA's standard back-end ratio and a widely used benchmark, and Fannie Mae's automated underwriting can approve up to 50% with strong credit and reserves. If a lender approved you at the 43% line with no other debts, your housing payment could reach $1,433 — enough for about $213,000 with 20% down — but that leaves far less for everything else.

At this income, buying a home at today's 7.24% rate is possible mainly in lower-cost metros and smaller towns. The $139,000 price the 28% rule allows is 67% below the national median, so programs that cut the down payment or the rate — FHA loans, state housing finance agency (HFA) first-time buyer loans and local down payment assistance — matter more than any formula.

Only $757 goes to the lender. The remaining $176 is escrowed for property tax and insurance — and those bills tend to rise over time even though a fixed-rate principal-and-interest payment never changes. In the first year about $1,077 of your payments would pay down principal; the rest is interest.

How car payments and student loans change what you can afford

The 36% back-end limit only bites once your other debts exceed $267 a month (8% of gross pay). Below that, the 28% front-end limit is the binding constraint and a small car payment doesn't change your price. Above it, every dollar of debt payment is a dollar less for housing.

Rent or buy on $40k?

The 30% rent rule would let you rent up to about $1,000 a month on $40,000 a year (see how much rent you can afford on $40k). Owning the $139,000 home costs $933 in PITI plus roughly $174 a month for maintenance — about $1,107 in all — and ties up $31,941 in down payment and closing costs.

Buying tends to win the longer you stay: in year one only about $1,077 of your payments builds equity, and selling costs (often 6%–8% of the price) can wipe out early gains. If you might move within about five years, renting and investing the down payment is often the cheaper path.

Tips to afford more house on $40,000 a year

  • Check your state housing finance agency (HFA) for first-time buyer loans and down payment assistance — many cap income at levels that include this salary.
  • Compare FHA (3.5% down, 580+ credit) with conventional 3%-down programs such as Fannie Mae HomeReady and Freddie Mac Home Possible, which have income limits and cheaper mortgage insurance.
  • Pay down a car loan before applying: at this income, even $300 a month of debt can cut your home budget.
  • Get a lender pre-approval before house hunting so you know whether your target price works at today's rate.
  • Budget for repairs: older starter homes often need 1%–2% of their value a year in maintenance.

FAQ: how much house can I afford on $40k?

How much house can I afford on $40k a year?

About $139,000 with 20% down and no other debts, using the 28/36 rule and the 7.24% average 30-year rate (Freddie Mac, October 29, 2026). That keeps your PITI payment at $933 a month. With 10% down it's about $121,000, with 5% down $114,000, and with a 3.5% FHA loan $111,000. These are estimates; lenders decide.

What is the 28/36 rule on $40,000?

Housing costs (principal, interest, property tax, insurance) should be at most 28% of gross monthly income — $933 on $40,000 — and all debt payments including housing at most 36%, or $1,200. That leaves $267 a month for car, student-loan and card payments before your home budget shrinks.

What mortgage payment can I afford on $40k?

A total housing payment (PITI) of about $933 a month under the 28% rule. Of that, roughly $757 would be principal and interest on a $111,099 loan at 7.24%, with about $176 for property tax and insurance. Some lenders approve up to 43% total DTI ($1,433) or more.

How much do I need for a down payment on a house on $40k?

For the $139,000 home, 20% down is $27,775; for the smaller $111,000 home an FHA loan needs $3,888 (3.5%). Add roughly 2%–5% of the price for closing costs. Saving 15% of take-home pay, that's about 1.8 years for the 5%-down option.

Can I buy a house in Los Angeles on $40k?

A median-priced home there is a stretch. The approximate Q2 2026 median single-family price in Los Angeles is $879,900 (NAR), and buying it with 20% down under the 28% rule needs about $253,000 a year. Condos, smaller homes or outer suburbs are more realistic.

Is it better to use the 28/36 rule or the 43% limit?

Use 28/36 as a comfortable budget and 43% as an upper limit. A 43% debt-to-income ratio would allow $1,433 a month for housing and debts on $40k, but it leaves much less for savings, repairs and emergencies.
