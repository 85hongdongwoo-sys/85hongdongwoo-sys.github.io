How much house can I afford on $45k a year?

With a $45,000 salary, the 28/36 rule caps your monthly housing payment (PITI) at about $1,050. At the 7.24% average 30-year rate (Freddie Mac, October 29, 2026) that buys a home of roughly $156,000 with 20% down, or $125,000 with a 3.5% FHA down payment.

You can afford a house of about $156,000 on $45,000 a year if you put 20% down and have no other debts — about 3.5× your salary. That keeps principal, interest, property tax and insurance at $1,050 a month, 28% of your $3,750 gross monthly pay. With less down, PMI and a larger loan shrink the price: $136,000 at 10% down, $128,000 at 5% and $125,000 with FHA. That is 63% below the U.S. median existing-home price of $417,500 (NAR, September 2026).

The 28/36 rule on $45k: your maximum monthly mortgage payment

Lenders size a mortgage with two debt-to-income (DTI) ratios. The front-end ratio compares your housing payment (PITI) with gross pay and traditionally tops out at 28%. The back-end ratio adds car, student-loan and credit-card minimums and traditionally tops out at 36%. Together they're the "28/36 rule" — the most common answer to "how much house can I afford?"

On $45,000 a year, gross pay is $3,750 a month, so the 28/36 rule gives:

What about 43%? It was the hard debt-to-income cap for a Qualified Mortgage until the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau replaced it with a price-based test in 2021 (mandatory since October 2022). Today 43% is still FHA's standard back-end ratio and a widely used benchmark, and Fannie Mae's automated underwriting can approve up to 50% with strong credit and reserves. If a lender approved you at the 43% line with no other debts, your housing payment could reach $1,613 — enough for about $240,000 with 20% down — but that leaves far less for everything else.

At this income, buying a home at today's 7.24% rate is possible mainly in lower-cost metros and smaller towns. The $156,000 price the 28% rule allows is 63% below the national median, so programs that cut the down payment or the rate — FHA loans, state housing finance agency (HFA) first-time buyer loans and local down payment assistance — matter more than any formula.

How car payments and student loans change what you can afford

The 36% back-end limit only bites once your other debts exceed $300 a month (8% of gross pay). Below that, the 28% front-end limit is the binding constraint and a small car payment doesn't change your price. Above it, every dollar of debt payment is a dollar less for housing.

Lenders count minimum required payments, so a credit card with a $2,000 balance typically adds only a small minimum payment, while a leased car counts in full.

Rent or buy on $45k?

The 30% rent rule would let you rent up to about $1,125 a month on $45,000 a year (see how much rent you can afford on $45k). Owning the $156,000 home costs $1,050 in PITI plus roughly $195 a month for maintenance — about $1,245 in all — and ties up $35,934 in down payment and closing costs.

If comparable homes in your area rent for much less than $1,245, renting while you save a larger down payment can make sense; if rents are close to that number, buying builds equity — $1,212 of principal in the first year alone — and locks in a fixed principal-and-interest payment.

Tips to afford more house on $45,000 a year

  • Check your state housing finance agency (HFA) for first-time buyer loans and down payment assistance — many cap income at levels that include this salary.
  • Compare FHA (3.5% down, 580+ credit) with conventional 3%-down programs such as Fannie Mae HomeReady and Freddie Mac Home Possible, which have income limits and cheaper mortgage insurance.
  • Pay down a car loan before applying: at this income, even $300 a month of debt can cut your home budget.
  • Get a lender pre-approval before house hunting so you know whether your target price works at today's rate.
  • Budget for repairs: older starter homes often need 1%–2% of their value a year in maintenance.

FAQ: how much house can I afford on $45k?

How much house can I afford on $45k a year?

About $156,000 with 20% down and no other debts, using the 28/36 rule and the 7.24% average 30-year rate (Freddie Mac, October 29, 2026). That keeps your PITI payment at $1,050 a month. With 10% down it's about $136,000, with 5% down $128,000, and with a 3.5% FHA loan $125,000. These are estimates; lenders decide.

What is the 28/36 rule on $45,000?

Housing costs (principal, interest, property tax, insurance) should be at most 28% of gross monthly income — $1,050 on $45,000 — and all debt payments including housing at most 36%, or $1,350. That leaves $300 a month for car, student-loan and card payments before your home budget shrinks.

What mortgage payment can I afford on $45k?

A total housing payment (PITI) of about $1,050 a month under the 28% rule. Of that, roughly $852 would be principal and interest on a $124,986 loan at 7.24%, with about $198 for property tax and insurance. Some lenders approve up to 43% total DTI ($1,613) or more.

How much do I need for a down payment on a house on $45k?

For the $156,000 home, 20% down is $31,247; for the smaller $125,000 home an FHA loan needs $4,374 (3.5%). Add roughly 2%–5% of the price for closing costs. Saving 15% of take-home pay, that's about 1.8 years for the 5%-down option.

Can I buy a house in Los Angeles on $45k?

A median-priced home there is a stretch. The approximate Q2 2026 median single-family price in Los Angeles is $879,900 (NAR), and buying it with 20% down under the 28% rule needs about $253,000 a year. Condos, smaller homes or outer suburbs are more realistic.

Is it better to use the 28/36 rule or the 43% limit?

Use 28/36 as a comfortable budget and 43% as an upper limit. A 43% debt-to-income ratio would allow $1,613 a month for housing and debts on $45k, but it leaves much less for savings, repairs and emergencies.
