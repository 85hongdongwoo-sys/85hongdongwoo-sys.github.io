How much house can I afford on $75k a year?

With a $75,000 salary, the 28/36 rule caps your monthly housing payment (PITI) at about $1,750. At the 7.24% average 30-year rate (Freddie Mac, October 29, 2026) that buys a home of roughly $260,000 with 20% down, or $208,000 with a 3.5% FHA down payment.

You can afford a house of about $260,000 on $75,000 a year if you put 20% down and have no other debts — about 3.5× your salary. That keeps principal, interest, property tax and insurance at $1,750 a month, 28% of your $6,250 gross monthly pay. With less down, PMI and a larger loan shrink the price: $226,000 at 10% down, $213,000 at 5% and $208,000 with FHA. That is 38% below the U.S. median existing-home price of $417,500 (NAR, September 2026).

The 28/36 rule on $75k: your maximum monthly mortgage payment

The 28/36 rule is the classic lender test. The "28" is the front-end ratio: your total housing payment — principal, interest, property taxes and homeowners insurance (PITI), plus PMI and any HOA dues — should be no more than 28% of gross monthly income. The "36" is the back-end ratio: housing plus every other monthly debt payment (car loans, student loans, minimum card payments) should stay under 36%.

On $75,000 a year, gross pay is $6,250 a month, so the 28/36 rule gives:

Room for other debts$500before budget shrinks

What about 43%? It was the hard debt-to-income cap for a Qualified Mortgage until the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau replaced it with a price-based test in 2021 (mandatory since October 2022). Today 43% is still FHA's standard back-end ratio and a widely used benchmark, and Fannie Mae's automated underwriting can approve up to 50% with strong credit and reserves. If a lender approved you at the 43% line with no other debts, your housing payment could reach $2,688 — enough for about $400,000 with 20% down — but that leaves far less for everything else.

This salary sits in the range where the answer depends heavily on where you live. Your 28% budget of $1,750 a month buys about $260,000 with 20% down — 38% below the U.S. median — which works in much of the Midwest and South but not in the coastal metros.

What a $1,750 housing payment covers (PITI breakdown)

The 28% rule caps the whole payment, not just the loan. With 20% down on a $260,000 home, here is how $1,750 a month splits up:

Only $1,420 goes to the lender. The remaining $330 is escrowed for property tax and insurance — and those bills tend to rise over time even though a fixed-rate principal-and-interest payment never changes. In the first year about $2,020 of your payments would pay down principal; the rest is interest.

How car payments and student loans change what you can afford

The 36% back-end limit only bites once your other debts exceed $500 a month (8% of gross pay). Below that, the 28% front-end limit is the binding constraint and a small car payment doesn't change your price. Above it, every dollar of debt payment is a dollar less for housing.

Rent or buy on $75k?

The 30% rent rule would let you rent up to about $1,875 a month on $75,000 a year (see how much rent you can afford on $75k). Owning the $260,000 home costs $1,750 in PITI plus roughly $325 a month for maintenance — about $2,075 in all — and ties up $59,889 in down payment and closing costs.

Buying tends to win the longer you stay: in year one only about $2,020 of your payments builds equity, and selling costs (often 6%–8% of the price) can wipe out early gains. If you might move within about five years, renting and investing the down payment is often the cheaper path.

Tips to afford more house on $75,000 a year

  • Get pre-approved by two or three lenders on the same day; rates and fees for the same borrower can differ noticeably.
  • Look at 5%–10% down rather than waiting years for 20% — PMI can be removed once you reach 20% equity.
  • Keep other monthly debts below 8% of gross pay so the 36% back-end limit never binds.
  • Raise your credit score above 740 before applying if you can; it lowers both your rate and PMI.
  • Ask the seller for closing-cost credits when the market is slow — they can cut your cash needed by thousands.

FAQ: how much house can I afford on $75k?

How much house can I afford on $75k a year?

About $260,000 with 20% down and no other debts, using the 28/36 rule and the 7.24% average 30-year rate (Freddie Mac, October 29, 2026). That keeps your PITI payment at $1,750 a month. With 10% down it's about $226,000, with 5% down $213,000, and with a 3.5% FHA loan $208,000. These are estimates; lenders decide.

What is the 28/36 rule on $75,000?

Housing costs (principal, interest, property tax, insurance) should be at most 28% of gross monthly income — $1,750 on $75,000 — and all debt payments including housing at most 36%, or $2,250. That leaves $500 a month for car, student-loan and card payments before your home budget shrinks.

What mortgage payment can I afford on $75k?

A total housing payment (PITI) of about $1,750 a month under the 28% rule. Of that, roughly $1,420 would be principal and interest on a $208,311 loan at 7.24%, with about $330 for property tax and insurance. Some lenders approve up to 43% total DTI ($2,688) or more.

How much do I need for a down payment on a house on $75k?

For the $260,000 home, 20% down is $52,078; for the smaller $208,000 home an FHA loan needs $7,289 (3.5%). Add roughly 2%–5% of the price for closing costs. Saving 15% of take-home pay, that's about 1.8 years for the 5%-down option.

Can I buy a house in Los Angeles on $75k?

A median-priced home there is a stretch. The approximate Q2 2026 median single-family price in Los Angeles is $879,900 (NAR), and buying it with 20% down under the 28% rule needs about $253,000 a year. Condos, smaller homes or outer suburbs are more realistic.

Is it better to use the 28/36 rule or the 43% limit?

Use 28/36 as a comfortable budget and 43% as an upper limit. A 43% debt-to-income ratio would allow $2,688 a month for housing and debts on $75k, but it leaves much less for savings, repairs and emergencies.
