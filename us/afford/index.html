Quick answer

Take 28% of your gross monthly income — that's the most the 28/36 rule says to spend on your mortgage payment including property tax and insurance (PITI). At the 7.24% average 30-year rate (Freddie Mac, week of October 29, 2026) and 20% down, each $1,000 of monthly PITI buys roughly $149,000 of house; with 3.5% down on an FHA loan, about $119,000.

How much house can I afford? The 28/36 rule explained

Lenders don't start from the house price — they start from your income. The front-end ratio caps your housing payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, plus PMI and HOA dues) at about 28% of gross monthly pay. The back-end ratio caps housing plus every other debt payment at about 36%. Some loans go further: FHA's standard ratios are 31% and 43%, and 43% was the Qualified Mortgage cap until 2022.

Frequently asked questions

How much house can I afford?

Under the 28/36 rule, your monthly housing payment (principal, interest, property tax, insurance) should be no more than 28% of gross monthly income, and all debts no more than 36%. At the 7.24% average rate (October 29, 2026) with 20% down, that works out to roughly 3.5 times your annual salary if you have no other debts.

How much house can I afford on $80k?

About $278,000 with 20% down, or $222,000 with a 3.5% FHA down payment, keeping PITI at $1,867 a month (28% of $6,667). Estimates assume 0.9% property tax and national-average insurance.

What is the 28/36 rule?

A lender guideline: spend no more than 28% of gross monthly income on housing (PITI) and no more than 36% on housing plus all other debt payments such as car loans, student loans and credit cards.

Is 43% DTI still the limit?

43% was the Qualified Mortgage cap until the CFPB replaced it with a price-based test (mandatory since October 2022). It remains FHA's standard back-end ratio and a common benchmark; some programs allow up to 50% with strong credit and reserves.

What salary do I need to buy a median-priced home?

About $120,000 a year to buy the $417,500 national median existing home (NAR, September 2026) with 20% down under the 28% rule at 7.24%, using our tax and insurance assumptions.

Does a home affordability calculator include PMI?

Ours does: with less than 20% down it adds estimated private mortgage insurance (0.60% a year at 5% down, 0.45% at 10% down) or FHA mortgage insurance (0.55% a year plus 1.75% upfront). Your actual quote depends on credit score.

Sources & assumptions

  • Mortgage rate: Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey (PMMS), week of October 29, 2026 — 30-year fixed 7.24% (prior week 7.32%, a year earlier 6.17%), 15-year fixed 6.6%. PMMS is a national average for borrowers with strong credit and 20% down; FHA and low-down-payment quotes differ.
  • National median: National Association of Realtors (NAR), existing-home sales for September 2026 (released October 13, 2026) — $417,500 for all existing homes; NAR's Q3 2026 single-family median was $432,900 (released October 29, 2026).
  • Metro prices (approximate): NAR metropolitan median single-family existing-home prices for Q2 2026, as tabulated in HSH.com's "Income you need to purchase a median-priced existing home" (Q2 2026). Metro medians cover whole metropolitan areas; city neighborhoods and condos differ widely.
  • Property tax: 0.9% of value per year, the 2025 national effective rate for single-family homes (ATTOM). Insurance: $2,490 a year per $400,000 of dwelling coverage (NerdWallet, 2026), scaled to price. Both are national averages — your state can be far higher or lower.
  • Mortgage insurance (estimates): FHA annual MIP 0.55% of the base loan plus 1.75% upfront MIP financed into the loan (HUD, for loans ≤ $726,200 with under 5% down). Conventional PMI assumed at 0.60% a year with 5% down and 0.45% with 10% down for a borrower with good credit; Freddie Mac cites roughly $30–$70 a month per $100,000 borrowed, and actual quotes depend on credit score.
  • Loan limits 2026: conforming baseline $832,750 (FHFA); FHA $541,287 in most counties up to $1,249,125 in high-cost areas (HUD). Closing costs assumed at 3% of price (2%–5% is typical). No HOA dues.
  • Take-home pay: single filer, 2026 federal brackets and $16,100 standard deduction (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32), FICA, no state tax — same engine as our paycheck calculator.
  • The 28/36 rule is a traditional lender guideline, not a law. The 43% debt-to-income cap was the Qualified Mortgage (QM) limit until the CFPB replaced it with a price-based test (mandatory since October 1, 2022); 43% remains FHA's standard back-end ratio and a common benchmark, while Fannie Mae's automated underwriting can approve up to 50% DTI.
