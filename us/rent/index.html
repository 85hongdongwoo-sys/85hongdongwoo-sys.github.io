Quick answer

Divide your yearly gross salary by 40. That is the most rent landlords using the 40× rent rule will approve — and it equals the 30% rule (30% of gross monthly pay). For a safer budget, keep rent and utilities near 30% of take-home pay, which this calculator estimates with 2026 federal, FICA and state taxes.

How much rent should I pay? Three rules of thumb

There's no law that sets how much of your income should go to rent, but three rules dominate: the 30% rule (keep rent and utilities at or below 30% of gross income — the same threshold HUD uses to define a "cost-burdened" household), the 40× rent rule landlords use to screen applicants (annual income at least 40 times the monthly rent), and the 50/30/20 budget, which splits take-home pay into needs, wants and savings. The first two always give the same number; the third is stricter because it starts from pay after taxes.

Each guide below works through all three for one salary or hourly wage, using 2026 federal tax brackets and FICA, a no-income-tax state alongside California and New York, and HUD's Fair Market Rents for big metros to show what that budget actually rents — a studio, a one-bedroom, or a room with roommates.

Frequently asked questions

How much rent can I afford?

A common rule is 30% of gross income: divide your annual salary by 40 to get a monthly rent ceiling (for example $60,000 ÷ 40 = $1,500). For a safer budget, use 30% of take-home pay instead.

What is the 40x rent rule?

Many landlords require your annual gross income to be at least 40 times the monthly rent. A $2,000 apartment needs $80,000 a year. It is mathematically the same as the 30% rule. Guarantors are usually asked for 80 times the rent.

What is the 3x rent rule?

Some landlords instead require monthly gross income of at least three times the rent, which allows rent up to about 33% of gross pay — slightly looser than the 40x rule.

How much rent can I afford on $60k?

About $1,500 a month by the 30% rule and 40x rule. With 2026 federal taxes, take-home in a no-income-tax state is about $4,199 a month, so 30% of take-home is about $1,260.

What salary do I need for a $2,000 apartment?

Under the 40x rule, $80,000 a year ($38.46 an hour full-time). Under the 3x rule, $72,000 a year.

Does the 30% rule include utilities?

Housing-cost guidelines such as HUD's cost-burden measure count rent plus utilities (gross rent). Landlords usually test only the base rent, so leave room for utilities in your own budget.
