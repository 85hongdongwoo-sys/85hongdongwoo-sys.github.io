30% rule vs 40× rent rule: how much rent $45k affords

$45,000 a year is $3,750 a month before taxes, or $21.63 an hour for a 40-hour week. Applying the most common rent rules of thumb to that gross figure gives these monthly limits:

Landlords typically screen with one of two income tests. The 40× rule (annual pay ≥ 40 × monthly rent) lets you rent up to $1,125 — exactly what the 30% rule gives. The 3× rule (monthly pay ≥ 3 × rent) is slightly more lenient at $1,250. Neither one looks at taxes, debt or savings, which is why they are ceilings rather than targets.

This is the income range where living alone becomes possible in much of the country but not in the expensive coastal metros. Your 30% budget of $1,125 is below the U.S. median gross rent of $1,487, so neighborhood and city choice matter more than any formula.

$45k after taxes: take-home pay and rent in three states

Rent comes out of take-home pay, not gross pay, so it helps to see what's actually left. State income tax is where things diverge: Texas, Florida, Washington and six other states don't tax wages, while California and New York do.

50/30/20 budget on $45,000 a year: where rent fits

The 50/30/20 rule splits take-home pay into needs (50%), wants (30%) and savings or extra debt payments (20%). Using the no-tax-state take-home of $3,195 a month, that's $1,597 for needs, $958 for wants and $639 for savings. Rent and utilities have to fit inside the needs bucket together with groceries, transportation, insurance and minimum debt payments.

If rent takes $958, only $639 is left in the needs bucket for food, a car or transit pass, phone and insurance — tight for most people. That's the honest math at this income: either rent below $958, share housing, or let needs run to 55%–60% and save less.

What size apartment can you rent on $45,000 a year?

Tips for renting on $45,000 a year

  • Compare the 30% rule with 30% of your take-home pay; the second is the safer number at this income.
  • Consider a roommate for the first year to build an emergency fund, then move to a solo lease.
  • Ask about utilities: an "all bills paid" unit can be cheaper overall than a lower base rent plus electric, gas and internet.
  • Negotiate lease length — a 13- or 15-month lease sometimes comes with a lower monthly rent or a free month.
  • Keep total debt payments plus rent below about 43% of gross pay; many landlords also look at your debt-to-income ratio.

FAQ: how much rent on $45k

How much rent can I afford on $45,000 a year?

About $1,125 a month including utilities. That's 30% of your $3,750 gross monthly income, and the same as the 40× rent rule ($45,000 ÷ 40). Budgeting from take-home pay, $958 is a more comfortable target.

Is the 40× rent rule the same as the 30% rule?

Yes. 30% of monthly pay equals annual pay ÷ 40, so both give $1,125 on $45,000. The looser "3× the rent" rule allows up to $1,250 (33% of gross).

$45,000 a year is how much a month after taxes?

About $3,195 a month in a no-income-tax state such as Texas or Florida, $3,130 in California and $3,042 in New York (before NYC tax), for a single filer with the 2026 standard deduction and no pre-tax deductions.

Can I afford a 1-bedroom in New York City on $45k?

Not by the 30% rule. HUD's FY2027 Fair Market Rent for a one-bedroom in New York City is $2,729, or 73% of your gross pay; under the 40× rule you'd need about $109,160. Half of a two-bedroom ($1,486) is still above your budget.

Should I use gross or take-home pay for the 30% rule?

Landlords use gross pay. For your own budget, 30% of take-home pay ($958 here) is safer because it accounts for taxes; the gap is $167 a month at this salary.
