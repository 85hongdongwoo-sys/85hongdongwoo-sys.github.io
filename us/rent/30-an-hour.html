30% rule vs 40× rent rule: how much rent $30 an hour affords

$30 an hour for a full-time 40-hour week is $62,400 a year (2,080 hours), or $5,200 a month before taxes. Applying the most common rent rules of thumb to that gross figure gives these monthly limits:

25% of gross$1,300comfortable
30% rule / 40× rule$1,560standard ceiling
3× rent rule$1,733landlord minimum
30% of take-home$1,308no-tax state

The 40× rule is just the 30% rule in landlord language: they multiply the monthly rent by 40 and check that your annual pay is at least that much. $62,400 ÷ 40 = $1,560, the same as 30% of $5,200. Landlords who use "3× the monthly rent" instead allow up to $1,733, because they divide monthly gross pay by three.

Your $1,560 budget sits 5% above the U.S. median gross rent, which means a one-bedroom on your own is realistic in most of the country. The big coastal metros are where it still gets tight — and where landlords enforce the 40× rule most strictly.

$30/hour after taxes: take-home pay and rent in three states

What size apartment can you rent on $30 an hour?

Put simply, $1,560 a month typically gets you a one-bedroom in typical U.S. markets and a studio or small one-bedroom in pricier cities. Nationally, the median household renting pays $1,487 a month in gross rent (Census ACS 2024), so your budget is 5% above the middle of the market.

  • Studio: fits in every metro where a one-bedroom fits, often 10%–20% cheaper.
  • One-bedroom: fits the 30% rule in 1 of 8 big metros above.
  • Two-bedroom alone: fits in 0 of 8 — but a shared two-bedroom is a different story (below).

50/30/20 budget on $30 an hour: where rent fits

The 50/30/20 rule splits take-home pay into needs (50%), wants (30%) and savings or extra debt payments (20%). Using the no-tax-state take-home of $4,360 a month, that's $2,180 for needs, $1,308 for wants and $872 for savings. Rent and utilities have to fit inside the needs bucket together with groceries, transportation, insurance and minimum debt payments.

Tips for renting on $30 an hour

  • Use the 3× rule as an absolute ceiling, not a target — it leaves less room for savings.
  • If you have a car payment, subtract it from your rent budget; transportation is the next-largest expense for most renters.
  • Ask for move-in specials (one month free, waived fees) in newer buildings, which are common when vacancy is high.
  • Raise your 401(k) contribution before you sign a lease — it lowers taxes and forces the rent number to fit what is left.
  • Keep at least three months of rent plus essentials in an emergency fund before upgrading to a pricier apartment.

FAQ: rent on $30 an hour

How much rent can I afford making $30 an hour?

About $1,560 a month if you work 40 hours a week. $30 an hour is $62,400 a year, and the 30% rule (the same as the 40× rent rule) caps rent at $62,400 ÷ 40 = $1,560. Budgeting from take-home pay gives a more comfortable $1,308.

$30 an hour is how much a month after taxes?

About $4,360 a month in a state with no income tax, $4,211 in California and $4,129 in New York (state tax only), for a single filer working 2,080 hours with the 2026 standard deduction. Gross monthly pay is $5,200.

Is $30 an hour enough to rent an apartment by myself?

In many places, yes. A $1,560 budget covers a typical one-bedroom (HUD FY2027 Fair Market Rent) in 1 of the 8 big metros we checked, and it is above the U.S. median gross rent of $1,487.

What hourly wage do I need for a $2,700 apartment?

Under the 40× rule you need about $108,000 a year, which is $51.92 an hour full-time. That's roughly a typical one-bedroom in New York City.

Do landlords count overtime and tips?

Usually only if you can document them consistently — recent pay stubs, a W-2 or tax return. If your hours vary, plan your rent on the hours you are sure to work, and consider a guarantor (often 80× the rent) if you fall short of 40×.
