30% rule vs 40× rent rule: how much rent $60k affords

$60,000 a year is $5,000 a month before taxes, or $28.85 an hour for a 40-hour week. Applying the most common rent rules of thumb to that gross figure gives these monthly limits:

3× rent rule$1,667landlord minimum
30% of take-home$1,260no-tax state

Landlords typically screen with one of two income tests. The 40× rule (annual pay ≥ 40 × monthly rent) lets you rent up to $1,500 — exactly what the 30% rule gives. The 3× rule (monthly pay ≥ 3 × rent) is slightly more lenient at $1,667. Neither one looks at taxes, debt or savings, which is why they are ceilings rather than targets.

Your $1,500 budget sits 1% above the U.S. median gross rent, which means a one-bedroom on your own is realistic in most of the country. The big coastal metros are where it still gets tight — and where landlords enforce the 40× rule most strictly.

$60k after taxes: take-home pay and rent in three states

Rent comes out of take-home pay, not gross pay, so it helps to see what's actually left. For tax year 2026, a single filer earning $60,000 with the $16,100 standard deduction owes about $5,020 in federal income tax and $4,590 in Social Security and Medicare (FICA). State income tax is where things diverge: Texas, Florida, Washington and six other states don't tax wages, while California and New York do.

50/30/20 budget on $60,000 a year: where rent fits

The 50/30/20 rule splits take-home pay into needs (50%), wants (30%) and savings or extra debt payments (20%). Using the no-tax-state take-home of $4,199 a month, that's $2,100 for needs, $1,260 for wants and $840 for savings. Rent and utilities have to fit inside the needs bucket together with groceries, transportation, insurance and minimum debt payments.

Tips for renting on $60,000 a year

  • Use the 3× rule as an absolute ceiling, not a target — it leaves less room for savings.
  • If you have a car payment, subtract it from your rent budget; transportation is the next-largest expense for most renters.
  • Ask for move-in specials (one month free, waived fees) in newer buildings, which are common when vacancy is high.
  • Raise your 401(k) contribution before you sign a lease — it lowers taxes and forces the rent number to fit what is left.
  • Keep at least three months of rent plus essentials in an emergency fund before upgrading to a pricier apartment.

FAQ: how much rent on $60k

How much rent can I afford on $60,000 a year?

About $1,500 a month including utilities. That's 30% of your $5,000 gross monthly income, and the same as the 40× rent rule ($60,000 ÷ 40). Budgeting from take-home pay, $1,260 is a more comfortable target.

Is the 40× rent rule the same as the 30% rule?

Yes. 30% of monthly pay equals annual pay ÷ 40, so both give $1,500 on $60,000. The looser "3× the rent" rule allows up to $1,667 (33% of gross).

$60,000 a year is how much a month after taxes?

About $4,199 a month in a no-income-tax state such as Texas or Florida, $4,063 in California and $3,979 in New York (before NYC tax), for a single filer with the 2026 standard deduction and no pre-tax deductions.

Can I afford a 1-bedroom in New York City on $60k?

Not by the 30% rule. HUD's FY2027 Fair Market Rent for a one-bedroom in New York City is $2,729, or 55% of your gross pay; under the 40× rule you'd need about $109,160. Half of a two-bedroom ($1,486) is within your budget.

Should I use gross or take-home pay for the 30% rule?

Landlords use gross pay. For your own budget, 30% of take-home pay ($1,260 here) is safer because it accounts for taxes; the gap is $240 a month at this salary.
