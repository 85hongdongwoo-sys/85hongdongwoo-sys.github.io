Your numbers

The FIRE number

Your FIRE number is the portfolio that can pay your yearly spending forever (or for a long retirement): spending ÷ withdrawal rate. At 4%, that is 25× your annual spending — $1.25 million for $50,000 a year.

Coast FI

You are Coast FI when your current investments, left to grow with no new savings, will reach your FIRE number by a traditional retirement age (65 here). After that you only need to cover today's expenses.

Lean and Fat FIRE

Lean FIRE targets a minimal budget; Fat FIRE allows a more comfortable lifestyle. Remember health insurance before Medicare at 65 and taxes on withdrawals when you set your spending.

FAQ

What is the 4% rule?

It suggests you can withdraw 4% of your portfolio in the first year of retirement and adjust for inflation afterwards, with a high historical chance of the money lasting 30 years.

How much do I need to retire early?

About 25 to 30 times your yearly spending. Someone spending $40,000 a year needs roughly $1.0–1.2 million.

Should I use real or nominal returns?

Use real (after-inflation) returns so your FIRE number stays in today's dollars. 5–7% is a common assumption for a stock-heavy portfolio.
