$60k a year is how much a month in Colorado? Compared with what people earn

$60,000 works out to $5,000 a month before tax and $4,038 after. To judge whether that's "good", compare it with the people around you. The U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey puts median earnings for a Colorado worker at $54,163 (all workers with earnings, 2024 ACS 1-year) and median household income at $99,729 (2025 ACS 1-year, released October 2026). Your salary is 11% above the typical individual and 40% below the typical household, which often has two earners.

Median earnings include part-time workers, so full-time salaries naturally sit above it. National medians: earnings $46,949, household income $84,780.

How much rent can you afford on $60k in Colorado?

The 30% rule says housing (rent plus utilities) should stay at or below 30% of gross income. On $60,000 that is $1,500 a month. A stricter version uses take-home pay: 30% of $4,038 is $1,211.

The median gross rent in Colorado is $1,822 a month (rent plus utilities, ACS 2024), which is 36% of your gross pay — above the 30% rule. You can make it work, but you would want a place below the median or a roommate to keep saving.

Can you live on $60k in Denver?

Colorado has a flat income tax a little above 4%. The Front Range — Denver, Boulder, Fort Collins and Colorado Springs — holds most jobs and the highest rents; mountain resort towns are even pricier.

MIT's Living Wage Calculator (data updated February 2026) puts the living wage for a single adult with no children in Colorado at $26.00 an hour, or $54,071 a year before tax. $60,000 equals $28.85 an hour for a 40-hour week — $2.85 an hour above that line.

In Denver specifically, $60k is likely to feel tight: the statewide living-wage figure averages expensive metro counties with cheaper rural ones, and big-city rents usually sit above the state median. Denver and Boulder are the costliest Front Range areas; Aurora, Colorado Springs and Pueblo are cheaper. Denver and Aurora also charge a small monthly occupational privilege tax.

How much can you save on $60,000 in Colorado?

Setting aside the 20% share — about $808 a month or $9,692 a year — would build a six-month emergency fund ($12,115, based on the 50% needs bucket) in about 15 months. Kept up for ten years at an assumed 5% annual return, those deposits would grow to roughly $125,412 — an illustration, not a forecast. Contributing to a 401(k) also lowers taxable income: each $1,000 put in pre-tax saves about $164 in federal and state income tax at this salary.
