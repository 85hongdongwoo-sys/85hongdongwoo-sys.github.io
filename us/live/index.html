How to read these guides

A salary is only “good” relative to where you live. $60,000 leaves about $4,199 a month in Texas but $4,063 in California — and rent differs even more than taxes. Each guide answers one question, such as is 60k a good salary in Texas or can you live on 80k in California, using the same 2026 tax engine as our salary-after-tax tables, Census Bureau medians and MIT's Living Wage Calculator.

Pick your state and salary below. Every page shows take-home pay (annual, monthly, biweekly, hourly), the rent you can afford by the 30% rule against the state's median rent, a 50/30/20 budget, savings potential and a comparison with neighbouring states.

Frequently asked questions

What salary do you need to live comfortably in the U.S.?

It depends heavily on the state. MIT's Living Wage Calculator (Feb 2026) puts the pre-tax income a single adult needs for basic costs between about $43,700 (Ohio) and $63,600 (Massachusetts) among the 20 largest states. "Comfortable" — with room to save 20% — typically takes roughly 1.5–2× that.

Which large states leave the most take-home pay?

Texas, Florida, Washington and Tennessee do not tax wages, so a given salary leaves the most there. Among large states with an income tax, Arizona (flat 2.5%) and Ohio are lightest at middle incomes.

How are these salary guides calculated?

Each guide uses the same 2026 tax engine as our salary-after-tax pages (single filer, standard deduction, no 401(k)), Census ACS medians for income and rent, and MIT living-wage cost estimates.

Is household income the right comparison for my salary?

Only partly. Median household income often includes two earners; median earnings per worker (also shown on each page) is the closer comparison for one person's salary.
