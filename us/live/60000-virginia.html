$60k a year is how much a month in Virginia? Compared with what people earn

$60,000 works out to $5,000 a month before tax and $3,980 after. To judge whether that's "good", compare it with the people around you. The U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey puts median earnings for a Virginia worker at $51,322 (all workers with earnings, 2024 ACS 1-year) and median household income at $99,322 (2025 ACS 1-year, released October 2026). Your salary is 17% above the typical individual and 40% below the typical household, which often has two earners.

Median earnings include part-time workers, so full-time salaries naturally sit above it. National medians: earnings $46,949, household income $84,780.

How much rent can you afford on $60k in Virginia?

The 30% rule says housing (rent plus utilities) should stay at or below 30% of gross income. On $60,000 that is $1,500 a month. A stricter version uses take-home pay: 30% of $3,980 is $1,194.

The median gross rent in Virginia is $1,646 a month (rent plus utilities, ACS 2024), which is 33% of your gross pay — above the 30% rule. You can make it work, but you would want a place below the median or a roommate to keep saving.

Can you live on $60k in Northern Virginia (Washington, D.C. metro)?

Virginia's top income tax rate of 5.75% starts at just $17,000 of taxable income, so most earners effectively pay close to a flat rate. Northern Virginia is one of the highest-income, highest-rent regions in the country; Richmond, Hampton Roads and Roanoke cost less.

MIT's Living Wage Calculator (data updated February 2026) puts the living wage for a single adult with no children in Virginia at $25.72 an hour, or $53,505 a year before tax. $60,000 equals $28.85 an hour for a 40-hour week — $3.13 an hour above that line.

In Northern Virginia (Washington, D.C. metro) specifically, $60k is likely to feel tight: the statewide living-wage figure averages expensive metro counties with cheaper rural ones, and big-city rents usually sit above the state median. Northern Virginia (Arlington, Alexandria, Fairfax, Loudoun) rents are close to those of the District itself; Richmond and Hampton Roads give a similar paycheck much more room.

How much can you save on $60,000 in Virginia?

Setting aside the 20% share — about $796 a month or $9,551 a year — would build a six-month emergency fund ($11,939, based on the 50% needs bucket) in about 15 months. Kept up for ten years at an assumed 5% annual return, those deposits would grow to roughly $123,589 — an illustration, not a forecast. Contributing to a 401(k) also lowers taxable income: each $1,000 put in pre-tax saves about $178 in federal and state income tax at this salary.
