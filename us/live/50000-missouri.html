$50k a year is how much a month in Missouri? Compared with what people earn

$50,000 works out to $4,167 a month before tax and $3,416 after. To judge whether that's "good", compare it with the people around you. The U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey puts median earnings for a Missouri worker at $43,094 (all workers with earnings, 2024 ACS 1-year) and median household income at $75,485 (2025 ACS 1-year, released October 2026). Your salary is 16% above the typical individual and 34% below the typical household, which often has two earners.

Median earnings include part-time workers, so full-time salaries naturally sit above it. National medians: earnings $46,949, household income $84,780.

How much rent can you afford on $50k in Missouri?

The 30% rule says housing (rent plus utilities) should stay at or below 30% of gross income. On $50,000 that is $1,250 a month. A stricter version uses take-home pay: 30% of $3,416 is $1,025.

The median gross rent in Missouri is $1,067 a month (rent plus utilities, ACS 2024). That is only 26% of your gross monthly pay — inside the classic 30% guideline — so a typical apartment is within reach without roommates.

Can you live on $50k in St. Louis?

Missouri's income tax tops out below 5%, but Kansas City and St. Louis each levy a 1% earnings tax on residents and people who work in the city. Statewide rents are among the lowest of the large states.

MIT's Living Wage Calculator (data updated February 2026) puts the living wage for a single adult with no children in Missouri at $21.27 an hour, or $44,244 a year before tax. $50,000 equals $24.04 an hour for a 40-hour week — $2.77 an hour above that line.

In St. Louis specifically, $50k is likely to feel tight: the statewide living-wage figure averages expensive metro counties with cheaper rural ones, and big-city rents usually sit above the state median. St. Louis and Kansas City are both comparatively affordable big metros; remember the 1% city earnings tax if you live or work inside city limits.

How much can you save on $50,000 in Missouri?

Setting aside the 20% share — about $683 a month or $8,197 a year — would build a six-month emergency fund ($10,247, based on the 50% needs bucket) in about 15 months. Kept up for ten years at an assumed 5% annual return, those deposits would grow to roughly $106,076 — an illustration, not a forecast. Even a small 401(k) contribution with an employer match is worth prioritizing at this income.

$50,000 a year is how much a month after taxes in Missouri?

About $3,416 a month, $1,576 every two weeks, or $788 a week, for a single filer taking the 2026 standard deduction with no 401(k) or other pre-tax deductions. Total taxes are $9,013 (18.0% of gross).
