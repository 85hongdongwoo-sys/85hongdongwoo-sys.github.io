$90k a year is how much a month in Illinois? Compared with what people earn

$90,000 works out to $7,500 a month before tax and $5,653 after. To judge whether that's "good", compare it with the people around you. The U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey puts median earnings for a Illinois worker at $49,650 (all workers with earnings, 2024 ACS 1-year) and median household income at $86,676 (2025 ACS 1-year, released October 2026). Your salary is 81% above the typical individual and 4% above the typical household, which often has two earners.

Median earnings include part-time workers, so full-time salaries naturally sit above it. National medians: earnings $46,949, household income $84,780.

How much rent can you afford on $90k in Illinois?

The 30% rule says housing (rent plus utilities) should stay at or below 30% of gross income. On $90,000 that is $2,250 a month. A stricter version uses take-home pay: 30% of $5,653 is $1,696.

The median gross rent in Illinois is $1,322 a month (rent plus utilities, ACS 2024). That is only 18% of your gross monthly pay — inside the classic 30% guideline — so a typical apartment is within reach without roommates.

Can you live on $90k in Chicago?

Illinois taxes income at a flat 4.95%, and its property taxes are among the highest in the country. Housing costs are dominated by the Chicago area; downstate cities such as Peoria, Springfield and Champaign–Urbana are far cheaper.

MIT's Living Wage Calculator (data updated February 2026) puts the living wage for a single adult with no children in Illinois at $24.42 an hour, or $50,796 a year before tax. $90,000 equals $43.27 an hour for a 40-hour week — $18.85 an hour above that line.

In Chicago, $90k is workable for one person, especially outside the most in-demand neighborhoods, but it is not a salary that makes big-city living effortless. Chicago is one of the more affordable of the very large U.S. metros: neighborhood choice (North Side lakefront vs. outlying neighborhoods or suburbs) matters more than anything else for a single earner's budget.

How much can you save on $90,000 in Illinois?

Setting aside the 20% share — about $1,131 a month or $13,567 a year — would build a six-month emergency fund ($16,959, based on the 50% needs bucket) in about 15 months. Kept up for ten years at an assumed 5% annual return, those deposits would grow to roughly $175,559 — an illustration, not a forecast. Contributing to a 401(k) also lowers taxable income: each $1,000 put in pre-tax saves about $270 in federal and state income tax at this salary.
