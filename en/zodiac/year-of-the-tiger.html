Tiger personality and symbolism

Tiger years are read as courageous, energetic and independent. Tigers are said to love a challenge, stand up for the underdog and lead naturally, sometimes before checking who is following.

In tradition the Tiger is bold and magnetic: it acts on conviction, protects its own fiercely and prefers freedom to rules it did not write.

The tiger was revered across Korea, China and Vietnam as the king of the mountains and a protector against evil, which is why tiger paintings were hung at the New Year. The Tiger month opens the solar year at Ipchun (Start of Spring).

Strengths (traditional)

  • courageous
  • protective
  • charismatic
  • decisive
  • generous

Cautions (traditional)

  • impulsive
  • rebellious against authority
  • short-tempered
  • restless with routine

Love and relationships

Passionate and generous, with grand gestures; Tigers need a partner who respects their independence and can match their energy.

Career and work style

At home in leadership, entrepreneurship, advocacy, sport, the military, emergency services and any role that needs nerve.

These are traditional folk associations, not psychological findings.

FAQ: Year of the Tiger

What years are the Year of the Tiger?

Recent Tiger years are 1950, 1962, 1974, 1986, 1998, 2010, 2022. Each starts at Lunar New Year, not on January 1, so check the exact dates in the table.

Who should a Tiger marry, according to tradition?

Tradition favours the Pig (Six Harmony) and the Horse and Dog (Three Harmony). The Monkey is the clashing sign. This is folklore for entertainment; real relationships depend on the people.

What are the five types of Tiger?

Depending on the year’s stem, a Tiger is Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal or Water: Wood Tiger 1974; Fire Tiger 1986; Earth Tiger 1998; Metal Tiger 1950, 2010; Water Tiger 1962, 2022.

Is 2027 a good year for the Tiger?

What is the Tiger in Korean?

Sources and method

  • Lunar New Year dates, new-moon instants and Ipchun (立春) times computed with the open-source lunar-javascript calendar library (Chinese calendar, Beijing time, UTC+8), bundled on this site. Korean Seollal differs from the Chinese date when the new moon falls just before midnight Beijing time; Korean dates here are derived for Korea Standard Time (e.g. Seollal 2027 is Feb 7 in Korea, Feb 6 in China — matching the published Korean public-holiday calendar).
  • Vietnamese calendar differences (1968 split between North and South, 1985 a month apart, Cat instead of Rabbit, Water Buffalo for Ox): Wikipedia, “Vietnamese calendar”.
  • Six Harmony, Three Harmony, Clash, Harm and Punishment groupings and the 60 nayin (納音) names: standard tables of the traditional Chinese calendar, also used by the site’s Saju calculator.
  • Lucky numbers and colours: as commonly listed in modern popular zodiac guides (e.g. eastchinatrip.com); lists vary between sources and are folklore, not tradition with a single authority.
  • Korea abolished “Korean age” for legal and administrative use on June 28, 2023 (amended Civil Act), so ages here are international ages unless labelled.
