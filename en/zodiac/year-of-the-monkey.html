Monkey personality and symbolism

Monkey years are associated with wit, curiosity and versatility. Monkeys are said to learn fast, solve problems creatively and keep any group entertained.

Tradition sees the Monkey as the inventor of the zodiac: quick, playful and endlessly inventive, at its best when it has a new puzzle to crack.

The Monkey King, Sun Wukong of Journey to the West, gave the sign its reputation for brilliance, mischief and endless tricks. Japan’s three wise monkeys (see no evil, hear no evil, speak no evil) are another monkey motif.

Strengths (traditional)

  • intelligent
  • versatile
  • humorous
  • inventive
  • sociable

Cautions (traditional)

  • restless
  • can be mischievous
  • bored by routine
  • over-confident

Love and relationships

Fun and lively, needing mental stimulation; Monkeys look for a partner who can keep up with their humour and ideas.

Career and work style

Strong in technology, engineering, trading, marketing, entertainment, journalism and problem-solving roles.

These are traditional folk associations, not psychological findings.

FAQ: Year of the Monkey

What years are the Year of the Monkey?

Recent Monkey years are 1944, 1956, 1968, 1980, 1992, 2004, 2016, 2028. Each starts at Lunar New Year, not on January 1, so check the exact dates in the table.

Who should a Monkey marry, according to tradition?

Tradition favours the Snake (Six Harmony) and the Rat and Dragon (Three Harmony). The Tiger is the clashing sign. This is folklore for entertainment; real relationships depend on the people.

What are the five types of Monkey?

Depending on the year’s stem, a Monkey is Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal or Water: Metal Monkey 1980; Water Monkey 1992; Wood Monkey 1944, 2004; Fire Monkey 1956, 2016; Earth Monkey 1968, 2028.

  • Lunar New Year dates, new-moon instants and Ipchun (立春) times computed with the open-source lunar-javascript calendar library (Chinese calendar, Beijing time, UTC+8), bundled on this site.
