How the chart is built: the five classical relationships

The twelve earthly branches sit around a circle like a clock: Rat at north (midnight), Horse at south (noon). Compatibility rules come from their geometry and the elements they represent.

1. Six Harmony (六合, 육합)

Six pairs that combine into a new element. Tradition calls them “secret friends” who support each other quietly: Rat–Ox, Tiger–Pig, Rabbit–Dog, Dragon–Rooster, Snake–Monkey, Horse–Goat.

2. Three Harmony (三合, 삼합)

Signs four places apart form a triangle with a shared element and similar goals:

  • Water trine: Monkey – Rat – Dragon
  • Metal trine: Snake – Rooster – Ox
  • Fire trine: Tiger – Horse – Dog
  • Wood trine: Pig – Rabbit – Goat

3. Clash (六冲, 충)

Directly opposite signs have contrasting rhythms: Rat–Horse, Ox–Goat, Tiger–Monkey, Rabbit–Rooster, Dragon–Dog, Snake–Pig. Many traditions still see attraction in opposites; the advice is to negotiate rather than avoid.

4. Harm (六害, 해) and Punishment (刑, 형)

Harm pairs (Rat–Goat, Ox–Horse, Tiger–Snake, Rabbit–Dragon, Monkey–Pig, Rooster–Dog) are said to cause slow friction. Punishment groups are Tiger–Snake–Monkey, Ox–Goat–Dog and Rat–Rabbit; Dragon, Horse, Rooster and Pig “punish themselves”, which tradition reads as being one’s own toughest critic.

5. Korean wonjin (원진살)

Korean folk gunghap adds six wonjin pairs, believed to bring resentment: Rat–Goat, Ox–Horse, Tiger–Rooster, Rabbit–Monkey, Dragon–Pig, Snake–Dog. Rat–Goat and Ox–Horse appear in both the harm and wonjin lists.

Why one table is never the full story

Year-animal compatibility was traditionally a quick first filter, often used by matchmakers alongside family background. A Saju compatibility (gunghap) reading compares the day masters, the five-element balance of both charts and how the two charts’ branches combine. Two “clashing” year animals can have very harmonious day pillars, and vice versa. Use the chart for conversation and fun; use communication for the relationship.

FAQ: zodiac compatibility

Which Chinese zodiac signs are most compatible?

Tradition rates the Six Harmony pairs highest: Rat–Ox, Tiger–Pig, Rabbit–Dog, Dragon–Rooster, Snake–Monkey and Horse–Goat. The four Three Harmony trines (Rat–Dragon–Monkey, Ox–Snake–Rooster, Tiger–Horse–Dog, Rabbit–Goat–Pig) come next.

Which zodiac signs clash?

Signs six places apart clash: Rat–Horse, Ox–Goat, Tiger–Monkey, Rabbit–Rooster, Dragon–Dog and Snake–Pig.

What is wonjin in Korean compatibility?

Wonjin (원진) is a Korean folk pairing said to cause resentment: Rat–Goat, Ox–Horse, Tiger–Rooster, Rabbit–Monkey, Dragon–Pig and Snake–Dog.

Is zodiac compatibility scientific?

No. It is a cultural tradition enjoyed for fun. Many happy couples are “clashing” signs, and Saju compatibility looks at the whole birth chart rather than the year animal alone.

How do I find my sign if I was born in January or February?

Check the Lunar New Year date for your birth year on its year page; births before that date belong to the previous animal.

Sources and method

  • Lunar New Year dates, new-moon instants and Ipchun (立春) times computed with the open-source lunar-javascript calendar library (Chinese calendar, Beijing time, UTC+8), bundled on this site. Korean Seollal differs from the Chinese date when the new moon falls just before midnight Beijing time; Korean dates here are derived for Korea Standard Time (e.g. Seollal 2027 is Feb 7 in Korea, Feb 6 in China — matching the published Korean public-holiday calendar).
  • Vietnamese calendar differences (1968 split between North and South, 1985 a month apart, Cat instead of Rabbit, Water Buffalo for Ox): Wikipedia, “Vietnamese calendar”.
  • Six Harmony, Three Harmony, Clash, Harm and Punishment groupings and the 60 nayin (納音) names: standard tables of the traditional Chinese calendar, also used by the site’s Saju calculator.
  • Lucky numbers and colours: as commonly listed in modern popular zodiac guides (e.g. eastchinatrip.com); lists vary between sources and are folklore, not tradition with a single authority.
  • Korea abolished “Korean age” for legal and administrative use on June 28, 2023 (amended Civil Act), so ages here are international ages unless labelled.
