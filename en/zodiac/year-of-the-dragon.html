Dragon personality and symbolism

Dragon years are associated with confidence, vision and vitality. Dragons are said to aim high, enjoy the spotlight and inspire others with their certainty.

Tradition sees the Dragon as larger than life: idealistic, energetic and lucky, with a pride that pushes it to great things and occasionally into great arguments.

The only mythical creature in the cycle, the dragon was the symbol of emperors, rain and good fortune. Dragon years are still popular for births in Chinese communities, a well-documented "dragon baby" effect in places such as Taiwan, Hong Kong and Singapore.

Love and relationships

Passionate and loyal, sometimes demanding; Dragons want an admiring partner who also has a life and goals of their own.

Career and work style

Drawn to leadership, entrepreneurship, politics, the arts, media, law and large-scale projects.

These are traditional folk associations, not psychological findings.

The five types of Dragon: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water

FAQ: Year of the Dragon

What years are the Year of the Dragon?

Recent Dragon years are 1940, 1952, 1964, 1976, 1988, 2000, 2012, 2024. Each starts at Lunar New Year, not on January 1, so check the exact dates in the table.

Who should a Dragon marry, according to tradition?

Tradition favours the Rooster (Six Harmony) and the Monkey and Rat (Three Harmony). The Dog is the clashing sign. This is folklore for entertainment; real relationships depend on the people.

What are the five types of Dragon?

Depending on the year’s stem, a Dragon is Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal or Water: Fire Dragon 1976; Earth Dragon 1988; Metal Dragon 1940, 2000; Water Dragon 1952, 2012; Wood Dragon 1964, 2024.

Is 2027 a good year for the Dragon?

No special relationship with the Goat: Your sign has no classical harmony or clash with the Goat, so 2027 is read as a "neutral" year in which results depend on your own effort and on the rest of your chart rather than on the year sign.

What is the Dragon in Korean?

The Dragon is 용 (辰) in Korean, and people born in its years are called 용띠.

Sources and method

  • Lunar New Year dates, new-moon instants and Ipchun (立春) times computed with the open-source lunar-javascript calendar library (Chinese calendar, Beijing time, UTC+8), bundled on this site. Korean Seollal differs from the Chinese date when the new moon falls just before midnight Beijing time; Korean dates here are derived for Korea Standard Time (e.g. Seollal 2027 is Feb 7 in Korea, Feb 6 in China — matching the published Korean public-holiday calendar).
  • Vietnamese calendar differences (1968 split between North and South, 1985 a month apart, Cat instead of Rabbit, Water Buffalo for Ox): Wikipedia, “Vietnamese calendar”.
  • Six Harmony, Three Harmony, Clash, Harm and Punishment groupings and the 60 nayin (納音) names: standard tables of the traditional Chinese calendar, also used by the site’s Saju calculator.
  • Lucky numbers and colours: as commonly listed in modern popular zodiac guides (e.g. eastchinatrip.com); lists vary between sources and are folklore, not tradition with a single authority.
  • Korea abolished “Korean age” for legal and administrative use on June 28, 2023 (amended Civil Act), so ages here are international ages unless labelled.
