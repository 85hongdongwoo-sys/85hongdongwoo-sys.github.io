Average IQ of Teachers

Three independent data sources on teachers’ IQ, how much teachers vary, and what research says about teacher ability and student learning.

The average IQ of teachers is about 110–112, roughly the 75th–79th percentile. Two very different sources — 524 UK secondary teachers in the Understanding Society survey (Wolfram, 2023) and US Wonderlic data on teacher applicants (Gottfredson, 1997) — land in almost the same place.

The sources agree closely: school teachers average around 110–112, roughly the 75th–79th percentile, and university teachers slightly higher. The secondary-teacher figure is one of the most precise in the UK data because it rests on more than 500 people (95% uncertainty of the mean about ±1.1 points).

We could not find a comparable, verified figure for primary or elementary teachers in the same dataset, so we do not report one. Claims that primary teachers score notably lower or higher should be checked against their source.

Do smarter teachers produce smarter students?

Teacher cognitive skills do appear to matter somewhat. Using international adult-skills (PIAAC) data, Hanushek, Piopiunik and Wiederhold (2019) found that countries whose teachers have stronger numeracy and literacy skills tend to have higher student achievement. Within schools, however, research on teacher effectiveness consistently finds that classroom management, subject knowledge, feedback and relationships with students explain much of the difference between teachers — and that a teacher’s test scores are only a modest predictor.

Teaching also demands things an IQ test does not sample: patience, organisation, emotional regulation in a room of 30 people, and the ability to explain an idea several different ways. This is one reason teachers with very similar scores can differ a great deal in effectiveness.

Teachers vs. other graduate professions

In the same UK dataset, secondary teachers (109.7) sit close to chartered accountants (110.3) and actuaries, economists and statisticians (110.5), a few points below medical practitioners (112.7) and solicitors (114.2). Those gaps are small relative to the spread within each group, so a randomly chosen teacher has a substantial chance of outscoring a randomly chosen doctor.

Illustration: with means of 109.7 and 112.7 and SD 13 in each group, a random teacher would outscore a random doctor about 56% of the time.

How to read these numbers

Every number on this page comes with three caveats. First, the UK estimates are based on a short survey battery — word recall, number series, everyday numeric problems, serial subtraction and verbal fluency — rather than a full clinical IQ test, so they are less precise for individuals and tend to compress group differences. Second, they describe people working in an occupation at one time (2011–2013), not what the job requires. Third, they are averages: Hauser’s analysis of the Wisconsin Longitudinal Study found that the correlation between occupation and measured ability was only moderate (about .39–.44), and that the spread of ability was almost as wide in high-status occupations as in others.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average IQ of teachers?

About 110–112 in the best available data: 109.7 for UK secondary teachers (Wolfram, 2023, N = 524) and a median of about 112 for teacher applicants on the Wonderlic test (Gottfredson, 1997).

Do university professors have higher IQs than school teachers?

Slightly, on average: about 111 for UK higher education teachers versus about 110 for secondary teachers in the same dataset, a small difference.

What IQ do you need to be a teacher?

There is no IQ requirement. Teacher training selects on degrees and, in some countries, literacy and numeracy tests.

Does teacher IQ affect student results?

Somewhat. Cross-country evidence links higher teacher cognitive skills to higher student achievement, but classroom skills and relationships explain much of the difference between individual teachers.
