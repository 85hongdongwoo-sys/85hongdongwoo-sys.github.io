IQ Score Ranges & Classification

How the major IQ tests label each score range, how many people fall in each, and how to read the labels responsibly.

IQ scores are grouped into descriptive ranges. On today’s Wechsler tests, 90–109 is Average, 110–119 High Average, 120–129 Superior (WAIS-IV) or Very High (WISC-V), and 130+ is the top band; below average, 80–89 is Low Average, 70–79 Borderline or Very Low, and 69 or below Extremely Low. The labels differ between tests, but the numbers mean the same thing on a mean-100, SD-15 scale.

The Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale is the most widely used individual adult IQ test. Its full-scale IQ ranges from 40 to 160, and its norms are based on 2,200 people aged 16 to 90. The fifth edition (WAIS-5, 2024) is now replacing it; its sample score report uses plainer descriptors such as “Average”, “Above average” and “Very high”.

The WISC-V replaced value-laden words like “Superior” and “Borderline” with neutral, symmetrical descriptors (“Very High” / “Very Low”). The bands themselves are the same 10-point bands used by the WAIS-IV.

Where the ranges come from

Modern IQ is a deviation score: your performance is compared with people of your age and converted to a scale with mean 100 and standard deviation 15. The bands are mostly 10 points wide, centred on the mean, so that 90–109 captures the middle 50%. Earlier tests used a ratio IQ (mental age ÷ chronological age × 100), and Terman’s 1916 Stanford-Binet attached labels to the results, such as “near genius or genius” for scores above 140. Some historical labels for low scores are now considered offensive and were dropped from clinical use decades ago; modern descriptors are chosen to describe a score’s position, not a person.

What the ranges do and do not mean

  • The label describes the score, not the person. Clinicians are trained to write “a score in the Low Average range”, not “a low-average person”.
  • Boundaries are arbitrary: 109 and 110 are statistically identical, even though the label changes.
  • A score of 70 or below is not by itself an intellectual disability; diagnosis also requires deficits in adaptive functioning and onset in childhood (DSM-5).
  • Giftedness programmes use different cut-offs (often 125–130) and usually require additional evidence.
  • Every score has a confidence interval of roughly ±5–6 points on a full test, so it can straddle two bands.

Frequently asked questions

What are the IQ score ranges?

On Wechsler tests: 130+ top band, 120–129 Superior/Very High, 110–119 High Average, 90–109 Average, 80–89 Low Average, 70–79 Borderline/Very Low, 69 and below Extremely Low.

What percentage of people have an average IQ?

About 50% score between 90 and 109 on the normal model.

What is the difference between WAIS-IV and WISC-V labels?

The score bands are the same; the WISC-V uses neutral words such as Very High and Very Low instead of Superior and Borderline.

Is an IQ of 130 gifted?

On most definitions yes: 130+ is the top band on Wechsler tests and the SB5 calls 130–144 Gifted.

Does an IQ below 70 mean intellectual disability?

Not on its own. Diagnosis also needs deficits in everyday adaptive functioning that began during development.
