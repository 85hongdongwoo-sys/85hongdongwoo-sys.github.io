How the ovulation calculator works

The luteal phase — the time from ovulation to your next period — is fairly constant — usually 10 to 16 days, about 14 on average, while the first half of the cycle varies. So the calculator works backwards: next period = last period + cycle length, then ovulation = next period − luteal phase. With a 28-day cycle and a 14-day luteal phase, ovulation falls on cycle day 15 (day 14 after your period started).

The fertile window is the 5 days before ovulation, ovulation day itself and the day after. Sperm can live inside the body for about 3–5 days (UK guidance says up to 7), but the egg can be fertilized for only about 24 hours, so the two days before ovulation and ovulation day give the highest chance of pregnancy. The due date if you conceive is ovulation + 266 days, the same as 280 days from the last period in a standard cycle.

Frequently asked questions

How is ovulation day calculated?
After ovulation, the luteal phase lasts a fairly steady 10–16 days (about 14 on average) before the next period starts. So the calculator predicts your next period from your cycle length and counts back the luteal phase: with a 28-day cycle and a 14-day luteal phase, ovulation is day 15, counting the first day of your period as day 1.
What is the fertile window?
It is the six days ending on ovulation day plus the following day. Sperm can survive in the reproductive tract for about 3–5 days (sometimes longer), while the egg can be fertilized for only about 24 hours after ovulation, so sex in the days before ovulation gives the best chance of pregnancy.
Can I use this calculator as birth control?
No. Calendar predictions can be off by several days because ovulation varies from cycle to cycle, especially with irregular cycles, after childbirth or while breastfeeding. Use a reliable contraceptive method if you do not want to get pregnant.
