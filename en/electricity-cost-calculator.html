How to calculate electricity cost

Energy (kWh) = power (W) × hours per day × days ÷ 1,000. Cost = kWh × price per kWh. For example, a 1,000 W heater used 1 hour a day for 30 days uses 30 kWh; at US$0.18 per kWh that is about US$5.40 a month.

The default prices are typical all-in household rates (energy plus taxes where the source includes them) and do not include fixed daily or monthly charges. Your own rate can be very different by state, province, supplier, plan or time of day, so type the unit rate from your bill into the price box to override it.

South Korea uses a progressive tariff: the price per kWh depends on the household's total monthly use. With the KEPCO option on, the calculator works out the bill with and without your appliances (tiers 120.0 / 214.6 / 307.3 won per kWh, climate charge 9.0 won, Q4 2026 fuel adjustment +5.0 won, 10% VAT and the 2.7% power fund) and shows the difference.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to run a 1,000-watt appliance?
A 1,000 W (1 kW) appliance uses 1 kWh per hour. Running it 1 hour a day for 30 days uses 30 kWh, which costs 30 × your price per kWh — about US$5.50 at the US average of 18.34 cents.
How do I convert watts to kWh?
Multiply watts by hours of use and divide by 1,000. A 60 W laptop used 6 hours a day uses 0.36 kWh per day, or about 10.8 kWh in a 30-day month.
Why is my bill higher than the calculator result?
Bills add fixed daily or monthly charges, and many tariffs have tiers, time-of-use pricing or local taxes. The calculator shows the usage cost of the appliances you list, so enter the unit rate from your own bill for the closest result.
