  • Hidden stems: 癸 (Seven Killings), 辛 (Indirect Wealth), 己 (Eating God) · life stage Tomb

Jeong-Chuk day pillar personality

  • determined
  • frugal
  • skilled

Jeong-Chuk is a small flame protected inside a storehouse. The Ox earth absorbs Fire as Eating God, so the reading is craftsmanship, patience and a quiet capacity to produce. It is also one of the classical Baekho pillars, which tradition associates with hidden intensity. Jeong-Chuk people look calm but carry strong determination; they need to vent before frustration builds.

The Jeong day master

Jeong (丁) is the candle and the starlight: focused, warm and perceptive. Its light is small but steady, so Jeong people tend to give deep attention to a few people and a few crafts.

Sitting on Chuk (丑)

Chuk (丑) is frozen winter soil that stores water and metal. As a day branch it adds patience, frugality and a slow, sure way of building things. Its main hidden stem, 己, is Eating God to a Jeong day master (talent, enjoyment, steady output), so the inner self leans toward talent.

Like every one of the 60 pillars, Jeong-Chuk pairs a stem and a branch of the same polarity, so it is a purely yin pillar: receptive, inward and subtle in expression.

Hidden stems and the 12 life stages

Every earthly branch carries one to three hidden stems (지장간, jijanggan). Their share follows the traditional day-count table (월률분야) used by the site’s Saju calculator. Each hidden stem becomes a ten god when compared with the day master 丁.

Life stage: Tomb (묘)

Tomb (Myo) is the storehouse where things are kept: thrift, patience and hidden reserves. It suggests someone who saves, plans and reveals their strength slowly. For Jeong, that stage falls exactly on Chuk, so the pillar’s own branch shows storage and thrift.

Five-element balance

On its own, the Jeong-Chuk pillar is led by Fire (50%), followed by Earth (30%), Water (15%), Metal (5%). Wood is absent from the pillar itself.

One pillar is only two of the eight characters in a full chart; the year, month and hour pillars often fill the gaps. Tradition treats under-represented elements as balancing themes, for example Wood → green, mornings, walks among trees, starting a course; Metal → decluttering, deep breathing, clear decisions.

Jeong-Chuk in love and compatibility

The day branch is called the spouse palace. With Eating God in the spouse palace, home life is relaxed, affectionate and food-loving. This is traditionally one of the most comfortable spouse-palace stars, as long as comfort does not turn into complacency.

As a Jeong day master, Jeong-Chuk is devoted and romantic, remembers small details and expects the same sincerity in return. Storage (“flower-canopy”) branch: Jin, Sul, Chuk and Mi close each season and store its energy. Tradition links them with Hwagae (華蓋): reflection, study, art and spiritual interests.

Compatible and challenging day pillars

  • Best traditional match: Im-Ja (壬子) — stems combine (丁 + 壬 → Wood) and branches form a six-harmony (丑 + 子).
  • Friendly branches: Ja (Rat) (six-harmony) and Sa (巳) and Yu (酉) (three-harmony Metal frame).
  • Most tension: Gye-Mi (癸未) — the branches clash (丑 vs 未) and the stems clash.

Compatibility in Saju compares whole charts; the day pillar is only one piece. Treat this as a conversation starter, not a verdict on any relationship.

Jeong-Chuk day pillar for women and men

Personality traits above apply to everyone. Gender enters only through an old convention: classical texts treat Wealth as the spouse star in a man’s chart and Officer in a woman’s chart. Modern readers apply the spouse palace to any partner.

Jeong-Chuk woman

Seven Killings (癸) sits in the spouse palace as the residual qi, which classical texts read, for a woman’s chart, as a close and quiet but present bond: the partner star is “at home”. Being a hidden minor star, its influence is subtler and often grows with age.

Jeong-Chuk man

Indirect Wealth (辛) sits in the spouse palace as the middle qi, which classical texts read, for a man’s chart, as a close and quiet but present bond: the partner star is “at home”. Being a hidden minor star, its influence is subtler and often grows with age.

Jeong-Chuk career and work style

Career themes in Saju come from the day master and from the star in the spouse palace, which also shows how a person behaves in private and in partnerships.

  • From the Jeong stem: research and analysis, medicine and care, teaching and mentoring, arts, writing and craft.
  • From Eating God in the day branch: skilled crafts, food, teaching and steady creative output.
  • From the Tomb stage: finance, archives, property and long-term building.

Work that draws on the pillar’s keywords (determined, frugal, skilled) tends to feel most natural for Jeong-Chuk.

For Jeong day masters 2027 is a Friend year: the yearly stem is your own character, and Mi is a Coming-of-Age stage for Jeong. Tradition reads confidence, independence and new circles of peers; keep an eye on splitting resources too thin.

Chuk and Mi clash directly (丑未沖), and both belong to the Chuk-Sul-Mi punishment group. The spouse palace is stirred: moves, job changes or relationship shifts are traditionally expected, so plan changes deliberately rather than reactively.

Jeong-Chuk days in 2027: Feb 27, Apr 28, Jun 27, Aug 26, Oct 25, Dec 24. Some people like to schedule small personal milestones on their own pillar’s days, purely for fun.

Traditional labels and concept notes

  • Baekho (白虎, White Tiger) — traditionally linked with intensity and sudden events; modern readers treat it as a reminder to stay calm and safety-minded, not as a prediction.
  • Storage (“flower-canopy”) branch — Jin, Sul, Chuk and Mi close each season and store its energy. Tradition links them with Hwagae (華蓋): reflection, study, art and spiritual interests.
  • Nayin: Valley Stream Water (澗下水), the “sound element” shared with Byeong-Ja (丙子).
  • Empty branches (gongmang, 空亡): Sin and Yu (申酉), because Jeong-Chuk belongs to the decade that starts with 甲戌.
  • Same pillar as the years 1937, 1997, 2057 (for the year pillar, not the day).

Labels such as Goegang or Baekho come from classical star lists that vary between schools. They are historical vocabulary, not predictions.

Frequently asked questions about the Jeong-Chuk day pillar

What does the Jeong-Chuk (丁丑) day pillar mean?

Jeong-Chuk is Yin Fire (丁) over the Ox branch 丑 (Earth). In Korean Saju tradition it is read as determined, frugal and skilled, with the day master at the Tomb (묘) stage of the 12 life stages. It is a cultural tradition enjoyed for entertainment, not a scientific prediction.

Which day pillars are compatible with Jeong-Chuk?

Traditionally the best match is Im-Ja (壬子), because the stems combine (丁–壬) and the branches form a six-harmony (丑–子). Day branches Sa and Yu are also friendly to Chuk. The most tense pairing is Gye-Mi (癸未), where the stems clash and the branches clash.

What are the hidden stems of the Jeong-Chuk day pillar?

The Chuk branch holds 癸 (Gye, 9 days), 辛 (Sin, 3 days), 己 (Gi, 18 days). Relative to the Jeong day master they are Seven Killings, Indirect Wealth, Eating God.

What does 2027 bring for Jeong-Chuk day pillar people?

In the 2027 Jeongmi (丁未) year the stem Jeong is Friend for a Jeong day master and the Mi branch’s main qi is Eating God. Chuk and Mi clash directly (丑未沖), and both belong to the Chuk-Sul-Mi punishment group. The spouse palace is stirred: moves, job changes or relationship shifts are traditionally expected, so plan changes deliberately rather than reactively. Read it as light entertainment, not advice.
