What Is Saju? The Korean Four Pillars Explained

Saju is the Korean version of the Four Pillars of Destiny: your birth year, month, day and hour written as eight characters, then read through the five elements. This guide explains each part in plain English, so the day pillar articles make sense.

Saju in one paragraph

Saju (사주, 四柱) means “four pillars”. It is the Korean name for the East Asian practice, also called BaZi in Chinese, of writing a birth moment as four pairs of characters: one pair each for the year, month, day and hour. Each pair is a pillar made of a heavenly stem on top and an earthly branch below, so a full chart has eight characters, which is why Koreans also call it palja (팔자, “eight characters”). Readers look at how the five elements in those characters balance, support and clash, and then tell a story about temperament, relationships and timing. In Korea, Saju is part of everyday culture: people check it at the New Year, before weddings, or just for fun.

The four pillars and what each stands for

PillarChangesTraditionally linked with
Year (연주)at Ipchun, around 4 Februaryancestry, social background, early childhood
Month (월주)at each of 12 solar termsparents, upbringing, career environment; the month branch sets the season, the single biggest factor for element strength
Day (일주)every day, continuous 60-day cycleyou (the day stem or day master) and your spouse palace (the day branch)
Hour (시주)every two hourschildren, later life, private ambitions

Because the year and month follow the 24 solar terms rather than the calendar month, someone born on 2 February belongs to the previous Saju year. The day pillar is different: it simply counts days in an unbroken 60-day cycle that has run for centuries, which is why a calculator can find it from any date.

Stems, branches and the 60 cycle

There are ten heavenly stems (Gap, Eul, Byeong, Jeong, Mu, Gi, Gyeong, Sin, Im, Gye) and twelve earthly branches (Ja, Chuk, In, Myo, Jin, Sa, O, Mi, Sin, Yu, Sul, Hae), the branches being the familiar zodiac animals. They advance together, one step at a time: Gap-Ja, Eul-Chuk, Byeong-In… Because 10 and 12 share a factor of 2, a yang stem always meets a yang branch and a yin stem a yin branch, so only 60 of the 120 possible pairs exist. That is the 60 Gapja, the same cycle that names years such as 2027, the Jeongmi (丁未) year of the Red Sheep.

Hidden stems

Each branch also contains one to three hidden stems (지장간). The Rat branch Ja, for example, holds Im and Gye Water; the Tiger branch In holds Mu Earth, Byeong Fire and Gap Wood. Hidden stems are why a chart with no visible Fire can still have some Fire inside it.

The five elements and the ten gods

Every stem and branch belongs to one of five elements: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water. They relate through two cycles. In the generating cycle Wood feeds Fire, Fire makes Earth (ash), Earth bears Metal, Metal carries Water (condensation) and Water nourishes Wood. In the controlling cycle Wood breaks Earth, Earth dams Water, Water quenches Fire, Fire melts Metal and Metal cuts Wood.

Compared with your day master, every other character takes one of ten roles called the ten gods (십성): Friend and Rob Wealth (same element), Eating God and Hurting Officer (the element you produce), Indirect and Direct Wealth (what you control), Seven Killings and Direct Officer (what controls you), and Indirect and Direct Resource (what produces you). A reader who says “you have strong Officer” is describing this relationship, not a job title.

The 12 life stages

A further layer, the 12 life stages (12운성), tracks how strong a stem feels in each branch, from Birth through Peak to Tomb and Conception, like the life of a plant through the year. On the day pillar pages you will find the stage of each day master on its own branch.

How a reading is built

  1. Convert the birth time. Korean practice uses Korea Standard Time history (including past daylight saving) and, often, a correction to local solar time.
  2. Write the eight characters using solar terms for the year and month, the 60-day count for the day and two-hour blocks for the hour.
  3. Weigh the elements, giving the month branch extra weight, to judge whether the day master is strong or weak.
  4. Name the ten gods and look for combinations and clashes between branches.
  5. Add timing: ten-year luck cycles (대운) and the yearly pillar (세운), such as 2027 Jeongmi.
Entertainment, not prediction. Saju is a long-standing cultural tradition. It has not been shown to predict personality or events in controlled studies, so enjoy it for reflection and conversation and make real decisions on facts and professional advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Saju and BaZi?

They are the same Four Pillars system. BaZi is the Chinese name (eight characters); Saju (four pillars) and palja (eight characters) are the Korean names. Korean practice adds its own conventions, such as Korean Standard Time history and local solar-time correction.

Do I need my birth time for Saju?

The year, month and day pillars only need the date. The hour pillar needs the birth time to within two hours. Without it, readers work with six of the eight characters.

What is the day master?

The day master is the heavenly stem of your day pillar. Saju treats it as you, and every other character is interpreted by its relationship to it, the ten gods.

Is Saju the same as the Korean zodiac animal?

Your zodiac animal is just the branch of your year pillar, one of eight characters. Saju looks at all four pillars, so two people with the same animal can have very different charts.
