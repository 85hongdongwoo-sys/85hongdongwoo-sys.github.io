Gi-Myo (己卯) Day Pillar: Personality, Love, Career & 2027 Outlook

Gi-Myo (己卯), read in Chinese as Ji Mao, is the 16th day pillar: Yin Earth (己) sitting on the Rabbit branch (卯, Wood). Tradition reads it as sensitive, sharp and sociable. Below is what the pillar means in Korean Saju, built from its elements, hidden stems and life stage.

Quick answer

  • Gi-Myo = Yin Earth + Wood Rabbit (己卯), Mandarin Ji Mao
  • Keywords: sensitive, sharp, sociable
  • Hidden stems: 甲 (Direct Officer), 乙 (Seven Killings) · life stage Illness
  • Best match Gap-Sul · 2027: Indirect Resource year
  • A cultural tradition for entertainment, not a prediction

Gi-Myo day pillar personality

  • sensitive
  • sharp
  • sociable

Gi-Myo is soft farmland covered with new shoots. Wood controls Earth, so the branch is the Seven Killings star and Gi sits in Illness. Tradition reads a sensitive, observant person, quick to notice what others need and good with people, but prone to nervous stress. Gi-Myo people do best when they set boundaries on how much they absorb.

The Gi day master

Gi (己) is the garden soil: practical, nurturing and detail-minded. It grows things step by step and quietly manages what others overlook.

Sitting on Myo (卯)

Myo (卯) is pure spring wood, grass and blossoms. As a day branch it adds charm, sensitivity and a gentle but persistent will. Its main hidden stem, 乙, is Seven Killings to a Gi day master (pressure, courage, authority), so the inner self leans toward pressure.

Like every one of the 60 pillars, Gi-Myo pairs a stem and a branch of the same polarity, so it is a purely yin pillar: receptive, inward and subtle in expression.

Hidden stems and the 12 life stages

Every earthly branch carries one to three hidden stems (지장간, jijanggan). Their share follows the traditional day-count table (월률분야) used by the site’s Saju calculator. Each hidden stem becomes a ten god when compared with the day master 己.

Life stage: Illness (병)

Illness (Byeong) is the stage of slowing down: gentle, sympathetic and imaginative. It suggests compassion, artistic or caring talents and a need to manage energy wisely. For Gi, that stage falls exactly on Myo, so the pillar’s own branch shows sensitivity.

Five-element balance

On its own, the Gi-Myo pillar is led by Wood (50%), followed by Earth (50%). Fire, Metal and Water are absent from the pillar itself.

One pillar is only two of the eight characters in a full chart; the year, month and hour pillars often fill the gaps. Tradition treats under-represented elements as balancing themes, for example Fire → warm light, exercise that makes you sweat, social plans; Metal → decluttering, deep breathing, clear decisions; Water → sleep, water, journaling, quiet reading.

Gi-Myo in love and compatibility

The day branch is called the spouse palace. With Seven Killings in the spouse palace, attraction to strong, intense personalities is common and the relationship carries some tension. Respecting each other’s space keeps the spark without the strain.

As a Gi day master, Gi-Myo is caring and attentive, builds a cosy everyday life and worries about the partner’s wellbeing. Cardinal (“peach-blossom”) branch: Ja, O, Myo and Yu are the four cardinal branches. Tradition links them with Dohwa (桃花, peach blossom): charm, visibility and popularity.

Compatible and challenging day pillars

  • Best traditional match: Gap-Sul (甲戌) — stems combine (己 + 甲 → Earth) and branches form a six-harmony (卯 + 戌).
  • Friendly branches: Sul (Dog) (six-harmony) and Hae (亥) and Mi (未) (three-harmony Wood frame).
  • Most tension: Eul-Yu (乙酉) — the branches clash (卯 vs 酉) and the stem 乙 controls 己.

Compatibility in Saju compares whole charts; the day pillar is only one piece. Treat this as a conversation starter, not a verdict on any relationship.

Gi-Myo day pillar for women and men

Personality traits above apply to everyone. Gender enters only through an old convention: classical texts treat Wealth as the spouse star in a man’s chart and Officer in a woman’s chart. Modern readers apply the spouse palace to any partner.

Gi-Myo woman

Seven Killings (乙) sits in the spouse palace as the main qi, which classical texts read, for a woman’s chart, as a close and strong, visible bond: the partner star is “at home”. Being the main star, it tends to show clearly in daily life.

Gi-Myo man

No Wealth star sits inside the Gi-Myo spouse palace, so for a man’s chart classical texts read partner timing from the other pillars and the ten-year luck cycles. The palace’s main star, Seven Killings, sets the tone of the relationship instead.

Gi-Myo career and work style

Career themes in Saju come from the day master and from the star in the spouse palace, which also shows how a person behaves in private and in partnerships.

  • From the Gi stem: accounting and operations, education and childcare, healthcare and care work, food, agriculture and retail.
  • From Seven Killings in the day branch: high-pressure roles: police, military, medicine, crisis management.
  • From the Illness stage: care, healing, arts and imaginative work.

Work that draws on the pillar’s keywords (sensitive, sharp, sociable) tends to feel most natural for Gi-Myo.

Traditional labels and concept notes

  • Cardinal (“peach-blossom”) branch — Ja, O, Myo and Yu are the four cardinal branches. Tradition links them with Dohwa (桃花, peach blossom): charm, visibility and popularity.
  • Nayin: City Wall Earth (城頭土), the “sound element” shared with Mu-In (戊寅).
  • Empty branches (gongmang, 空亡): Sin and Yu (申酉), because Gi-Myo belongs to the decade that starts with 甲戌.
  • Same pillar as the years 1939, 1999, 2059 (for the year pillar, not the day).

Labels such as Goegang or Baekho come from classical star lists that vary between schools. They are historical vocabulary, not predictions.

Frequently asked questions about the Gi-Myo day pillar

What does the Gi-Myo (己卯) day pillar mean?

Gi-Myo is Yin Earth (己) over the Rabbit branch 卯 (Wood). In Korean Saju tradition it is read as sensitive, sharp and sociable, with the day master at the Illness (병) stage of the 12 life stages. It is a cultural tradition enjoyed for entertainment, not a scientific prediction.

Which day pillars are compatible with Gi-Myo?

Traditionally the best match is Gap-Sul (甲戌), because the stems combine (己–甲) and the branches form a six-harmony (卯–戌). Day branches Hae and Mi are also friendly to Myo. The most tense pairing is Eul-Yu (乙酉), where the stems are in a controlling relationship and the branches clash.

What are the hidden stems of the Gi-Myo day pillar?

The Myo branch holds 甲 (Gap, 10 days), 乙 (Eul, 20 days). Relative to the Gi day master they are Direct Officer, Seven Killings.

What does 2027 bring for Gi-Myo day pillar people?

In the 2027 Jeongmi (丁未) year the stem Jeong is Indirect Resource for a Gi day master and the Mi branch’s main qi is Friend. Myo joins Mi in the Hae-Myo-Mi Wood triad (half-combination). Tradition reads cooperation, new allies and smooth teamwork in 2027. Read it as light entertainment, not advice.
