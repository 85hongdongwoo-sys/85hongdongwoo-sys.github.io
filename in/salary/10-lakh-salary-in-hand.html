How ₹10 lakh CTC becomes your salary in hand

CTC (cost to company) is everything your employer spends on you, including its own 12% EPF contribution, which goes to your PF account rather than your bank. Out of the gross salary of ₹9,40,000, you then lose your own EPF share, professional tax and TDS on salary. For FY 2026-27 the Union Budget kept the new-regime slabs unchanged: nil up to ₹4 lakh, 5% to ₹8 lakh, 10% to ₹12 lakh, 15% to ₹16 lakh, 20% to ₹20 lakh, 25% to ₹24 lakh and 30% above, plus 4% health and education cess.

Zero income tax: your taxable income of ₹8,65,000 (gross minus the ₹75,000 standard deduction) is within ₹12 lakh, so the rebate (formerly section 87A) wipes out the full tax under the new regime. Note: the rebate does not cover special-rate income such as capital gains.

Professional tax is a state levy capped at ₹2,500 a year. States such as Delhi, Uttar Pradesh and Haryana do not charge it — add ₹208 a month to the figure above if you work there. It is deductible only under the old regime.

FAQ

What is the in-hand salary for ₹10 lakh CTC in FY 2026-27?

About ₹73,125 a month under the new tax regime, assuming basic pay is 50% of CTC, 12% EPF from both sides and ₹2,500 professional tax. Income tax is ₹0 a year.

Is the new or old regime better for ₹10 lakh salary?

The new regime: it saves ₹81,120 a year compared with the old regime when your only deduction is EPF. The old regime would need about ₹3,27,500 of extra deductions such as HRA, 80D or home-loan interest to break even.

How much income tax is due on ₹10 lakh CTC?

₹0 a year under the new regime (taxable income ₹8,65,000 after the ₹75,000 standard deduction) and ₹81,120 under the old regime, both including 4% cess.
