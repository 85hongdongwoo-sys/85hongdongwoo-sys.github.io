Prepay home loan or invest? A worked example

Prepaying ₹5 lakh after 3 years on a ₹50 lakh, 8%, 20-year home loan saves ₹11.95 lakh of interest and ends the loan 3 yrs 4 mo earlier. Here is how that compares with investing the same money.

The core idea: prepayment is a guaranteed return equal to your loan rate

Every rupee you prepay stops attracting interest at your loan rate for the rest of the tenure. So prepaying an 8% home loan is like earning a risk-free, tax-free 8% on that money. Investing makes sense only if you can reasonably expect to beat that after tax and are comfortable with the risk that you may not.

Worked example: ₹5 lakh on a ₹50 lakh loan

Take a ₹50 lakh loan at 8% for 20 years (EMI ₹41,822). After three years you have a ₹5 lakh bonus.

A debt investment or FD earning 7–8% loses clearly after tax. An equity fund needs to earn roughly 9% or more a year, every year on average, to beat prepayment after the 12.5% long-term capital gains tax (on gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year). Long-run equity returns in India have often been higher than that, but there have also been 5–10-year stretches with low returns — the gap is the price of taking risk.

How your tax regime changes the answer

New regime: no deduction for interest on a self-occupied home, so your effective loan rate is the full 8%. Prepayment looks better.

Old regime: you can deduct up to ₹2 lakh of interest a year. On a ₹50 lakh loan the interest is about ₹3,67,621 in year 4 — above the cap — so prepaying ₹5 lakh does not reduce your deduction for several years, and your effective rate is still 8%. Once interest falls below ₹2 lakh, each rupee of interest saves you 20–30% tax, and the effective rate drops to roughly 5.5–6.5%: then investing becomes relatively more attractive.

Monthly surplus: ₹10,000 extra on the EMI

Adding ₹10,000 a month to the EMI from day one closes the same loan in 13 yrs instead of 20 years and saves ₹19.99 lakh of interest. After the loan closes you can redirect the whole EMI plus ₹10,000 into a SIP for the remaining years — a “prepay first, invest later” approach that many borrowers find psychologically easier.

A balanced approach

  • First, keep 6 months of expenses plus EMIs as an emergency fund — money prepaid into a loan cannot be withdrawn (unless you use an overdraft-linked home loan).
  • Clear expensive debt first: credit cards (~36–42% a year) and personal loans (10–16%) before an 8% home loan.
  • Keep investing for goals with deadlines (children’s education, retirement) and use bonuses or windfalls for prepayment.
  • Prepay early. The first 5–7 years give the most benefit because the outstanding balance is highest.
  • After the October 2026 repo hike, floating rates are rising — that tilts the scales slightly toward prepayment.
  • Prepaying floating-rate home loans is free of charges for individual borrowers. Ask the bank to reduce the tenure, not the EMI.

FAQ: prepay or invest

Is it better to prepay a home loan or invest?

Prepaying earns a guaranteed return equal to your loan rate (about 8% now). Investing can earn more over long periods but with risk. Many borrowers split surplus money between the two after keeping an emergency fund.

How much interest does prepaying ₹5 lakh save on a ₹50 lakh loan?

About ₹11,95,431 if prepaid after three years on a 20-year loan at 8%, and the loan ends about 3 yrs 4 mo earlier.

Should I reduce EMI or tenure after prepayment?

Reducing the tenure saves much more interest. Reduce the EMI only if you need cash-flow relief.

Are there charges for prepaying a home loan?

No — banks and NBFCs cannot levy prepayment charges on floating-rate loans to individuals. Fixed-rate loans may have charges.

Does prepaying make sense in the old tax regime?

Yes while yearly interest exceeds ₹2 lakh, because prepaying does not reduce the deduction you can claim. Once interest falls below ₹2 lakh, the tax benefit lowers your effective loan rate and investing becomes relatively more attractive.
