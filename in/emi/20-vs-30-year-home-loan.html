20 vs 30 year home loan: which to choose?

On ₹50 lakh at 8%, a 30-year loan cuts the EMI from ₹41,822 to ₹36,688 but adds ₹31.7 lakh of interest. Here is how to get the best of both.

The trade-off in numbers

TenureEMI (₹50 lakh at 8%)Total interestTotal payable
15 years₹47,783₹36.01 lakh₹86.01 lakh
20 years₹41,822₹50.37 lakh₹1 crore
25 years₹38,591₹65.77 lakh₹1.16 crore
30 years₹36,688₹82.08 lakh₹1.32 crore

A 30-year tenure lowers the EMI by ₹5,134 a month (12%) but costs ₹31.7 lakh more interest. The relief per month is modest because, on a long loan, most of each EMI is interest anyway: in the first year of the 30-year loan only 9% of the EMIs reduce the principal.

Strategy: take 30 years, pay like 20

Many advisers suggest taking the longer tenure for its lower mandatory EMI, then voluntarily paying more. If you take 30 years but pay ₹41,822 every month (the 20-year EMI), the loan closes in 20 yrs with total interest of ₹50.37 lakh — the same as a 20-year loan, because floating-rate home loans can be prepaid free of charges. The difference: in a bad month (job loss, medical expense) you can fall back to the lower EMI of ₹36,688 without defaulting. Two catches: you need the discipline to keep paying extra, and some banks require part-payments in multiples of EMIs or through a branch request — check the process.

Rate hikes hurt long tenures much more

When rates rise, banks usually keep the EMI and extend the tenure. On a 30-year loan, very little of the EMI goes to principal, so even small hikes add years:

  • 20-year loan, rate +1% (to 9%), EMI unchanged: tenure becomes about 25 yrs 4 mo.
  • 30-year loan, rate +0.5% (to 8.5%), EMI unchanged: tenure becomes about 39 yrs 8 mo.
  • 30-year loan, rate +1%: the EMI of ₹36,688 would no longer cover even the first month’s interest of ₹37,500 — the loan would never be repaid without a higher EMI.

Since the October 2026 repo hike to 5.50%, this is not theoretical. RBI’s 2023 rules require banks to give you the choice of a higher EMI instead, and to avoid tenure extensions that lead to negative amortisation — but on a 30-year loan you are more likely to be forced into a higher EMI after a hike.

Age matters

Lenders want the loan repaid by around 60 (salaried) to 70, depending on the bank. At 30 you can take 30 years; at 40 you may get only 20–25. A longer loan also means paying EMIs closer to retirement, when income may fall — plan to close the loan before you stop working.

How to decide

  • Choose 20 years (or less) if the EMI fits comfortably within 35–40% of take-home and you prefer certainty.
  • Choose 25–30 years if you need the lower EMI to qualify for the loan, or want flexibility — and commit to prepaying from bonuses and raises.
  • Review every year: as your salary grows, increase the EMI voluntarily (a 5% step-up each year cuts a 20-year loan to roughly 12–13 years).

FAQ: home loan tenure

Is a 20 or 30 year home loan better?

A 20-year loan costs much less interest; a 30-year loan has a lower EMI and more flexibility. If you take 30 years, prepaying to finish sooner gets you most of the interest savings.

How much more interest does a 30-year home loan cost?

On ₹50 lakh at 8%, about ₹31.7 lakh more than a 20-year loan (₹82.08 lakh vs ₹50.37 lakh).

What is the EMI difference between 20 and 30 years on ₹50 lakh?

₹41,822 vs ₹36,688 at 8% — ₹5,134 a month less on 30 years.

Can I take a 30-year loan and close it in 20 years?

Yes. Paying the 20-year EMI on a 30-year loan closes it in about 20 yrs, and there are no prepayment charges on floating-rate home loans for individuals.

What happens to a 30-year loan when rates rise?

If the EMI is kept the same, the tenure stretches sharply: on ₹50 lakh at 8%, a 0.5% rise extends a fresh 30-year loan to about 39 yrs 8 mo, and after a 1% rise the old EMI would not even cover the interest. Banks must offer you a higher EMI instead.
