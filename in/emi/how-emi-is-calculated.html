How is EMI calculated? Formula with examples

The EMI formula

An EMI (equated monthly instalment) is a fixed monthly payment that repays both interest and principal so that the loan reaches exactly zero on the last instalment. Indian banks and NBFCs calculate it with the standard annuity formula:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ [(1 + r)n − 1]
  • P = principal, the loan amount (for example ₹30,00,000)
  • r = monthly interest rate = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100 (8% a year → 0.0066667)
  • n = number of monthly instalments (20 years → 240)

Worked example: EMI for ₹30 lakh at 8% for 20 years

Step 1: r = 8 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.0066667. Step 2: (1 + r)240 = 4.9268. Step 3: EMI = 30,00,000 × 0.0066667 × 4.9268 ÷ (4.9268 − 1) = ₹25,093. Over 240 months you pay ₹60,22,368, of which ₹30,22,368 is interest — 101% of the amount borrowed.

How each EMI is split between interest and principal

Interest is charged every month on the outstanding balance. The rest of the EMI repays principal, so the balance falls a little each month and the interest part shrinks while the principal part grows — the EMI itself stays the same.

MonthInterestPrincipalBalance after EMI
Month 1₹20,000₹5,093₹29,94,907
Month 2₹19,966₹5,127₹29,89,780
Month 3₹19,932₹5,161₹29,84,618

In this example the principal part of the EMI overtakes the interest part only in month 137. That is why early prepayments save so much interest, and why a loan that is half-way through its tenure still has well over half of the principal outstanding.

How to calculate EMI in Excel or Google Sheets

Use =PMT(8%/12, 240, -3000000) to get the EMI (₹25,093). =IPMT(8%/12, 1, 240, -3000000) gives the interest in month 1 and =PPMT(8%/12, 1, 240, -3000000) the principal. Rate +1%: 9%₹26,992+8%
Tenure 30 yrs₹22,013-12%
Tenure 10 yrs₹36,398+45%

The EMI is directly proportional to the loan amount, so doubling the loan doubles the EMI. A 1% higher rate raises a 20-year EMI by about 7%. Tenure works with diminishing returns: going from 20 to 30 years cuts the EMI by only about 12%, while going from 20 to 10 years raises it by 45%.

Flat rate vs reducing balance rate

Home loans are always on a reducing balance, but some personal, two-wheeler and consumer loans are quoted at a flat rate: interest = principal × flat rate × years, added to the principal and divided by the number of months. A ₹2 lakh loan at a 7% flat rate for 3 years has an EMI of (2,00,000 + 42,000) ÷ 36 = ₹6,722. The same EMI on a reducing balance corresponds to about 12.83% — almost double the quoted rate. The Key Fact Statement that lenders must give you shows the annual percentage rate (APR), which makes the comparison fair.

Small differences you may notice

  • Pre-EMI interest: on an under-construction flat with part disbursements, you may pay only interest on the amount disbursed until full disbursement; the EMI starts later.
  • Broken-period interest: interest for the days between disbursal and the first EMI date is often collected separately.
  • Rounding: banks round EMIs to the rupee and adjust the last instalment.
  • Rate resets: on floating loans, a rate change recalculates the EMI (or the tenure) from the outstanding balance using the same formula.

FAQ: EMI calculation

What is the formula to calculate EMI?

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n the number of months.

What is the EMI for ₹30 lakh at 8% for 20 years?

About ₹25,093 a month. Total interest over 240 months is about ₹30,22,368.

How do I calculate EMI in Excel?

Use =PMT(annual rate/12, months, -loan amount), for example =PMT(8%/12, 240, -3000000).

Why is most of my EMI interest in the first years?

Interest is charged on the outstanding balance, which is highest at the start. As principal is repaid, the interest part falls and the principal part rises, while the EMI stays the same.

Is a 7% flat rate the same as 7% reducing?

No. Over 3 years a 7% flat rate has the same EMI as a reducing-balance rate of about 12.83%.
