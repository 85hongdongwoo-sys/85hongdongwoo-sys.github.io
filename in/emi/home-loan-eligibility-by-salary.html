How much home loan can I get on my salary?

At 8% and a 50% FOIR, you can borrow roughly 60× your monthly take-home over 20 years. A ₹1 lakh take-home supports about ₹59.78 lakh; ₹50,000 supports about ₹29.89 lakh.

The quick rule: about 60× your monthly take-home

Banks start from your net monthly income (take-home after PF and tax) and allow total EMIs up to a fixed share of it — the fixed obligation to income ratio (FOIR). For most salaried borrowers it is about 50%, rising to 55–65% at higher incomes at some lenders. At 8% for 20 years, each ₹1 lakh of loan costs ₹836 a month, so a 50% FOIR lets you borrow about 60 times your monthly take-home (about 68× on 30 years).

Existing EMIs reduce your eligibility

Suppose you take home ₹1,00,000 and already pay a ₹15,000 car-loan EMI. The bank’s 50% limit is ₹50,000, so only ₹35,000 is left for the home loan — about ₹41.84 lakh over 20 years instead of ₹59.78 lakh. Closing a small personal loan or car loan before applying can raise your eligibility by more than ten times the amount you prepay. Credit-card balances also count: lenders often treat 5% of the outstanding as a monthly obligation.

Other factors banks look at

  • Credit score: 750+ gets the best rates; below 700 you may face a higher rate or lower amount.
  • Age and tenure: the loan must usually end by 60–70, so a 45-year-old may get only 15–20 years, which lowers eligibility.
  • Employer and job stability: government, PSU and large-company employees often get better terms.
  • Co-applicant: adding a spouse’s or parent’s income increases eligibility; both incomes are added before applying the FOIR.
  • Variable pay: banks may count only part of bonuses and incentives, or average them over two years.
  • Down payment: the LTV caps mean you always need 10–25% of the property value, plus stamp duty and registration, from your own funds.

From CTC to eligibility

Offer letters quote CTC, but banks work from take-home. Our CTC to in-hand guides convert a package into monthly take-home — for example, 12 LPA is about ₹85,388 a month, which supports a 20-year loan of about ₹51.04 lakh at 8%; 25 LPA (about ₹1,56,127 a month) supports about ₹93.33 lakh.

Eligible amount vs comfortable amount

The bank’s maximum assumes half your take-home goes to EMIs. Many planners suggest keeping the home-loan EMI to 30–40% of take-home so you can still save, handle rate rises (each 0.25% adds about ₹16 per lakh) and cope with a job change. Borrowing less than the maximum is often the wiser choice.

FAQ: home loan eligibility

How much home loan can I get on a ₹50,000 salary?

At a 50% FOIR and 8%, about ₹29.89 lakh over 20 years or ₹34.07 lakh over 30 years, if you have no other EMIs.

How much home loan can I get on a ₹1 lakh salary?

About ₹59.78 lakh over 20 years or ₹68.14 lakh over 30 years at 8%, with no other loans.

What is FOIR in a home loan?

Fixed obligation to income ratio: the share of your net monthly income that can go to all EMIs combined. Banks typically allow about 50%, more for higher incomes.

Does a co-applicant increase home loan eligibility?

Yes. Banks add the co-applicant’s income before applying the FOIR, which can substantially raise the eligible amount.

How much down payment do I need?

At least 10% for loans up to ₹30 lakh, 20% for loans up to ₹75 lakh and 25% above that (RBI LTV limits), plus stamp duty and registration.
