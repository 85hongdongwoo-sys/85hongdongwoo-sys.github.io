₹70 lakh home loan EMI: the short answer

The EMI for a ₹70 lakh housing loan works out to ₹58,551 per month over 20 years at a typical 8% rate (November 2026). Stretch it to 30 years and the EMI drops to ₹51,364, but total interest climbs to ₹1.15 crore. That is about ₹836 for every ₹1 lakh borrowed on a 20-year tenure at 8%. The calculation assumes a floating rate of 8%, which is what a salaried borrower with a good credit score (750+) is typically offered after the October 2026 repo hike: SBI’s regular home loans are priced at 7.5%–8.8% and HDFC Bank’s start at 8% (repo 5.50% + 2.50%).

Loans of ₹70 lakh are common for 2–3 BHK apartments in Mumbai’s suburbs, Gurugram, Noida, Bengaluru and Hyderabad. With the loan at ₹70 lakh, RBI caps the loan-to-value at 80%. A property of about ₹87.5 lakh therefore needs roughly ₹17.5 lakh of your own money, plus stamp duty, registration and interiors — a budget many buyers underestimate.

Salary required for a 70 lakh home loan

Most banks allow total EMIs of up to about 50% of net monthly income (the fixed obligation to income ratio, or FOIR) for typical salaries, a little more for high earners. To carry the 20-year EMI of ₹58,551 alone you therefore need a take-home salary of at least ₹1,17,100 a month; on a 30-year tenure that falls to ₹1,02,700. Using the typical salary structure from our CTC guides, that take-home corresponds to a package of about 18 LPA (or 16 LPA on a 30-year tenure). Any existing car or personal loan EMI is deducted from that limit first, so ₹10,000 of other EMIs cuts your eligibility by about ₹11.96 lakh of loan at 8%.

Banks cannot charge prepayment or foreclosure fees on floating-rate home loans taken by individuals, so part-payments are free. When you prepay, ask the bank to keep the EMI and cut the tenure — that is what saves the most interest; reducing the EMI instead saves far less. Whether to prepay or invest the surplus depends on your tax regime and risk appetite: see prepay vs invest.

Section 24(b) tax benefit on a 70 lakh home loan: old vs new regime

In the old regime, interest on a loan for a self-occupied home is deductible up to ₹2 lakh a year (Section 24(b) of the 1961 Act; the same limit carries into the Income-tax Act 2025 in force from 1 April 2026), and the principal counts toward the ₹1.5 lakh 80C limit. Year-one interest on this loan is ₹5,54,653, so you can claim the full ₹2 lakh — and interest stays above ₹2 lakh for the first 16 years. The tax saving is up to ₹62,400 a year in the 30% slab or ₹41,600 in the 20% slab (with 4% cess).

In the new regime (the default), there is no deduction for interest on a self-occupied home. For a let-out property, interest is deductible against rent in both regimes, but in the new regime a loss from house property cannot be set off against salary. For someone earning exactly 18 LPA (the minimum package above), the new regime still wins even after claiming ₹2,00,000 of interest, ₹1.5 lakh of 80C and ₹25,000 of 80D: old-regime tax would be ₹1,86,024 against ₹1,20,702. Low slab rates and the ₹12 lakh rebate in the new regime outweigh the deduction at this income.

Put differently: over 20 years the interest equals about 120 of your 240 EMIs — more than seven years of payments go purely to the lender.

Balance transfer: when switching lenders pays

On a ₹70 lakh loan, a 0.50% lower rate saves about ₹2,159 a month over 20 years — ₹5.18 lakh in total. Moving to another lender costs a processing fee (often 0.25%–1% of the loan plus GST), legal and valuation charges, and fresh stamp duty on the mortgage in some states. Before switching, ask your current bank to reprice the loan to its current rate for a conversion fee — it is often cheaper. A transfer makes most sense in the first third of the tenure, when most of the EMI is still interest.

Practical tips before you sign

  • A 0.25% rate difference on ₹70 lakh is worth ₹1,094 a month — negotiate, especially with a 750+ credit score.
  • Prepaying ₹7 lakh after three years shortens the 20-year loan by 3 yrs 4 mo — use bonuses or maturing FDs.
  • Budget for interiors, parking and maintenance deposit separately; they are rarely financed by the home loan.
  • Check the sanction letter for the spread over repo/EBLR and the reset frequency — on repo-linked loans the rate resets at least once every three months.

What is the EMI for a ₹70 lakh home loan for 20 years?

About ₹58,551 a month at 8%, with total interest of ₹70,52,193 and a total repayment of ₹1,40,52,193 over 240 EMIs.

How much down payment is needed for a ₹70 lakh home loan?

RBI caps the loan at 80% of the property value for this loan size, so the property would be about ₹87.5 lakh and the down payment about ₹17.5 lakh, plus stamp duty and registration.

How much tax can I save on a ₹70 lakh home loan?

Only in the old regime: up to ₹2 lakh of interest a year under Section 24(b) (first-year interest here is ₹5,54,653) plus principal within the ₹1.5 lakh 80C limit. The interest deduction saves up to ₹62,400 a year in the 30% slab. The new regime gives no deduction for a self-occupied home.
