Old-regime basic exemption is ₹3 lakh for senior citizens (60–79) and ₹5 lakh for super seniors (80+). FY 2026-27 is the first year under the Income-tax Act 2025, which replaces the 1961 Act; the rates stay the same as last year.

Zero tax up to ₹12.75 lakh salary

Under the new regime, section 87A rebate (up to ₹60,000) makes tax nil when taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less. Salaried people also get a ₹75,000 standard deduction, so a salary of ₹12.75 lakh pays no tax. Just above ₹12 lakh, marginal relief caps your tax at the amount by which income exceeds ₹12 lakh.

When does the old regime still win?

Only if you have large deductions — typically HRA plus full 80C, 80D and home-loan interest. The calculator shows the exact break-even amount of deductions for your salary.

FAQ

Is income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free in FY 2026-27?

Yes, under the new regime. Taxable income up to ₹12 lakh gets a full 87A rebate, and salaried taxpayers also deduct ₹75,000, so a salary up to ₹12.75 lakh pays no tax. Special-rate income like capital gains is taxed separately.

Which tax regime is better for a ₹15 lakh salary?

The new regime is better for most people at ₹15 lakh unless their old-regime deductions exceed roughly ₹5–6 lakh. Enter your figures above for the exact answer.

What is the standard deduction for FY 2026-27?

₹75,000 under the new regime and ₹50,000 under the old regime for salaried employees and pensioners.
