How £28 an hour is taxed in 2026/27

Everyone gets a £12,570 personal allowance. On £54,600 that leaves £42,030 taxable: the first £37,700 at 20% and £4,330 at 40%, giving £9,272 of income tax. Employees then pay Class 1 National Insurance of 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above — £3,103 here. In Scotland the six-band system (19% to 48%) gives £10,914 of income tax, so Scottish take-home is £1,642 lower at £40,583. The marginal rate on your next pound is 42% in England, Wales and NI.

Income tax and NI only start once annual pay passes £12,570 — at £28 that happens at about 8.6 hours a week. Below that you keep every penny. A Plan 2 student loan starts at 20.2 hours a week (£29,385 a year). The 40% higher rate begins at 34.5 hours a week (£50,270) — below a normal full-time week.

Above the median. £28 is 35% more than the £20.81 full-time median hourly pay (ONS ASHE 2026, provisional) and more than double the £10.85 rate for 18 to 20-year-olds. At 37.5 hours you are already over the £50,270 higher-rate threshold, so the top £4,330 of pay is taxed at 40% plus 2% NI.

Tips to keep more of £28 an hour

Make the pension match count. Auto-enrolment means at least 8% of qualifying earnings goes into your pension (at least 3% from the employer). Opting out gives up the employer’s money; at this rate a 5% contribution costs you only £1,638 a year in take-home.
Ask about salary sacrifice. Salary-sacrifice pension, cycle-to-work or EV schemes reduce gross pay, saving 8% NI (2% above £50,270) on top of income tax.
Know your student loan plan. Plan 2 takes 9% above £29,385, Plan 1 above £26,900, Plan 4 (Scotland) above £33,795 and Plan 5 above £25,000. At £28 on 37.5 hours a Plan 2 loan costs £189 a month.
Mind the £50,270 line. Above £50,270, a pension contribution saves 40% tax; above £60,000 the High Income Child Benefit Charge starts to claw back Child Benefit, removing it fully at £80,000.
Use the full calculator for your payslip. Bonuses, Plan 1/4/5 or postgraduate loans, different pension percentages and Scotland are all handled in the take-home pay calculator.

FAQ

£28 an hour is how much a year?

At 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, £28 an hour is £54,600 a year before tax. At 40 hours a week it is £58,240, at 30 hours £43,680 and at 20 hours £29,120.

What is £28 per hour as an annual salary after tax?

In 2026/27 a full-time £28 an hour job (37.5 h/week) leaves £42,225 a year after £9,272 income tax and £3,103 National Insurance in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, or £40,583 in Scotland. That is £21.65 an hour after tax.

How much is £28 an hour per month after tax?

About £3,519 a month on 37.5 hours a week (£3,695 on 40 hours) in England, Wales and NI. With a Plan 2 student loan it is £3,330; in Scotland £3,382.

Is £28 an hour a good wage in the UK?

£28 is 120% above the £12.71 National Living Wage and 35% above the £20.81 median hourly pay for full-time employees (ONS ASHE, April 2026, provisional).

How much is £28 an hour part-time?

At 20 hours a week £28 is £29,120 a year (£2,041 a month after tax); at 30 hours it is £43,680 (£2,914 a month), in England, Wales and NI for 2026/27.

How do I convert hourly pay to an annual salary in the UK?

Multiply the hourly rate by hours per week, then by 52. For a 37.5-hour week that is the rate × 1,950; for 40 hours, × 2,080. Paid holiday is included because the 52 weeks assume holiday is paid at your normal rate.
