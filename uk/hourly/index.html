How the numbers are worked out

All figures use the 2026/27 rates published by HMRC and the Scottish Government: a £12,570 personal allowance; 20% / 40% / 45% income tax in England, Wales and Northern Ireland; 19% to 48% across six Scottish bands; and employee National Insurance of 8% between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above. Minimum wage figures come from GOV.UK and median pay from the ONS Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings 2026 (provisional). These are estimates — your payslip may differ slightly.

FAQ

How do I work out my annual salary from an hourly rate?

Multiply your hourly rate by the hours you work each week and then by 52. For a 37.5-hour week that is the rate × 1,950; for a 40-hour week, × 2,080.

What is the National Living Wage in 2026?

From 1 April 2026 the National Living Wage for workers aged 21 and over is £12.71 an hour, up from £12.21. Full time (37.5 hours) that is £24,785 a year, or about £1,780 a month after tax in England, Wales and NI.

What is the average hourly wage in the UK?

ONS ASHE 2026 (provisional) puts median hourly pay excluding overtime for full-time employees at £20.81 in April 2026. At 37.5 hours that is £40,580 a year, or £32,737 after tax.

Why do these pages use 37.5 hours?

A 37.5-hour week (7.5 hours a day, five days) is the most common full-time contract in UK offices and the public sector. Every page also shows 40-hour, 30-hour and 20-hour figures.
