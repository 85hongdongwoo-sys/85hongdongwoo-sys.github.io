How £21 an hour is taxed in 2026/27

Everyone gets a £12,570 personal allowance. On £40,950 that leaves £28,380 taxable at the 20% basic rate, giving £5,676 of income tax. Employees then pay Class 1 National Insurance of 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above — £2,270 here. In Scotland the six-band system (19% to 48%) gives £5,751 of income tax, so Scottish take-home is £75 lower at £32,929. The marginal rate on your next pound is 28% in England, Wales and NI.

Income tax and NI only start once annual pay passes £12,570 — at £21 that happens at about 11.5 hours a week. Below that you keep every penny. A Plan 2 student loan starts at 26.9 hours a week (£29,385 a year). The 40% higher rate begins at 46.0 hours a week (£50,270).

Close to typical full-time pay. The ONS Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings puts median hourly pay for full-time employees at £20.81 in April 2026 (provisional, up 5.7% from £19.68). £21 is £0.19 above that median, and 65% above the National Living Wage. At this level a Plan 2 student loan starts to bite (9% of pay over £29,385), and in Scotland the 21% intermediate rate applies to income above £29,527.

Student loan Plan 2 and 5% pension examples

At 37.5 hours your £40,950 salary is £11,565 above the Plan 2 threshold, so 9% of that — £1,040 a year or £87 a month — goes to the Student Loans Company. A 5% pension contribution (£2,048) taken under a net pay arrangement reduces take-home by only £1,638, because it comes out before income tax. Auto-enrolment minimums are worked out on qualifying earnings above £6,240, so your scheme may deduct slightly less.

Tips to keep more of £21 an hour

Check your tax code. Most employees should be on 1257L. An emergency code (1257L W1/M1 or 0T/BR) on a second job or new starter form can over-tax you for months — HMRC’s app shows your code and refunds overpayments.
Make the pension match count. Auto-enrolment means at least 8% of qualifying earnings goes into your pension (at least 3% from the employer). Opting out gives up the employer’s money; at this rate a 5% contribution costs you only £1,638 a year in take-home.
Ask about salary sacrifice. Salary-sacrifice pension, cycle-to-work or EV schemes reduce gross pay, saving 8% NI (2% above £50,270) on top of income tax.
Know your student loan plan. Plan 2 takes 9% above £29,385, Plan 1 above £26,900, Plan 4 (Scotland) above £33,795 and Plan 5 above £25,000. At £21 on 37.5 hours a Plan 2 loan costs £87 a month.
Negotiate in hourly terms. A £1 an hour rise is worth £1,950 gross and about £1,404 after tax a year at 37.5 hours — a useful number to bring to a pay review.

FAQ

£21 an hour is how much a year?

At 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, £21 an hour is £40,950 a year before tax. At 40 hours a week it is £43,680, at 30 hours £32,760 and at 20 hours £21,840.

What is £21 per hour as an annual salary after tax?

In 2026/27 a full-time £21 an hour job (37.5 h/week) leaves £33,004 a year after £5,676 income tax and £2,270 National Insurance in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, or £32,929 in Scotland. That is £16.92 an hour after tax.

How much is £21 an hour per month after tax?

About £2,750 a month on 37.5 hours a week (£2,914 on 40 hours) in England, Wales and NI. With a Plan 2 student loan it is £2,664; in Scotland £2,744.

Is £21 an hour a good wage in the UK?

£21 is 65% above the £12.71 National Living Wage and 1% above the £20.81 median hourly pay for full-time employees (ONS ASHE, April 2026, provisional).

How much is £21 an hour part-time?

At 20 hours a week £21 is £21,840 a year (£1,604 a month after tax); at 30 hours it is £32,760 (£2,259 a month), in England, Wales and NI for 2026/27.

How do I convert hourly pay to an annual salary in the UK?

Multiply the hourly rate by hours per week, then by 52. For a 37.5-hour week that is the rate × 1,950; for 40 hours, × 2,080. Paid holiday is included because the 52 weeks assume holiday is paid at your normal rate.
