How £10.85 an hour is taxed in 2026/27

Everyone gets a £12,570 personal allowance. On £21,158 that leaves £8,588 taxable at the 20% basic rate, giving £1,718 of income tax. Employees then pay Class 1 National Insurance of 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above — £687 here. In Scotland the six-band system (19% to 48%) gives £1,678 of income tax, so Scottish take-home is £40 higher at £18,793. The marginal rate on your next pound is 28% in England, Wales and NI.

Income tax and NI only start once annual pay passes £12,570 — at £10.85 that happens at about 22.3 hours a week. Below that you keep every penny. A Plan 2 student loan starts at 52.1 hours a week (£29,385 a year), so full-timers on this rate never repay unless they do overtime. The 40% higher rate (from £50,270) is out of reach at any realistic number of hours.

Is £10.85 an hour legal? £10.85 is exactly the National Minimum Wage for 18 to 20-year-olds from 1 April 2026 (up from £10.00), so it is the lowest lawful rate for that age group. Workers aged 21 or over must be paid at least £12.71 for every hour worked, including time on training and some travel between jobs.

Student loan Plan 2 and 5% pension examples

At 37.5 hours your £21,158 salary is below the £29,385 Plan 2 threshold, so a Plan 2 loan costs nothing. A 5% pension contribution (£1,058) taken under a net pay arrangement reduces take-home by only £846, because it comes out before income tax. Auto-enrolment minimums are worked out on qualifying earnings above £6,240, so your scheme may deduct slightly less.

Tips to keep more of £10.85 an hour

Check your tax code. Most employees should be on 1257L. An emergency code (1257L W1/M1 or 0T/BR) on a second job or new starter form can over-tax you for months — HMRC’s app shows your code and refunds overpayments.
Make the pension match count. Auto-enrolment means at least 8% of qualifying earnings goes into your pension (at least 3% from the employer). Opting out gives up the employer’s money; at this rate a 5% contribution costs you only £846 a year in take-home.
Use the full calculator for your payslip. Bonuses, Plan 1/4/5 or postgraduate loans, different pension percentages and Scotland are all handled in the take-home pay calculator.
Check your age band. Your minimum rate rises on your 18th and 21st birthdays — from the next pay period. Payslips must show hours if pay varies with time worked, so you can check the rate yourself.
Overtime vs extra hours. There is no legal overtime premium in the UK, so check your contract. Each extra hour at £10.85 adds about £7.81 after tax and NI.

FAQ

£10.85 an hour is how much a year?

At 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, £10.85 an hour is £21,158 a year before tax. At 40 hours a week it is £22,568, at 30 hours £16,926 and at 20 hours £11,284.

What is £10.85 per hour as an annual salary after tax?

In 2026/27 a full-time £10.85 an hour job (37.5 h/week) leaves £18,753 a year after £1,718 income tax and £687 National Insurance in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, or £18,793 in Scotland. That is £9.62 an hour after tax.

How much is £10.85 an hour per month after tax?

About £1,563 a month on 37.5 hours a week (£1,647 on 40 hours) in England, Wales and NI. With a Plan 2 student loan it is £1,563; in Scotland £1,566.

Is £10.85 an hour above minimum wage?

£10.85 is below the £12.71 National Living Wage for workers aged 21+ from April 2026. It meets the £10.85 minimum for 18 to 20-year-olds.

How much is £10.85 an hour part-time?

At 20 hours a week £10.85 is £11,284 a year (£940 a month after tax); at 30 hours it is £16,926 (£1,309 a month), in England, Wales and NI for 2026/27.

How do I convert hourly pay to an annual salary in the UK?

Multiply the hourly rate by hours per week, then by 52. For a 37.5-hour week that is the rate × 1,950; for 40 hours, × 2,080. Paid holiday is included because the 52 weeks assume holiday is paid at your normal rate.
