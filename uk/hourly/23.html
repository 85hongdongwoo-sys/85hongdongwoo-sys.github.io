How £23 an hour is taxed in 2026/27

Everyone gets a £12,570 personal allowance. On £44,850 that leaves £32,280 taxable at the 20% basic rate, giving £6,456 of income tax. Employees then pay Class 1 National Insurance of 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above — £2,582 here. In Scotland the six-band system (19% to 48%) gives £6,819 of income tax, so Scottish take-home is £363 lower at £35,449. The marginal rate on your next pound is 28% in England, Wales and NI.

Income tax and NI only start once annual pay passes £12,570 — at £23 that happens at about 10.5 hours a week. Below that you keep every penny. A Plan 2 student loan starts at 24.6 hours a week (£29,385 a year). The 40% higher rate begins at 42.0 hours a week (£50,270).

Above the median. £23 is 11% more than the £20.81 full-time median hourly pay (ONS ASHE 2026, provisional) and more than double the £10.85 rate for 18 to 20-year-olds. Even at 40 hours a week (£47,840) you stay below the £50,270 higher-rate threshold.

Student loan Plan 2 and 5% pension examples

At 37.5 hours your £44,850 salary is £15,465 above the Plan 2 threshold, so 9% of that — £1,391 a year or £116 a month — goes to the Student Loans Company. A 5% pension contribution (£2,243) taken under a net pay arrangement reduces take-home by only £1,794, because it comes out before income tax. Auto-enrolment minimums are worked out on qualifying earnings above £6,240, so your scheme may deduct slightly less.

Is £23 an hour a good wage? NLW and median compared

Tips to keep more of £23 an hour

Make the pension match count. Auto-enrolment means at least 8% of qualifying earnings goes into your pension (at least 3% from the employer). Opting out gives up the employer’s money; at this rate a 5% contribution costs you only £1,794 a year in take-home.
Ask about salary sacrifice. Salary-sacrifice pension, cycle-to-work or EV schemes reduce gross pay, saving 8% NI (2% above £50,270) on top of income tax.
Know your student loan plan. Plan 2 takes 9% above £29,385, Plan 1 above £26,900, Plan 4 (Scotland) above £33,795 and Plan 5 above £25,000. At £23 on 37.5 hours a Plan 2 loan costs £116 a month.
Use the full calculator for your payslip. Bonuses, Plan 1/4/5 or postgraduate loans, different pension percentages and Scotland are all handled in the take-home pay calculator.
Check your tax code. Most employees should be on 1257L. An emergency code (1257L W1/M1 or 0T/BR) on a second job or new starter form can over-tax you for months — HMRC’s app shows your code and refunds overpayments.

FAQ

£23 an hour is how much a year?

At 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, £23 an hour is £44,850 a year before tax. At 40 hours a week it is £47,840, at 30 hours £35,880 and at 20 hours £23,920.

What is £23 per hour as an annual salary after tax?

In 2026/27 a full-time £23 an hour job (37.5 h/week) leaves £35,812 a year after £6,456 income tax and £2,582 National Insurance in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, or £35,449 in Scotland. That is £18.36 an hour after tax.

How much is £23 an hour per month after tax?

About £2,984 a month on 37.5 hours a week (£3,164 on 40 hours) in England, Wales and NI. With a Plan 2 student loan it is £2,868; in Scotland £2,954.

Is £23 an hour a good wage in the UK?

£23 is 81% above the £12.71 National Living Wage and 11% above the £20.81 median hourly pay for full-time employees (ONS ASHE, April 2026, provisional).

How much is £23 an hour part-time?

At 20 hours a week £23 is £23,920 a year (£1,729 a month after tax); at 30 hours it is £35,880 (£2,446 a month), in England, Wales and NI for 2026/27.

How do I convert hourly pay to an annual salary in the UK?

Multiply the hourly rate by hours per week, then by 52. For a 37.5-hour week that is the rate × 1,950; for 40 hours, × 2,080. Paid holiday is included because the 52 weeks assume holiday is paid at your normal rate.
