Hourly to annual salary: £13 an hour by pay period

How £13 an hour is taxed in 2026/27

Everyone gets a £12,570 personal allowance. On £25,350 that leaves £12,780 taxable at the 20% basic rate, giving £2,556 of income tax. Employees then pay Class 1 National Insurance of 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above — £1,022 here. In Scotland the six-band system (19% to 48%) gives £2,516 of income tax, so Scottish take-home is £40 higher at £21,811. The marginal rate on your next pound is 28% in England, Wales and NI.

Income tax and NI only start once annual pay passes £12,570 — at £13 that happens at about 18.6 hours a week. Below that you keep every penny. A Plan 2 student loan starts at 43.5 hours a week (£29,385 a year), so full-timers on this rate never repay unless they do overtime. The 40% higher rate (from £50,270) is out of reach at any realistic number of hours.

Above the minimum, below the middle. £13 is 2% more than the £12.71 National Living Wage, worth £565 a year extra before tax at 37.5 hours, but still £7.81 an hour below the ONS median for full-time employees (£20.81 in April 2026). Every pound you earn above £12,570 is taxed at the 20% basic rate plus 8% NI, so you keep 72p of each extra pound in England, Wales and NI — and you are below the Plan 2 student loan threshold of £29,385 at full time.

£13 an hour part-time: 20, 30 and 40 hours

Fewer hours mean a smaller salary, but not proportionally less take-home — the £12,570 personal allowance and NI primary threshold cover a larger slice of a part-time wage. At 20 hours £13 keeps 98% of gross pay, compared with 86% at 37.5 hours.

Student loan Plan 2 and 5% pension examples

At 37.5 hours your £25,350 salary is below the £29,385 Plan 2 threshold, so a Plan 2 loan costs nothing. A 5% pension contribution (£1,268) taken under a net pay arrangement reduces take-home by only £1,014, because it comes out before income tax. Auto-enrolment minimums are worked out on qualifying earnings above £6,240, so your scheme may deduct slightly less.

Is £13 an hour a good wage? NLW and median compared

Compared with the National Living Wage (£12.71), £13 an hour adds £565 a year before tax and £407 after tax and NI on a 37.5-hour week. Reaching the ONS full-time median of £20.81 would add £15,229 gross, about £10,965 after tax. For context, median weekly pay for full-time employees was £811.20 in April 2026. A £1 an hour pay rise from here is worth £1,404 a year after tax.

Tips to keep more of £13 an hour

Ask about salary sacrifice. Salary-sacrifice pension, cycle-to-work or EV schemes reduce gross pay, saving 8% NI (2% above £50,270) on top of income tax.
Negotiate in hourly terms. A £1 an hour rise is worth £1,950 gross and about £1,404 after tax a year at 37.5 hours — a useful number to bring to a pay review.
Use the full calculator for your payslip. Bonuses, Plan 1/4/5 or postgraduate loans, different pension percentages and Scotland are all handled in the take-home pay calculator.
Overtime vs extra hours. There is no legal overtime premium in the UK, so check your contract. Each extra hour at £13 adds about £9.36 after tax and NI.
Check your tax code. Most employees should be on 1257L. An emergency code (1257L W1/M1 or 0T/BR) on a second job or new starter form can over-tax you for months — HMRC’s app shows your code and refunds overpayments.

FAQ

£13 an hour is how much a year?

At 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, £13 an hour is £25,350 a year before tax. At 40 hours a week it is £27,040, at 30 hours £20,280 and at 20 hours £13,520.

What is £13 per hour as an annual salary after tax?

In 2026/27 a full-time £13 an hour job (37.5 h/week) leaves £21,772 a year after £2,556 income tax and £1,022 National Insurance in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, or £21,811 in Scotland. That is £11.16 an hour after tax.

How much is £13 an hour per month after tax?

About £1,814 a month on 37.5 hours a week (£1,916 on 40 hours) in England, Wales and NI. With a Plan 2 student loan it is £1,814; in Scotland £1,818.

Is £13 an hour a good wage in the UK?

£13 is 2% above the £12.71 National Living Wage and 38% below the £20.81 median hourly pay for full-time employees (ONS ASHE, April 2026, provisional).

How much is £13 an hour part-time?

At 20 hours a week £13 is £13,520 a year (£1,105 a month after tax); at 30 hours it is £20,280 (£1,510 a month), in England, Wales and NI for 2026/27.

How do I convert hourly pay to an annual salary in the UK?

Multiply the hourly rate by hours per week, then by 52. For a 37.5-hour week that is the rate × 1,950; for 40 hours, × 2,080. Paid holiday is included because the 52 weeks assume holiday is paid at your normal rate.
