How £19 an hour is taxed in 2026/27

Everyone gets a £12,570 personal allowance. On £37,050 that leaves £24,480 taxable at the 20% basic rate, giving £4,896 of income tax. Employees then pay Class 1 National Insurance of 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above — £1,958 here. In Scotland the six-band system (19% to 48%) gives £4,932 of income tax, so Scottish take-home is £36 lower at £30,160. The marginal rate on your next pound is 28% in England, Wales and NI.

Income tax and NI only start once annual pay passes £12,570 — at £19 that happens at about 12.7 hours a week. Below that you keep every penny. A Plan 2 student loan starts at 29.7 hours a week (£29,385 a year). The 40% higher rate begins at 50.9 hours a week (£50,270).

Close to typical full-time pay. The ONS Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings puts median hourly pay for full-time employees at £20.81 in April 2026 (provisional, up 5.7% from £19.68). £19 is £1.81 below that median, and 49% above the National Living Wage. At this level a Plan 2 student loan starts to bite (9% of pay over £29,385), and in Scotland the 21% intermediate rate applies to income above £29,527.

£19 an hour part-time: 20, 30 and 40 hours

Fewer hours mean a smaller salary, but not proportionally less take-home — the £12,570 personal allowance and NI primary threshold cover a larger slice of a part-time wage. At 20 hours £19 keeps 90% of gross pay, compared with 81% at 37.5 hours.

Student loan Plan 2 and 5% pension examples

At 37.5 hours your £37,050 salary is £7,665 above the Plan 2 threshold, so 9% of that — £689 a year or £57 a month — goes to the Student Loans Company. A 5% pension contribution (£1,853) taken under a net pay arrangement reduces take-home by only £1,482, because it comes out before income tax. Auto-enrolment minimums are worked out on qualifying earnings above £6,240, so your scheme may deduct slightly less.

Is £19 an hour a good wage? NLW and median compared

Compared with the National Living Wage (£12.71), £19 an hour adds £12,265 a year before tax and £8,831 after tax and NI on a 37.5-hour week. Reaching the ONS full-time median of £20.81 would add £3,529 gross, about £2,541 after tax. For context, median weekly pay for full-time employees was £811.20 in April 2026. A £1 an hour pay rise from here is worth £1,404 a year after tax.

Tips to keep more of £19 an hour

Know your student loan plan. Plan 2 takes 9% above £29,385, Plan 1 above £26,900, Plan 4 (Scotland) above £33,795 and Plan 5 above £25,000. At £19 on 37.5 hours a Plan 2 loan costs £57 a month.
Negotiate in hourly terms. A £1 an hour rise is worth £1,950 gross and about £1,404 after tax a year at 37.5 hours — a useful number to bring to a pay review.
Use the full calculator for your payslip. Bonuses, Plan 1/4/5 or postgraduate loans, different pension percentages and Scotland are all handled in the take-home pay calculator.
Check your tax code. Most employees should be on 1257L. An emergency code (1257L W1/M1 or 0T/BR) on a second job or new starter form can over-tax you for months — HMRC’s app shows your code and refunds overpayments.
Make the pension match count. Auto-enrolment means at least 8% of qualifying earnings goes into your pension (at least 3% from the employer). Opting out gives up the employer’s money; at this rate a 5% contribution costs you only £1,482 a year in take-home.

FAQ

£19 an hour is how much a year?

At 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, £19 an hour is £37,050 a year before tax. At 40 hours a week it is £39,520, at 30 hours £29,640 and at 20 hours £19,760.

What is £19 per hour as an annual salary after tax?

In 2026/27 a full-time £19 an hour job (37.5 h/week) leaves £30,196 a year after £4,896 income tax and £1,958 National Insurance in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, or £30,160 in Scotland. That is £15.48 an hour after tax.

How much is £19 an hour per month after tax?

About £2,516 a month on 37.5 hours a week (£2,665 on 40 hours) in England, Wales and NI. With a Plan 2 student loan it is £2,459; in Scotland £2,513.

Is £19 an hour a good wage in the UK?

£19 is 49% above the £12.71 National Living Wage and 9% below the £20.81 median hourly pay for full-time employees (ONS ASHE, April 2026, provisional).

How much is £19 an hour part-time?

At 20 hours a week £19 is £19,760 a year (£1,479 a month after tax); at 30 hours it is £29,640 (£2,072 a month), in England, Wales and NI for 2026/27.

How do I convert hourly pay to an annual salary in the UK?

Multiply the hourly rate by hours per week, then by 52. For a 37.5-hour week that is the rate × 1,950; for 40 hours, × 2,080. Paid holiday is included because the 52 weeks assume holiday is paid at your normal rate.
