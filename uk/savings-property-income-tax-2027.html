What changed in the 28 October 2026 Budget?

The previous Budget had already legislated a 2-point rise on savings and property income from April 2027 (to 22%, 42% and 47%). The Autumn Budget 2026 added another 2 points, so from 6 April 2027 the rates become:

What stays the same

The personal allowance (£12,570) and higher-rate threshold (£50,270) stay frozen until 2031. Dividend rates stay at 10.75%, 35.75% and 39.35%. The cash ISA allowance stays at £20,000 — the planned cut to £12,000 was scrapped.

How the calculator works

Your personal allowance is used against salary first, then rental profit, then savings. Savings interest gets the £5,000 starting rate (if your other taxable income is low) and the Personal Savings Allowance of £1,000 (basic rate), £500 (higher rate) or £0 (additional rate).

FAQ

When do the new savings and property tax rates start?

From 6 April 2027, the start of the 2027/28 tax year.

How much more tax will a landlord pay?

A basic-rate landlord pays 4p more per £1 of rental profit (24% instead of 20%). On £10,000 of profit that is £400 a year.

Is the Personal Savings Allowance changing?

No change was announced: it stays £1,000 for basic-rate and £500 for higher-rate taxpayers.

Does this affect ISAs?

No. Interest and income inside ISAs remain tax-free, and the £20,000 annual limit stays.
