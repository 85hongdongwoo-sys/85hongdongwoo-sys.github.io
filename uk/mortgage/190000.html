What a £190k mortgage costs at today’s rates

Average fixed rates jumped in autumn 2026: Moneyfacts recorded the average 5-year fix reaching 6% on 5 October for the first time in about three years, and it stood at 5.99% on 28 October, with the 2-year fix at 5.97%. Bank Rate has been 3.75% since the Monetary Policy Committee held it on 17 September 2026 (three members voted for a rise), and the next decision is on 5 November. That is why a tracker priced off Bank Rate currently looks cheaper than a fix — £1,083 a month at an assumed 4.75% — but its payment moves every time Bank Rate does. Every 1 percentage point on the rate changes the 25-year repayment on £190,000 by about £119 a month.

A typical first-time-buyer loan. The average first-time buyer paid £230,097 in August 2026 (UK HPI), so a £190k mortgage with a £21,111 deposit puts you a little below that benchmark. Prices under £300,000 also mean first-time buyers in England and Northern Ireland pay no Stamp Duty at all.

The term matters almost as much as the rate. At 5.99%, a 35-year term saves £141 a month compared with 25 years, yet the interest bill rises from £176,903 to £264,476. A 20-year term costs £137 more a month than 25 years but saves £40,474 of interest.

How the £190k balance falls: amortisation

In year one, £11,289 of your £14,676 of payments is interest and only £3,387 reduces the debt. Capital repaid overtakes interest in year 14, and half the loan is cleared by year 17 of 25. After a typical 5-year fix you would still owe about £170,846, which is the figure you would remortgage. That is why overpaying early has the biggest effect. Paying an extra £200 a month would save about £53,087 of interest and finish 6 years 7 months sooner.

How much salary do you need for a £190k mortgage?

Lenders start with an income multiple and then check your spending. At the common 4.5× income limit, a £190,000 mortgage needs £42,222 of gross household income — one earner on £42,222 or two on about £21,111 each. A smaller group of lenders will go to 5.5× for higher earners, certain professions or first-time buyers with strong credit, cutting the requirement to £34,545. The Bank of England limits each lender to 15% of new loans at 4.5× or more, so the higher multiples are not available to everyone.

Stress test: could you afford a higher rate?

Under FCA rules lenders must check that a mortgage stays affordable if rates rise, especially for deals fixed for less than five years. Many lenders test against a rate a few points above the product rate or their reversion rate. At an illustrative 8.99% (today’s average 5-year fix + 3 points) the 25-year payment on £190,000 would be £1,593 — £370 a month more. If that would break your budget, consider a longer fix, a smaller loan or a bigger deposit. When a fixed deal ends the loan moves to the lender’s standard variable rate, which averaged 7.13% — £1,359 a month on this loan.

Tips for a £190k mortgage

Think about the 35-year cost. A 35-year term makes the payment easier today, but on £190k it adds £87,572 of interest compared with 25 years at the same rate. You can shorten the term later when you remortgage.
Lifetime ISA and schemes. First-time buyers can get a 25% government bonus on Lifetime ISA savings for homes up to £450,000. The new “Your First Home” equity-loan scheme for new-builds in England was confirmed in the 28 October 2026 Budget, with first loans expected from April 2027.
Compare the total cost, not just the rate. A low rate with a £999–£1,999 fee can cost more than a fee-free deal. On £190k, 0.2 percentage points is worth about £23 a month over 25 years — compare that with the fee.
Overpay if your deal allows it. Most fixed rates let you overpay 10% of the balance a year without penalty. £200 a month extra on this loan saves £53,087 of interest and clears it 6 years 7 months early (25-year term at 5.99%).
Bigger deposit, better rate. Rates usually step down at 90%, 85%, 75% and 60% loan-to-value. Moving from 90% to 85% LTV can be worth more than the extra 5% deposit earns in savings.

FAQ

How much is a £190,000 mortgage per month?

At the 5.99% average 5-year fixed rate (Moneyfacts, 28 October 2026) a £190,000 repayment mortgage costs about £1,223 a month over 25 years, £1,138 over 30 years and £1,082 over 35 years. At the 5.97% average 2-year fix it is £1,221 over 25 years.

How much salary do I need for a £190k mortgage?

Most UK lenders lend up to about 4.5 times income, so a £190,000 mortgage needs a salary of around £42,222 for a single applicant or a combined £42,222 for a couple (for example two incomes of £21,111). Lenders offering 5.5 times income would need about £34,545. Affordability checks on your outgoings decide the final amount.

What deposit do I need for a £190,000 mortgage?

With a 10% deposit a £190,000 loan buys a £211,111 home, so the deposit is £21,111. At 5% the deposit is £10,000 (price £200,000); at 25% it is £63,333 (price £253,333).

What are the interest-only payments on £190,000?

Interest-only at 5.99% costs £948 a month, against £1,223 on a 25-year repayment mortgage. You still owe the full £190,000 at the end of the term, so lenders require a credible repayment plan and usually a larger deposit.

Is a 25-year or 35-year mortgage better for £190,000?

A 35-year term cuts the payment by £141 a month at 5.99%, but adds £87,572 of total interest compared with 25 years if the rate stayed the same.

How much Stamp Duty is due on a home bought with a £190k mortgage?

With a 10% deposit (price £211,111) a first-time buyer in England or Northern Ireland pays £0 and a home mover pays £1,722 under the rates in force since 1 April 2025. A second home or buy-to-let adds a 5% surcharge.
