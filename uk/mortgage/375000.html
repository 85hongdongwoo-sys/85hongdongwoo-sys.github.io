What a £375k mortgage costs at today’s rates

Average fixed rates jumped in autumn 2026: Moneyfacts recorded the average 5-year fix reaching 6% on 5 October for the first time in about three years, and it stood at 5.99% on 28 October, with the 2-year fix at 5.97%. Bank Rate has been 3.75% since the Monetary Policy Committee held it on 17 September 2026 (three members voted for a rise), and the next decision is on 5 November. That is why a tracker priced off Bank Rate currently looks cheaper than a fix — £2,138 a month at an assumed 4.75% — but its payment moves every time Bank Rate does. Every 1 percentage point on the rate changes the 25-year repayment on £375,000 by about £234 a month.

South East and London territory. A £375k loan typically means a home of £441,176–£500,000, above the £294,000 English average but still below London’s £554,430. Income needed at 4.5× is £83,333, so many buyers here rely on joint incomes or the 5–5.5× “professional” multiples that some lenders offer to higher earners.

25 vs 35 years: total interest on £375,000

Choosing the term is a trade-off between monthly comfort and lifetime cost. At 5.99%, a 35-year term saves £278 a month compared with 25 years, yet the interest bill rises from £349,152 to £521,991. A 20-year term costs £271 more a month than 25 years but saves £79,883 of interest.

How the £375k balance falls: amortisation

In year one, £22,281 of your £28,966 of payments is interest and only £6,685 reduces the debt. Capital repaid overtakes interest in year 14, and half the loan is cleared by year 17 of 25. After a typical 5-year fix you would still owe about £337,197, which is the figure you would remortgage. This front-loading is why early overpayments save so much. Paying an extra £200 a month would save about £63,099 of interest and finish 3 years 11 months sooner.

How much salary do you need for a £375k mortgage?

UK affordability rules boil down to two tests: an income multiple and a stress-tested budget. At the common 4.5× income limit, a £375,000 mortgage needs £83,333 of gross household income — one earner on £83,333 or two on about £41,667 each. A smaller group of lenders will go to 5.5× for higher earners, certain professions or first-time buyers with strong credit, cutting the requirement to £68,182. The Bank of England limits each lender to 15% of new loans at 4.5× or more, so the higher multiples are not available to everyone.

Stress test: could you afford a higher rate?

Under FCA rules lenders must check that a mortgage stays affordable if rates rise, especially for deals fixed for less than five years. Many lenders test against a rate a few points above the product rate or their reversion rate. At an illustrative 8.99% (today’s average 5-year fix + 3 points) the 25-year payment on £375,000 would be £3,144 — £731 a month more. If that would break your budget, consider a longer fix, a smaller loan or a bigger deposit. When a fixed deal ends the loan moves to the lender’s standard variable rate, which averaged 7.13% — £2,682 a month on this loan.

Tips for a £375k mortgage

Think about the 35-year cost. A 35-year term makes the payment easier today, but on £375k it adds £172,840 of interest compared with 25 years at the same rate. You can shorten the term later when you remortgage.
Lifetime ISA and schemes. First-time buyers can get a 25% government bonus on Lifetime ISA savings for homes up to £450,000. The new “Your First Home” equity-loan scheme for new-builds in England was confirmed in the 28 October 2026 Budget, with first loans expected from April 2027.
Compare the total cost, not just the rate. A low rate with a £999–£1,999 fee can cost more than a fee-free deal. On £375k, 0.2 percentage points is worth about £46 a month over 25 years — compare that with the fee.
Overpay if your deal allows it. Most fixed rates let you overpay 10% of the balance a year without penalty. £200 a month extra on this loan saves £63,099 of interest and clears it 3 years 11 months early (25-year term at 5.99%).
Bigger deposit, better rate. Rates usually step down at 90%, 85%, 75% and 60% loan-to-value. Moving from 90% to 85% LTV can be worth more than the extra 5% deposit earns in savings.

FAQ

How much is a £375,000 mortgage per month?

At the 5.99% average 5-year fixed rate (Moneyfacts, 28 October 2026) a £375,000 repayment mortgage costs about £2,414 a month over 25 years, £2,246 over 30 years and £2,136 over 35 years. At the 5.97% average 2-year fix it is £2,409 over 25 years.

How much salary do I need for a £375k mortgage?

Most UK lenders lend up to about 4.5 times income, so a £375,000 mortgage needs a salary of around £83,333 for a single applicant or a combined £83,333 for a couple (for example two incomes of £41,667). Lenders offering 5.5 times income would need about £68,182. Affordability checks on your outgoings decide the final amount.

What deposit do I need for a £375,000 mortgage?

With a 10% deposit a £375,000 loan buys a £416,667 home, so the deposit is £41,667. At 5% the deposit is £19,737 (price £394,737); at 25% it is £125,000 (price £500,000).

What are the interest-only payments on £375,000?

Interest-only at 5.99% costs £1,872 a month, against £2,414 on a 25-year repayment mortgage. You still owe the full £375,000 at the end of the term, so lenders require a credible repayment plan and usually a larger deposit.

Is a 25-year or 35-year mortgage better for £375,000?

A 35-year term cuts the payment by £278 a month at 5.99%, but adds £172,840 of total interest compared with 25 years if the rate stayed the same.

How much Stamp Duty is due on a home bought with a £375k mortgage?

With a 10% deposit (price £416,667) a first-time buyer in England or Northern Ireland pays £5,833 and a home mover pays £10,833 under the rates in force since 1 April 2025. A second home or buy-to-let adds a 5% surcharge.
