What a £100k mortgage costs at today’s rates

Average fixed rates jumped in autumn 2026: Moneyfacts recorded the average 5-year fix reaching 6% on 5 October for the first time in about three years, and it stood at 5.99% on 28 October, with the 2-year fix at 5.97%. Bank Rate has been 3.75% since the Monetary Policy Committee held it on 17 September 2026 (three members voted for a rise), and the next decision is on 5 November. That is why a tracker priced off Bank Rate currently looks cheaper than a fix — £570 a month at an assumed 4.75% — but its payment moves every time Bank Rate does. Every 1 percentage point on the rate changes the 25-year repayment on £100,000 by about £62 a month.

Who borrows £100k? Loans of this size are common for remortgages after years of repayments, for buyers in lower-priced areas of the North East, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, and for people adding a smaller mortgage to a large deposit. Some lenders set minimum loan sizes (often £25,000 to £50,000) and charge the same arrangement fee whatever the loan, so on a small mortgage a £999 fee can add more to the true cost than a slightly higher rate — compare the total cost over the fixed period, not just the headline rate.

25 vs 35 years: total interest on £100,000

The term matters almost as much as the rate. At 5.99%, a 35-year term saves £74 a month compared with 25 years, yet the interest bill rises from £93,107 to £139,198. A 20-year term costs £72 more a month than 25 years but saves £21,302 of interest.

How the £100k balance falls: amortisation

In year one, £5,942 of your £7,724 of payments is interest and only £1,783 reduces the debt. Capital repaid overtakes interest in year 14, and half the loan is cleared by year 17 of 25. After a typical 5-year fix you would still owe about £89,919, which is the figure you would remortgage. That is why overpaying early has the biggest effect. Paying an extra £100 a month would save about £26,974 of interest and finish 6 years 4 months sooner.

How much salary do you need for a £100k mortgage?

Lenders start with an income multiple and then check your spending. At the common 4.5× income limit, a £100,000 mortgage needs £22,222 of gross household income — one earner on £22,222 or two on about £11,111 each. A smaller group of lenders will go to 5.5× for higher earners, certain professions or first-time buyers with strong credit, cutting the requirement to £18,182. The Bank of England limits each lender to 15% of new loans at 4.5× or more, so the higher multiples are not available to everyone.

Stress test: could you afford a higher rate?

Under FCA rules lenders must check that a mortgage stays affordable if rates rise, especially for deals fixed for less than five years. Many lenders test against a rate a few points above the product rate or their reversion rate. At an illustrative 8.99% (today’s average 5-year fix + 3 points) the 25-year payment on £100,000 would be £839 — £195 a month more. If that would break your budget, consider a longer fix, a smaller loan or a bigger deposit. When a fixed deal ends the loan moves to the lender’s standard variable rate, which averaged 7.13% — £715 a month on this loan.

Tips for a £100k mortgage

Check what counts as income. Lenders treat overtime, bonus and self-employed income differently — often only 50–100% of variable pay counts, and the self-employed usually need two years of accounts.
Think about the 35-year cost. A 35-year term makes the payment easier today, but on £100k it adds £46,091 of interest compared with 25 years at the same rate. You can shorten the term later when you remortgage.
Lifetime ISA and schemes. First-time buyers can get a 25% government bonus on Lifetime ISA savings for homes up to £450,000. The new “Your First Home” equity-loan scheme for new-builds in England was confirmed in the 28 October 2026 Budget, with first loans expected from April 2027.
Compare the total cost, not just the rate. A low rate with a £999–£1,999 fee can cost more than a fee-free deal. On £100k, 0.2 percentage points is worth about £12 a month over 25 years — compare that with the fee.
Overpay if your deal allows it. Most fixed rates let you overpay 10% of the balance a year without penalty. £100 a month extra on this loan saves £26,974 of interest and clears it 6 years 4 months early (25-year term at 5.99%).

FAQ

How much is a £100,000 mortgage per month?

At the 5.99% average 5-year fixed rate (Moneyfacts, 28 October 2026) a £100,000 repayment mortgage costs about £644 a month over 25 years, £599 over 30 years and £570 over 35 years. At the 5.97% average 2-year fix it is £642 over 25 years.

How much salary do I need for a £100k mortgage?

Most UK lenders lend up to about 4.5 times income, so a £100,000 mortgage needs a salary of around £22,222 for a single applicant or a combined £22,222 for a couple (for example two incomes of £11,111). Lenders offering 5.5 times income would need about £18,182. Affordability checks on your outgoings decide the final amount.

What deposit do I need for a £100,000 mortgage?

With a 10% deposit a £100,000 loan buys a £111,111 home, so the deposit is £11,111. At 5% the deposit is £5,263 (price £105,263); at 25% it is £33,333 (price £133,333).

What are the interest-only payments on £100,000?

Interest-only at 5.99% costs £499 a month, against £644 on a 25-year repayment mortgage. You still owe the full £100,000 at the end of the term, so lenders require a credible repayment plan and usually a larger deposit.

Is a 25-year or 35-year mortgage better for £100,000?

A 35-year term cuts the payment by £74 a month at 5.99%, but adds £46,091 of total interest compared with 25 years if the rate stayed the same.

How much Stamp Duty is due on a home bought with a £100k mortgage?

With a 10% deposit (price £111,111) a first-time buyer in England or Northern Ireland pays £0 and a home mover pays £0 under the rates in force since 1 April 2025. A second home or buy-to-let adds a 5% surcharge.
