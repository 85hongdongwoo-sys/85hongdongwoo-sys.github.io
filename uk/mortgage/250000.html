What a £250k mortgage costs at today’s rates

Average fixed rates jumped in autumn 2026: Moneyfacts recorded the average 5-year fix reaching 6% on 5 October for the first time in about three years, and it stood at 5.99% on 28 October, with the 2-year fix at 5.97%. Bank Rate has been 3.75% since the Monetary Policy Committee held it on 17 September 2026 (three members voted for a rise), and the next decision is on 5 November. That is why a tracker priced off Bank Rate currently looks cheaper than a fix — £1,425 a month at an assumed 4.75% — but its payment moves every time Bank Rate does. Every 1 percentage point on the rate changes the 25-year repayment on £250,000 by about £156 a month.

Around the national average. With a 10% deposit, £250k buys about £277,778 — 2% above the £273,037 UK average and below England’s £294,000. First-time buyers still pay no Stamp Duty below £300,000.

How the £250k balance falls: amortisation

In year one, £14,854 of your £19,311 of payments is interest and only £4,457 reduces the debt. Capital repaid overtakes interest in year 14, and half the loan is cleared by year 17 of 25. After a typical 5-year fix you would still owe about £224,798, which is the figure you would remortgage. That is why overpaying early has the biggest effect. Paying an extra £200 a month would save about £57,505 of interest and finish 5 years 4 months sooner.

How much salary do you need for a £250k mortgage?

Lenders start with an income multiple and then check your spending. At the common 4.5× income limit, a £250,000 mortgage needs £55,556 of gross household income — one earner on £55,556 or two on about £27,778 each. A smaller group of lenders will go to 5.5× for higher earners, certain professions or first-time buyers with strong credit, cutting the requirement to £45,455. The Bank of England limits each lender to 15% of new loans at 4.5× or more, so the higher multiples are not available to everyone.

The loan-to-value (LTV) ratio decides both the price you can reach and the rate you are offered. A £250,000 mortgage at 90% LTV means a £277,778 home and a £27,778 deposit. Stress test: could you afford a higher rate?

Under FCA rules lenders must check that a mortgage stays affordable if rates rise, especially for deals fixed for less than five years. Many lenders test against a rate a few points above the product rate or their reversion rate. At an illustrative 8.99% (today’s average 5-year fix + 3 points) the 25-year payment on £250,000 would be £2,096 — £487 a month more. If that would break your budget, consider a longer fix, a smaller loan or a bigger deposit. When a fixed deal ends the loan moves to the lender’s standard variable rate, which averaged 7.13% — £1,788 a month on this loan.

Tips for a £250k mortgage

Overpay if your deal allows it. Most fixed rates let you overpay 10% of the balance a year without penalty. £200 a month extra on this loan saves £57,505 of interest and clears it 5 years 4 months early (25-year term at 5.99%).
Bigger deposit, better rate. Rates usually step down at 90%, 85%, 75% and 60% loan-to-value. Moving from 90% to 85% LTV can be worth more than the extra 5% deposit earns in savings.
Book your next deal early. Most lenders let you lock in a new rate up to six months before your current fix ends. Rolling onto the standard variable rate (about 7.13% on average) can add hundreds a month.
Use a mortgage broker. Brokers can see lenders that do not deal direct, and some deals are broker-only. Check whether they charge a fee and whether they search the whole market.
Check what counts as income. Lenders treat overtime, bonus and self-employed income differently — often only 50–100% of variable pay counts, and the self-employed usually need two years of accounts.

FAQ

How much is a £250,000 mortgage per month?

At the 5.99% average 5-year fixed rate (Moneyfacts, 28 October 2026) a £250,000 repayment mortgage costs about £1,609 a month over 25 years, £1,497 over 30 years and £1,424 over 35 years. At the 5.97% average 2-year fix it is £1,606 over 25 years.

How much salary do I need for a £250k mortgage?

Most UK lenders lend up to about 4.5 times income, so a £250,000 mortgage needs a salary of around £55,556 for a single applicant or a combined £55,556 for a couple (for example two incomes of £27,778). Lenders offering 5.5 times income would need about £45,455. Affordability checks on your outgoings decide the final amount.

What deposit do I need for a £250,000 mortgage?

With a 10% deposit a £250,000 loan buys a £277,778 home, so the deposit is £27,778. At 5% the deposit is £13,158 (price £263,158); at 25% it is £83,333 (price £333,333).

What are the interest-only payments on £250,000?

Interest-only at 5.99% costs £1,248 a month, against £1,609 on a 25-year repayment mortgage. You still owe the full £250,000 at the end of the term, so lenders require a credible repayment plan and usually a larger deposit.

Is a 25-year or 35-year mortgage better for £250,000?

A 35-year term cuts the payment by £185 a month at 5.99%, but adds £115,227 of total interest compared with 25 years if the rate stayed the same.

How much Stamp Duty is due on a home bought with a £250k mortgage?

With a 10% deposit (price £277,778) a first-time buyer in England or Northern Ireland pays £0 and a home mover pays £3,889 under the rates in force since 1 April 2025. A second home or buy-to-let adds a 5% surcharge.
