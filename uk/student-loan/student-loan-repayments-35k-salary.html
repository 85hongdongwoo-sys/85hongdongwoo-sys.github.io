How the £35,000 student loan repayment is worked out

Here is the sum your payroll software does every month. For Plan 2, the 2026/27 threshold is £29,385 a year, or £2,448.75 a month. Monthly pay on £35,000 is £2,916.67, so the amount above the threshold is £467.92. Nine per cent of that is £42.11, and payroll rounds down to the whole pound: £42 a month. Over twelve months that is £504 — a few pounds below the exact annual figure of £505 because of the rounding.

Every plan is repaying. At £35,000 you are above all five thresholds, so each plan takes 9% (6% for the Postgraduate Loan) of the slice above its own threshold. Because the percentage is the same, the difference between plans is fixed by the thresholds alone: Plan 5 always costs £395 a year more than Plan 2 once both are repaying, and Plan 1 costs £224 more than Plan 2. You are still a basic-rate taxpayer, so your combined marginal rate on extra pay with an undergraduate loan is about 37%.

Is your balance going down? Interest vs repayments on £35,000

Repayments and interest are separate: you repay a percentage of income, while interest is charged on the whole balance. On an example balance of £45,000, a Plan 2 graduate on £35,000 is charged about £2,168 of interest this academic year at 4.82%, against repayments of £504 — so the balance still grows by around £1,664. At £35,000 you are on the sloping part of the Plan 2 scale, below the 6% cap that starts at about £44,268.

Will you pay it off before it is written off?

Monthly repayments tell only half the story — the other half is whether the balance ever reaches zero. We modelled a graduate who starts repaying in April 2026 with a £45,000 balance on £35,000, with pay rising 3% a year. On Plan 2 the loan is still £91,449 owed when it is written off after 30 years, having repaid £34,266 in total. On Plan 5, with a lower threshold and a 40-year term, it is still £32,621 owed when it is written off after 40 years, having repaid £67,861 in total. Even the longer Plan 5 term does not clear the balance at this starting salary, so much of the debt behaves more like a graduate tax.

A projection, not a forecast: real pay, inflation and future threshold decisions will differ. Plan 2 thresholds are frozen at £29,385 to April 2030 and then assumed to rise 3% a year.

Tips for graduates on £35k

Moving abroad? Tell the SLC. Repayments stop coming out through PAYE when you leave UK employment. You must tell the Student Loans Company and send an overseas income assessment, or you risk fixed repayments and arrears.
Self-employed income counts too. Profits from self-employment are added to your income in Self Assessment, and 9% of the total above your threshold is due with your tax bill on 31 January.
Check your payslip shows the right plan. Your employer only knows the plan type HMRC or you tell them. If you started an English course after August 2023 but are being charged as Plan 2 (or the other way round), the deductions will be wrong — check the plan in your Student Loans Company online account.
Repayments are based on gross pay. Deductions are calculated on pay before income tax, from each pay period on its own. A one-off bonus month can trigger a deduction even if your annual salary is below the threshold; you can ask the Student Loans Company for a refund after the tax year ends.

FAQ

How much student loan do I pay on £35,000 a year?

In 2026/27 a £35,000 salary means monthly repayments of £60 on Plan 1, £42 on Plan 2, £9 on Plan 4 and £75 on Plan 5, plus £70 on a Postgraduate Loan if you have one. That is 9% (6% for the Postgraduate Loan) of income above each plan’s threshold.

What is my take-home pay on £35,000 with a Plan 2 student loan?

After income tax, National Insurance and Plan 2 repayments you take home about £28,216 a year, or £2,351 a month, in England, Wales or Northern Ireland in 2026/27 (no pension). Without a student loan it would be £28,720 a year.

What interest is charged on a Plan 2 loan at £35,000?

From 1 September 2026 to 31 August 2027 Plan 2 interest after you leave your course is 4.82% at an income of £35,000: RPI of 4.1% up to £29,385 of income, rising towards RPI + 3% at £52,885, but capped at 6% for this academic year.

Will my student loan be written off on £35,000?

In our illustration a £45,000 balance, with pay starting at £35,000 and rising 3% a year, would be written off after 30 years on Plan 2 with about £91,449 left and written off after 40 years on Plan 5 with about £32,621 left. Your outcome depends on your real balance and future pay.

Does the amount I borrowed change my monthly repayment?

No. Repayments depend only on your income and plan threshold. A larger balance only changes how long you keep paying and whether anything is left to be written off.
