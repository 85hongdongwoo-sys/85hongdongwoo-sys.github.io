How the £30,000 student loan repayment is worked out

The calculation is the same for every employer. For Plan 2, the 2026/27 threshold is £29,385 a year, or £2,448.75 a month. Monthly pay on £30,000 is £2,500.00, so the amount above the threshold is £51.25. Nine per cent of that is £4.61, and payroll rounds down to the whole pound: £4 a month. Over twelve months that is £48 — a few pounds below the exact annual figure of £55 because of the rounding.

Plan 4 still pays nothing. Scottish graduates on Plan 4 have the highest threshold (£33,795), so at £30,000 they repay £0, while Plan 1, 2 and 5 borrowers are all making deductions. The gap between plans is widest at salaries like this one: Plan 5 takes £444 a year against £48 on Plan 2 — a difference of £396 purely because of the lower threshold.

Is your balance going down? Interest vs repayments on £30,000

Repayments and interest are separate: you repay a percentage of income, while interest is charged on the whole balance. On an example balance of £45,000, a Plan 2 graduate on £30,000 is charged about £1,880 of interest this academic year at 4.18%, against repayments of £48 — so the balance still grows by around £1,832. At £30,000 you are on the sloping part of the Plan 2 scale, below the 6% cap that starts at about £44,268.

Take-home pay on £30,000 with a student loan

Student loan repayments come out of the same payslip as income tax and National Insurance, so the useful number is what lands in your bank account. With no student loan, a £30,000 salary leaves £25,120 a year after 2026/27 income tax and National Insurance (England, Wales and Northern Ireland, no pension). A Plan 2 loan reduces that to £25,072, and a Plan 5 loan to £24,676. If you also took a Postgraduate Loan, a further £540 a year comes off. Full details are on the £30,000 salary after tax page.

Will you pay it off before it is written off?

Because unpaid balances are cancelled, the real cost of a student loan is what you repay before the write-off date. We modelled a graduate who starts repaying in April 2026 with a £45,000 balance on £30,000, with pay rising 3% a year. On Plan 2 the loan is still £104,159 owed when it is written off after 30 years, having repaid £12,857 in total. On Plan 5, with a lower threshold and a 40-year term, it is still £90,490 owed when it is written off after 40 years, having repaid £33,931 in total. Even the longer Plan 5 term does not clear the balance at this starting salary, so much of the debt behaves more like a graduate tax.

A projection, not a forecast: real pay, inflation and future threshold decisions will differ. Plan 2 thresholds are frozen at £29,385 to April 2030 and then assumed to rise 3% a year.

Tips for graduates on £30k

Keep your contact details up to date. The Student Loans Company uses your online account to send annual statements and to switch you to Direct Debit in the final years so you do not over-repay. Out-of-date details are the most common reason refunds are delayed.
Think before overpaying. Voluntary payments are allowed at any time with no penalty, but if your balance would be written off anyway, every pound you overpay is a pound you would never have had to pay. Read our overpayment guide first.
Moving abroad? Tell the SLC. Repayments stop coming out through PAYE when you leave UK employment. You must tell the Student Loans Company and send an overseas income assessment, or you risk fixed repayments and arrears.
Self-employed income counts too. Profits from self-employment are added to your income in Self Assessment, and 9% of the total above your threshold is due with your tax bill on 31 January.

FAQ

How much student loan do I pay on £30,000 a year?

In 2026/27 a £30,000 salary means monthly repayments of £23 on Plan 1, £4 on Plan 2, £0 on Plan 4 and £37 on Plan 5, plus £45 on a Postgraduate Loan if you have one. That is 9% (6% for the Postgraduate Loan) of income above each plan’s threshold.

What is my take-home pay on £30,000 with a Plan 2 student loan?

After income tax, National Insurance and Plan 2 repayments you take home about £25,072 a year, or £2,089 a month, in England, Wales or Northern Ireland in 2026/27 (no pension). Without a student loan it would be £25,120 a year.

What interest is charged on a Plan 2 loan at £30,000?

From 1 September 2026 to 31 August 2027 Plan 2 interest after you leave your course is 4.18% at an income of £30,000: RPI of 4.1% up to £29,385 of income, rising towards RPI + 3% at £52,885, but capped at 6% for this academic year.

Will my student loan be written off on £30,000?

In our illustration a £45,000 balance, with pay starting at £30,000 and rising 3% a year, would be written off after 30 years on Plan 2 with about £104,159 left and written off after 40 years on Plan 5 with about £90,490 left. Your outcome depends on your real balance and future pay.

Does the amount I borrowed change my monthly repayment?

No. Repayments depend only on your income and plan threshold. A larger balance only changes how long you keep paying and whether anything is left to be written off.
