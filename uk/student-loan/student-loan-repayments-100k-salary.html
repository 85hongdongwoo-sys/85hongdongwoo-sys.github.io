How the £100,000 student loan repayment is worked out

The calculation is the same for every employer. For Plan 2, the 2026/27 threshold is £29,385 a year, or £2,448.75 a month. Monthly pay on £100,000 is £8,333.33, so the amount above the threshold is £5,884.58. Nine per cent of that is £529.61, and payroll rounds down to the whole pound: £529 a month. Over twelve months that is £6,348 — a few pounds below the exact annual figure of £6,355 because of the rounding.

The £100,000 trap, plus a student loan. Between £100,000 and £125,140 the personal allowance is withdrawn at £1 for every £2 earned, so income tax alone works out at 60% on extra pay. Add 2% National Insurance and 9% student loan repayments and a Plan 2 graduate keeps only about 29p of each extra pound in this band — 23p with a Postgraduate Loan as well. Salary sacrifice into a pension is the standard way to bring pay back below £100,000, and it also cuts the student loan deduction because repayments are based on pay after salary sacrifice.

Take-home pay on £100,000 with a student loan

Student loan repayments come out of the same payslip as income tax and National Insurance, so the useful number is what lands in your bank account. With no student loan, a £100,000 salary leaves £68,557 a year after 2026/27 income tax and National Insurance (England, Wales and Northern Ireland, no pension). A Plan 2 loan reduces that to £62,209, and a Plan 5 loan to £61,813. If you also took a Postgraduate Loan, a further £4,728 a year comes off. Full details are on the £100,000 salary after tax page.

Is your balance going down? Interest vs repayments on £100,000

Repayments and interest are separate: you repay a percentage of income, while interest is charged on the whole balance. On an example balance of £45,000, a Plan 2 graduate on £100,000 is charged about £2,700 of interest this academic year at 6.00%, against repayments of £6,348 — so the balance falls by around £3,648. At £100,000 you are past the point (about £44,268) where the Plan 2 sliding scale reaches the temporary 6% cap; without the cap your rate would be 7.10%.

Will you pay it off before it is written off?

Because unpaid balances are cancelled, the real cost of a student loan is what you repay before the write-off date. We modelled a graduate who starts repaying in April 2026 with a £45,000 balance on £100,000, with pay rising 3% a year. On Plan 2 the loan is cleared in about 8 years, after repaying £56,566 in total. On Plan 5, with a lower threshold and a 40-year term, it is cleared in about 7 years, after repaying £50,347 in total. At this income both plans are likely to be repaid in full, so the interest rate matters more than the threshold.

A projection, not a forecast: real pay, inflation and future threshold decisions will differ. Plan 2 thresholds are frozen at £29,385 to April 2030 and then assumed to rise 3% a year.

Tips for graduates on £100k

Think before overpaying. Voluntary payments are allowed at any time with no penalty, but if your balance would be written off anyway, every pound you overpay is a pound you would never have had to pay. Read our overpayment guide first.
Moving abroad? Tell the SLC. Repayments stop coming out through PAYE when you leave UK employment. You must tell the Student Loans Company and send an overseas income assessment, or you risk fixed repayments and arrears.
Self-employed income counts too. Profits from self-employment are added to your income in Self Assessment, and 9% of the total above your threshold is due with your tax bill on 31 January.
Check your payslip shows the right plan. Your employer only knows the plan type HMRC or you tell them. If you started an English course after August 2023 but are being charged as Plan 2 (or the other way round), the deductions will be wrong — check the plan in your Student Loans Company online account.

FAQ

How much student loan do I pay on £100,000 a year?

In 2026/27 a £100,000 salary means monthly repayments of £548 on Plan 1, £529 on Plan 2, £496 on Plan 4 and £562 on Plan 5, plus £394 on a Postgraduate Loan if you have one. That is 9% (6% for the Postgraduate Loan) of income above each plan’s threshold.

What is my take-home pay on £100,000 with a Plan 2 student loan?

After income tax, National Insurance and Plan 2 repayments you take home about £62,209 a year, or £5,184 a month, in England, Wales or Northern Ireland in 2026/27 (no pension). Without a student loan it would be £68,557 a year.

What interest is charged on a Plan 2 loan at £100,000?

From 1 September 2026 to 31 August 2027 Plan 2 interest after you leave your course is 6.00% at an income of £100,000: RPI of 4.1% up to £29,385 of income, rising towards RPI + 3% at £52,885, but capped at 6% for this academic year.

Will my student loan be written off on £100,000?

In our illustration a £45,000 balance, with pay starting at £100,000 and rising 3% a year, would be cleared in about 8 years on Plan 2 and cleared in about 7 years on Plan 5. Your outcome depends on your real balance and future pay.

Does the amount I borrowed change my monthly repayment?

No. Repayments depend only on your income and plan threshold. A larger balance only changes how long you keep paying and whether anything is left to be written off.
