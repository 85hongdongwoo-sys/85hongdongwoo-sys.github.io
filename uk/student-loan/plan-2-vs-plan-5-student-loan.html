Plan 2 vs Plan 5 student loan explained

Plan 5 repays £395 a year more than Plan 2 on the same salary — and for 10 years longer.

Plan 5 charges less interest than Plan 2 but starts repayments earlier and lasts ten years longer. The threshold is £25,000 instead of £29,385, so on any salary above both a Plan 5 graduate repays £395 a year more, and the balance is written off after 40 years rather than 30. For most middle earners that means repaying far more over a lifetime, even though the interest rate is lower.

Why Plan 5 was introduced

Plan 5 came from the government’s 2022 response to the Augar review of post-18 education. Under Plan 2, official estimates suggested a large share of the money lent would never be repaid, because many graduates do not earn enough over 30 years to clear balances that keep growing at up to RPI + 3%. Plan 5 shifts that balance: interest is cut to RPI so balances no longer grow faster than inflation, but the lower threshold and 40-year term mean most graduates repay for most of their working lives. Tuition fees themselves were not cut, so balances at graduation are similar.

Monthly repayments: Plan 5 always costs more

Because both plans take 9%, the monthly difference is fixed by the gap between thresholds. Above £29,385, a Plan 5 graduate pays £395 a year more (about £33 a month). Between £25,000 and £29,385 the Plan 5 graduate repays and the Plan 2 graduate does not — on £28,000 that is £22 a month against nothing.

Interest: Plan 2 grows faster

From 1 September 2026, Plan 5 interest is 4.1% — the March 2026 RPI. Plan 2 is 4.1% for incomes up to £29,385, rising on a sliding scale to RPI + 3% (7.1%) at £52,885; for 2026/27 the government has capped it at 6%, which bites from about £44,268. On a £45,000 balance, a Plan 2 graduate earning £50,000 is charged about £2,700 of interest this year; the same balance on Plan 5 attracts £1,845. See how student loan interest is calculated.

Lower and middle earners repay much more under Plan 5 because they keep paying for 40 years. Can I switch from Plan 2 to Plan 5?

No. Your plan is fixed by when your course started, and there is no option to move between plans. The comparison is useful mainly for understanding your own loan — for example, a Plan 5 graduate is more likely to clear the balance and so has a stronger case for considering overpayments, while many Plan 2 graduates do not (see should I overpay my student loan?).

Which is “better”?

Neither plan is a commercial debt: repayments stop if your income falls, nothing is owed on credit files, and the remainder is cancelled. Plan 2 is more generous to people with modest or interrupted earnings — career breaks, part-time work, or long periods below £29,385. Plan 5 is more predictable for higher earners, whose balance no longer grows faster than inflation. The Plan 2 threshold freeze to April 2030 narrows the gap a little, because more of each Plan 2 graduate’s salary becomes repayable as wages rise.

FAQ

What is the difference between Plan 2 and Plan 5?

Plan 5 has a lower repayment threshold (£25,000 against £29,385 in 2026/27), charges interest at RPI only (4.1% from September 2026) instead of up to RPI + 3%, and is written off after 40 years instead of 30.

Is Plan 5 worse than Plan 2?

For most low and middle earners Plan 5 means repaying more over a lifetime, because repayments start earlier and last ten years longer. Very high earners may repay less under Plan 5 because of its lower interest rate.

How much more do I pay each month on Plan 5?

Above £29,385 a Plan 5 graduate repays £395 a year (about £33 a month) more than a Plan 2 graduate on the same salary.

Can I move from Plan 2 to Plan 5?

No. Your plan type is set by your course start date and cannot be changed.

Does Plan 5 interest depend on income?

No. Plan 5 interest is RPI for everyone (4.1% from 1 September 2026 to 31 August 2027), whether studying or working.
