Hong Life › UK calculators › Student loan repayments › When is my student loan written off?

UK student loans are written off after a fixed period counted from the April you were first due to repay: 25 years for Plan 1, 30 years for Plan 2, Plan 4 and the Postgraduate Loan, and 40 years for Plan 5. Older Plan 1 and Plan 4 loans are cancelled at age 65 instead. Any balance left is cancelled automatically, and loans are also cancelled if you die or become permanently unable to work because of disability.

Write-off rules by plan

  • Plan 1 (England and Wales): if your first loan was for the 2006/07 academic year or later, 25 years after the April you were first due to repay. If your loans were taken before 2006/07, the loan is cancelled when you turn 65. In Northern Ireland the dividing line is the 2007/08 academic year.
  • Plan 2: 30 years after the April you were first due to repay. Plan 2 repayments could not start before April 2016, so the earliest Plan 2 write-offs will be in April 2046.
  • Plan 4 (Scotland): 30 years after the April you were first due to repay for loans from 2007/08; for earlier loans, at age 65 or after 30 years, whichever comes first.
  • Plan 5: 40 years after the April you were first due to repay. A student who started in September 2023 on a three-year course is first due to repay in April 2027, so the loan would be cancelled in April 2067.
  • Postgraduate Loan: 30 years after the April you were first due to repay.

How likely is it that your loan will be written off?

That depends on balance, plan and lifetime earnings. Plan 2 combines high balances with interest of up to RPI + 3% and a 30-year term, so a large share of Plan 2 graduates are expected not to repay in full. Plan 5 was designed to change that, with a 40-year term and a lower threshold. Our salary articles include an illustrative projection — for example on £30,000 a £45,000 Plan 2 balance is written off with about £104,159 left, whereas on £80,000 it is cleared in about 12 years.

Do you pay tax on a written-off student loan?

No. A student loan write-off is not treated as income and there is no tax to pay when it is cancelled. It does not affect your credit file either, because student loans are not reported to credit reference agencies.

Other ways a loan is cancelled

Loans are cancelled on death; your family does not inherit the debt. They can also be cancelled if you become permanently unfit for work because of disability and receive certain disability-related benefits — you apply to the Student Loans Company with evidence. There is no write-off for bankruptcy: student loans are not included in bankruptcy or debt relief orders.

What to do as the date approaches

Check your balance and write-off date in your SLC online account. If you are close to repaying in full, the SLC may offer to move you to Direct Debit for the final period so you do not over-repay through payroll. If deductions continue after the loan is written off or fully repaid, claim a refund.

FAQ

When is a Plan 2 student loan written off?

30 years after the April you were first due to repay — usually the April after you left your course. The first Plan 2 write-offs will happen in April 2046.

When is a Plan 1 student loan written off?

For loans from the 2006/07 academic year onwards in England and Wales, 25 years after the April you were first due to repay. Older loans are cancelled at age 65.

When is a Plan 5 student loan written off?

40 years after the April you were first due to repay. For a three-year course starting in 2023, that is April 2067.

Do I pay tax when my student loan is written off?

No. The cancelled amount is not taxable income and does not appear on your credit file.

Is a student loan written off when you die?

Yes. Student loans are cancelled on death and are not passed on to family members.
