How the £55,000 student loan repayment is worked out

Here is the sum your payroll software does every month. For Plan 2, the 2026/27 threshold is £29,385 a year, or £2,448.75 a month. Monthly pay on £55,000 is £4,583.33, so the amount above the threshold is £2,134.58. Nine per cent of that is £192.11, and payroll rounds down to the whole pound: £192 a month. Over twelve months that is £2,304 — a few pounds below the exact annual figure of £2,305 because of the rounding.

Higher-rate taxpayer with a student loan. Above £50,270 you pay 40% income tax and 2% National Insurance on extra pay, and the 9% student loan takes the combined deduction on each additional pound to about 51% — or 57% with a Postgraduate Loan on top. This is why salary sacrifice into a pension is especially valuable for graduates: it lowers the pay that both tax and student loan repayments are calculated on, whereas a relief-at-source pension does not reduce student loan repayments.

Is your balance going down? Interest vs repayments on £55,000

Repayments and interest are separate: you repay a percentage of income, while interest is charged on the whole balance. On an example balance of £45,000, a Plan 2 graduate on £55,000 is charged about £2,700 of interest this academic year at 6.00%, against repayments of £2,304 — so the balance still grows by around £396. At £55,000 you are past the point (about £44,268) where the Plan 2 sliding scale reaches the temporary 6% cap; without the cap your rate would be 7.10%.

Will you pay it off before it is written off?

Monthly repayments tell only half the story — the other half is whether the balance ever reaches zero. We modelled a graduate who starts repaying in April 2026 with a £45,000 balance on £55,000, with pay rising 3% a year. On Plan 2 the loan is cleared in about 26 years, after repaying £95,901 in total. On Plan 5, with a lower threshold and a 40-year term, it is cleared in about 18 years, after repaying £59,144 in total. At this income both plans are likely to be repaid in full, so the interest rate matters more than the threshold.

A projection, not a forecast: real pay, inflation and future threshold decisions will differ. Plan 2 thresholds are frozen at £29,385 to April 2030 and then assumed to rise 3% a year.

Tips for graduates on £55k

Think before overpaying. Voluntary payments are allowed at any time with no penalty, but if your balance would be written off anyway, every pound you overpay is a pound you would never have had to pay. Read our overpayment guide first.
Moving abroad? Tell the SLC. Repayments stop coming out through PAYE when you leave UK employment. You must tell the Student Loans Company and send an overseas income assessment, or you risk fixed repayments and arrears.
Self-employed income counts too. Profits from self-employment are added to your income in Self Assessment, and 9% of the total above your threshold is due with your tax bill on 31 January.
Check your payslip shows the right plan. Your employer only knows the plan type HMRC or you tell them. If you started an English course after August 2023 but are being charged as Plan 2 (or the other way round), the deductions will be wrong — check the plan in your Student Loans Company online account.

FAQ

How much student loan do I pay on £55,000 a year?

In 2026/27 a £55,000 salary means monthly repayments of £210 on Plan 1, £192 on Plan 2, £159 on Plan 4 and £225 on Plan 5, plus £170 on a Postgraduate Loan if you have one. That is 9% (6% for the Postgraduate Loan) of income above each plan’s threshold.

What is my take-home pay on £55,000 with a Plan 2 student loan?

After income tax, National Insurance and Plan 2 repayments you take home about £40,153 a year, or £3,346 a month, in England, Wales or Northern Ireland in 2026/27 (no pension). Without a student loan it would be £42,457 a year.

What interest is charged on a Plan 2 loan at £55,000?

From 1 September 2026 to 31 August 2027 Plan 2 interest after you leave your course is 6.00% at an income of £55,000: RPI of 4.1% up to £29,385 of income, rising towards RPI + 3% at £52,885, but capped at 6% for this academic year.

Will my student loan be written off on £55,000?

In our illustration a £45,000 balance, with pay starting at £55,000 and rising 3% a year, would be cleared in about 26 years on Plan 2 and cleared in about 18 years on Plan 5. Your outcome depends on your real balance and future pay.

Does the amount I borrowed change my monthly repayment?

No. Repayments depend only on your income and plan threshold. A larger balance only changes how long you keep paying and whether anything is left to be written off.
