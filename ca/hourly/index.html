How the numbers are worked out

All figures use the 2026 rates published by the Canada Revenue Agency and Revenu Québec: federal brackets of 14%, 20.5%, 26%, 29% and 33% with a $16,452 basic personal amount; provincial brackets and credits for Ontario (including the surtax and Ontario Health Premium), British Columbia (including the B.C. tax reduction), Alberta and Quebec; CPP of 5.95% up to $74,600 plus CPP2 of 4% up to $85,000; and EI of 1.63% up to $68,900. Quebec uses QPP, a 1.30% EI rate, QPIP and the 16.5% federal abatement. These are estimates for a single employee with employment income only; refundable benefits such as the Canada Workers Benefit are not included.

Annual salary = hourly rate × weekly hours × 52. Vacation pay (at least 4%) and statutory holidays are assumed to be paid, so a 52-week year matches a salaried job. If you are paid only for hours worked and take unpaid time off, scale the figures down.

FAQ

How do I convert hourly to salary in Canada?

Multiply your hourly rate by the hours you work each week and then by 52. For a 37.5-hour week that is the rate × 1,950; for 40 hours, × 2,080. For example, $20 an hour is $39,000 a year at 37.5 hours or $41,600 at 40 hours.

$20 an hour is how much a year after tax?

$20 an hour is $39,000 a year at 37.5 hours a week. After 2026 tax, CPP and EI that is about $32,065 in Ontario, or $2,672 a month.

$25 an hour after tax in Ontario — how much?

$25 an hour on 37.5 hours a week is $48,750 a year, which leaves about $39,285 after tax in Ontario in 2026 — $3,274 a month or $1,511 biweekly.

What is the minimum wage in Canada in 2026?

There is no single national rate. The federal minimum for federally regulated workplaces is $18.15 (from 1 April 2026). Provincial general minimums include B.C. $18.25, Ontario $17.95 (from 1 October 2026), Quebec $16.60 and Alberta $15.00. Full time in Ontario, $17.95 leaves about $2,435 a month after tax.

What is the median hourly wage in Canada?

Based on ESDC’s 2026 wage thresholds (which equal the provincial median hourly wage plus 20%, using Statistics Canada data), the median is roughly $30.77 in Ontario, $32.00 in B.C., $31.25 in Alberta and $30.00 in Quebec. At $30.77 an hour full time, Ontario take-home is about $47,341 a year.
