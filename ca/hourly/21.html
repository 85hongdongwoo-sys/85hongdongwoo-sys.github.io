How $21 an hour is taxed in 2026

The federal lowest rate is 14% for 2026 — the first full year after it was cut from 15% on 1 July 2025 — on taxable income up to $58,523, then 20.5% to $117,045, 26% to $181,440, 29% to $258,482 and 33% above. The $16,452 basic personal amount, the Canada employment amount ($1,501) and credits for base CPP and EI are worth 14% each, leaving $2,814 of federal tax on $40,950. Ontario charges 5.05% up to $53,891, minus its own $12,989 basic amount, plus an Ontario Health Premium of $450. Payroll deductions are CPP at 5.95% of earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 ($2,228) and EI at 1.63% up to $68,900 ($667). Of a $1 an hour raise ($1,950 a year) you would lose about 25% to tax and payroll deductions in Ontario.

Federal income tax only starts once annual income passes the $16,452 basic personal amount (plus CPP/EI credits) — at $21 that is roughly 15.1 hours a week, though CPP and EI are deducted from the first hour above the $3,500 CPP exemption. The 20.5% federal bracket begins at about 53.6 hours a week ($58,523). EI is deducted on every dollar all year, because you stay under the $68,900 maximum insurable earnings.

Above minimum wage, below the middle. $21 is $3.05 above Ontario’s $17.95 minimum and $2.75 above B.C.’s $18.25 — the highest provincial minimum. On 37.5 hours a week that premium over Ontario’s minimum is worth $5,948 a year before tax. It is still roughly $9.77 an hour below the estimated Ontario median of about $30.77.

$21 an hour after tax by province

Federal tax is the same everywhere except Quebec, where a 16.5% abatement lowers it but a separate, higher provincial tax applies. On $40,950, Alberta leaves the most ($34,017) and Quebec the least ($32,728) of the four largest provinces — a gap of $1,289 a year, or $107 a month. Quebec workers also pay QPP (6.3% instead of 5.95% CPP), a lower EI rate (1.30%) and QPIP parental insurance (0.430%).

Tips to keep more of $21 an hour

Fill in your TD1 forms. New hires should complete federal and provincial TD1 forms. Claiming the basic personal amount on two jobs at once leads to under-withholding and a tax bill in April; with two jobs, claim it on only one.
Model your own paycheque. Weekly or biweekly pay, RRSP deductions and bonuses change withholding. The Ontario take-home pay calculator handles these for your exact salary.
Check your vacation and holiday pay. Hourly workers in Ontario, B.C. and Alberta earn at least 4% vacation pay (6% after five years with the same employer); Quebec gives 4% and 6% after three years. Public holiday pay is on top — look for both lines on your pay stub.
Know your overtime threshold. Overtime at 1.5× starts after 44 hours a week in Ontario and Alberta (Alberta also after 8 hours a day), after 8 hours a day or 40 a week in B.C. and federally regulated jobs, and after 40 hours a week in Quebec. One overtime hour at $21 is $31.50 gross, about $23.57 after deductions in Ontario.
Open (or top up) a TFSA. The 2026 Tax-Free Savings Account limit is $7,000, and unused room carries forward. Interest and growth inside a TFSA are never taxed, which makes it the first stop for an emergency fund.

$21 an hour is how much a year in Canada?

At 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, $21 an hour is $40,950 a year before tax. At 40 hours a week it is $43,680, at 30 hours $32,760 and at 20 hours $21,840.

$21 an hour after tax in Ontario — how much is it?

In 2026 a full-time $21 an hour job (37.5 hours a week) leaves about $33,524 a year in Ontario after $2,814 federal tax, $1,716 Ontario tax and health premium, $2,228 CPP and $667 EI. That is $2,794 a month, $1,289 biweekly, or $17.19 an hour after tax.

How much is $21 an hour biweekly after tax?

About $1,289 every two weeks in Ontario, $1,308 in B.C., $1,308 in Alberta and $1,259 in Quebec, on 37.5 hours a week. Gross biweekly pay is $1,575.

Is $21 an hour a good wage in Canada?

$21 is 17% above Ontario’s $17.95 minimum wage and 32% below the estimated Ontario median hourly wage of about $30.77 (derived from ESDC’s 2026 thresholds based on Statistics Canada data).

How much is $21 an hour part-time?

At 20 hours a week $21 is $21,840 a year ($1,650 a month after tax in Ontario); at 30 hours it is $32,760 ($2,296 a month).

How do I convert hourly to salary in Canada?

Multiply the hourly rate by your weekly hours and then by 52. For a 37.5-hour week that is the rate × 1,950; for 40 hours, × 2,080. This assumes paid vacation and statutory holidays, so it matches a salaried job with the same hours.
