How $37 an hour is taxed in 2026

The federal lowest rate is 14% for 2026 — the first full year after it was cut from 15% on 1 July 2025 — on taxable income up to $58,523, then 20.5% to $117,045, 26% to $181,440, 29% to $258,482 and 33% above. The $16,452 basic personal amount, the Canada employment amount ($1,501) and credits for base CPP and EI are worth 14% each, leaving $7,700 of federal tax on $72,150. Ontario charges 5.05% up to $53,891, 9.15% to $107,785, minus its own $12,989 basic amount, plus an Ontario Health Premium of $600. Payroll deductions are CPP at 5.95% of earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 ($4,085) and EI at 1.63% up to $68,900 ($1,123). Of a $1 an hour raise ($1,950 a year) you would lose about 42% to tax and payroll deductions in Ontario, partly because the Ontario Health Premium steps up in this income range.

Federal income tax only starts once annual income passes the $16,452 basic personal amount (plus CPP/EI credits) — at $37 that is roughly 8.6 hours a week, though CPP and EI are deducted from the first hour above the $3,500 CPP exemption. The 20.5% federal bracket begins at about 30.4 hours a week ($58,523) — below a normal full-time week. EI premiums max out at 35.8 hours a week ($68,900 of insurable earnings), so you hit the annual maximum before December.

Well above the median. $37 an hour is about 20% more than the estimated Ontario median of $30.77 and 2.1× the province’s minimum wage. At this level you pay the maximum EI premium (EI stops at $68,900 of insurable earnings) and most or all of the CPP and CPP2 maximums, so payroll deductions stop part-way through the year and your last paycheques are bigger.

$37 an hour after tax by province

Federal tax is the same everywhere except Quebec, where a 16.5% abatement lowers it but a separate, higher provincial tax applies. On $72,150, British Columbia leaves the most ($55,791) and Quebec the least ($52,296) of the four largest provinces — a gap of $3,495 a year, or $291 a month. Quebec workers also pay QPP (6.3% instead of 5.95% CPP), a lower EI rate (1.30%) and QPIP parental insurance (0.430%).

Tips to keep more of $37 an hour

Use RRSP contributions to cut tax. An RRSP contribution is deducted from income, so at this pay level each $1,000 contributed saves about $284 of federal and Ontario income tax. Your limit is 18% of last year’s earned income (2026 maximum $33,810), minus pension adjustments.
Watch the $58,523 federal line. Income above $58,523 is taxed federally at 20.5% instead of 14%. An RRSP or FHSA contribution that brings taxable income back under that line saves the higher rate.
Ask for a raise in hourly terms. A $1 an hour raise is worth $1,950 a year before tax on a 37.5-hour week and about $1,130 after tax in Ontario — a concrete number for a pay review.
Fill in your TD1 forms. New hires should complete federal and provincial TD1 forms. Claiming the basic personal amount on two jobs at once leads to under-withholding and a tax bill in April; with two jobs, claim it on only one.
Consider an FHSA if you rent. The First Home Savings Account lets first-time buyers contribute $8,000 a year (up to $40,000), deductible like an RRSP and tax-free when used to buy a qualifying home.

$37 an hour after tax in Ontario — how much is it?

In 2026 a full-time $37 an hour job (37.5 hours a week) leaves about $55,198 a year in Ontario after $7,700 federal tax, $4,045 Ontario tax and health premium, $4,085 CPP and $1,123 EI. That is $4,600 a month, $2,123 biweekly, or $28.31 an hour after tax.

How much is $37 an hour biweekly after tax?

Is $37 an hour a good wage in Canada?

How much is $37 an hour part-time?

How do I convert hourly to salary in Canada?
