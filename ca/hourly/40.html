How $40 an hour is taxed in 2026

The federal lowest rate is 14% for 2026 — the first full year after it was cut from 15% on 1 July 2025 — on taxable income up to $58,523, then 20.5% to $117,045, 26% to $181,440, 29% to $258,482 and 33% above. The $16,452 basic personal amount, the Canada employment amount ($1,501) and credits for base CPP and EI are worth 14% each, leaving $8,849 of federal tax on $78,000. Ontario charges 5.05% up to $53,891, 9.15% to $107,785, minus its own $12,989 basic amount, plus an Ontario Health Premium of $750. Payroll deductions are CPP at 5.95% of earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 plus CPP2 at 4% up to $85,000 ($4,366) and EI at 1.63% up to $68,900 ($1,123). Of a $1 an hour raise ($1,950 a year) you would lose about 32% to tax and payroll deductions in Ontario.

Federal income tax only starts once annual income passes the $16,452 basic personal amount (plus CPP/EI credits) — at $40 that is roughly 7.9 hours a week, though CPP and EI are deducted from the first hour above the $3,500 CPP exemption. The 20.5% federal bracket begins at about 28.1 hours a week ($58,523) — below a normal full-time week. EI premiums max out at 33.1 hours a week ($68,900 of insurable earnings), so you hit the annual maximum before December.

Well above the median. $40 an hour is about 30% more than the estimated Ontario median of $30.77 and 2.2× the province’s minimum wage. At this level you pay the maximum EI premium (EI stops at $68,900 of insurable earnings) and most or all of the CPP and CPP2 maximums, so payroll deductions stop part-way through the year and your last paycheques are bigger.

$40 an hour after tax by province

Federal tax is the same everywhere except Quebec, where a 16.5% abatement lowers it but a separate, higher provincial tax applies. On $78,000, British Columbia leaves the most ($59,778) and Quebec the least ($55,793) of the four largest provinces — a gap of $3,985 a year, or $332 a month. Quebec workers also pay QPP (6.3% instead of 5.95% CPP), a lower EI rate (1.30%) and QPIP parental insurance (0.430%).

Tips to keep more of $40 an hour

Open (or top up) a TFSA. The 2026 Tax-Free Savings Account limit is $7,000, and unused room carries forward. Interest and growth inside a TFSA are never taxed, which makes it the first stop for an emergency fund.
Use RRSP contributions to cut tax. An RRSP contribution is deducted from income, so at this pay level each $1,000 contributed saves about $285 of federal and Ontario income tax. Your limit is 18% of last year’s earned income (2026 maximum $33,810), minus pension adjustments.
Ask for a raise in hourly terms. A $1 an hour raise is worth $1,950 a year before tax on a 37.5-hour week and about $1,317 after tax in Ontario — a concrete number for a pay review.
Fill in your TD1 forms. New hires should complete federal and provincial TD1 forms. Claiming the basic personal amount on two jobs at once leads to under-withholding and a tax bill in April; with two jobs, claim it on only one.
Consider an FHSA if you rent. The First Home Savings Account lets first-time buyers contribute $8,000 a year (up to $40,000), deductible like an RRSP and tax-free when used to buy a qualifying home.

$40 an hour is how much a year in Canada?

At 37.5 hours a week for 52 weeks, $40 an hour is $78,000 a year before tax. At 40 hours a week it is $83,200, at 30 hours $62,400 and at 20 hours $41,600.

$40 an hour after tax in Ontario — how much is it?

In 2026 a full-time $40 an hour job (37.5 hours a week) leaves about $58,952 a year in Ontario after $8,849 federal tax, $4,710 Ontario tax and health premium, $4,366 CPP and $1,123 EI. That is $4,913 a month, $2,267 biweekly, or $30.23 an hour after tax.

How much is $40 an hour biweekly after tax?

About $2,267 every two weeks in Ontario, $2,299 in B.C., $2,283 in Alberta and $2,146 in Quebec, on 37.5 hours a week. Gross biweekly pay is $3,000.

Is $40 an hour a good wage in Canada?

$40 is 123% above Ontario’s $17.95 minimum wage and 30% above the estimated Ontario median hourly wage of about $30.77 (derived from ESDC’s 2026 thresholds based on Statistics Canada data).

How much is $40 an hour part-time?

At 20 hours a week $40 is $41,600 a year ($2,834 a month after tax in Ontario); at 30 hours it is $62,400 ($4,074 a month).

How do I convert hourly to salary in Canada?

Multiply the hourly rate by your weekly hours and then by 52. For a 37.5-hour week that is the rate × 1,950; for 40 hours, × 2,080. This assumes paid vacation and statutory holidays, so it matches a salaried job with the same hours.
