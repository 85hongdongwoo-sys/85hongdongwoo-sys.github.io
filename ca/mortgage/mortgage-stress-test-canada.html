How the mortgage stress test works

The stress test is a qualification rule, not a rate you pay. When a federally regulated lender (all big banks and most others) approves a mortgage, it recalculates your payment at the minimum qualifying rate (MQR) and checks that payment against your income. For uninsured mortgages the rule is OSFI Guideline B-20; for insured mortgages the Department of Finance applies the same formula. OSFI has kept the 5.25% floor in place since June 2021.

Qualifying rate = max(5.25%, contract rate + 2.00%)

At today’s typical rates the “+2” leg always wins: 4.94% + 2 = 6.94% for a fixed rate and 3.95% + 2 = 5.95% for a variable. The 5.25% floor only bites when contract rates fall below 3.25%.

Example: a $500,000 mortgage

A $500,000 mortgage over 25 years at 4.94% costs $2,891 a month. For approval, the lender plugs in 6.94%: $3,484 a month, $593 more. Add property tax of $4,000 a year and $100 a month of heat, and the GDS ratio needs $121,000 of gross income — compared with $102,000 if you were tested at the real rate.

GDS and TDS: the two ratios

  • Gross debt service (GDS) = (qualifying mortgage payment + property tax + heating + 50% of condo fees) ÷ gross income. CMHC maximum: 39%.
  • Total debt service (TDS) = GDS costs + all other debt payments (car loans, credit cards at 3% of the balance, lines of credit, student loans) ÷ gross income. CMHC maximum: 44%.

Most lenders apply similar limits to uninsured loans. In practice, a buyer with no other debt hits the GDS limit first. Other debt payments only reduce what you can borrow once they exceed the 5-point gap between the two limits — 5% of gross income — so a $500-a-month car loan (6% of a $100,000 income) costs borrowing power at modest incomes but often none at high ones.

Exceptions and recent changes

Since 21 November 2024 OSFI no longer requires the stress test when an uninsured borrower simply switches lenders at renewal without increasing the loan or amortization (a “straight switch”), so you can shop your renewal freely. Renewing with your existing lender has never required re-qualification. Credit unions regulated only by a province are not bound by B-20, though many apply a similar test. The stress test still applies to every new purchase and refinance.

How to pass the stress test

Pay down other debts. When TDS is the binding limit, every $100 a month of debt removed raises the maximum mortgage by roughly $14,000 under TDS.
Consider a 30-year amortization. If you are a first-time buyer, buying a new build, or putting 20% down, 30 years lowers the qualifying payment by about 6%.
Add a co-borrower. Two incomes are combined for GDS and TDS.
Compare variable rates. A lower contract rate means a lower qualifying rate (5.95% vs 6.94%), though the payment can change with prime.

FAQ

What is the mortgage stress test rate in Canada?

The greater of 5.25% or your contract rate plus 2 percentage points. With a typical 4.94% 5-year fixed that is 6.94%; with a 3.95% variable it is 5.95%.

Does the stress test apply to insured mortgages?

Yes. Insured mortgages (less than 20% down) and uninsured mortgages from federally regulated lenders both qualify at the greater of 5.25% or the contract rate plus 2%.

How much income do I need to pass the stress test on a $500,000 mortgage?

About $121,000 with no other debts, assuming a 4.94% 25-year fixed, $4,000 property tax and $100/month heating, under the 39% GDS limit.

Do I have to pass the stress test when I renew?

No when you renew with your current lender. Since 21 November 2024 an uninsured straight switch to a new lender at renewal is also exempt, as long as the loan amount and amortization do not increase.

What are the GDS and TDS limits?

For insured mortgages CMHC allows a gross debt service ratio of up to 39% and a total debt service ratio of up to 44% of gross income, calculated with the stress-test payment.
