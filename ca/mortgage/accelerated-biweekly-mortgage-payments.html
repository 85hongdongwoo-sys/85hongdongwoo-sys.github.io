Accelerated bi-weekly mortgage payments are half your monthly payment every two weeks. Because there are 26 bi-weekly periods in a year, you pay the equivalent of 13 monthly payments instead of 12. On a $500,000 mortgage at 4.94% over 25 years that clears the loan in about 21 yrs 6 mo and saves roughly $59,000 of interest.

Why “accelerated” makes the difference

Regular bi-weekly payments are the monthly amount × 12 ÷ 26 — $1,334.29 — so you pay exactly the same per year and save only a little interest from paying slightly earlier. Accelerated bi-weekly is the monthly amount ÷ 2 — $1,445.49. The extra $2,891 a year goes entirely to principal, which is what shortens the amortization by years.

Is it worth it?

It matches many paycheques. If you are paid every two weeks, the payment lines up with your income, and two months a year you get a “third” paycheque that funds the extra payment.
It is not free money. You are simply paying $2,891 more a year. A lump-sum prepayment of the same amount each year saves a similar amount.
It doesn’t affect qualification. Lenders qualify you on the monthly payment, so choosing accelerated payments does not change the stress test.

You can usually switch frequency at any time without penalty. Combine it with the annual prepayment privilege (often 10–20% of the original principal) for even faster payoff.

FAQ

What is an accelerated bi-weekly mortgage payment?

Half of the monthly payment paid every two weeks. With 26 payments a year you pay the equivalent of 13 monthly payments, and the extra goes to principal.

How much does accelerated bi-weekly save?

On a $500,000 mortgage at 4.94% over 25 years, about $59,000 of interest, and the mortgage is paid off in about 21 yrs 6 mo instead of 25 years.

What is the difference between bi-weekly and accelerated bi-weekly?

Regular bi-weekly is the monthly payment × 12 ÷ 26 ($1,334.29 on $500,000), costing the same per year as monthly. Accelerated bi-weekly is the monthly payment ÷ 2 ($1,445.49).

Does accelerated bi-weekly affect my mortgage approval?

No. Lenders qualify you on the regular monthly payment under the stress test.

  • Typical rates: 4.94% 5-year fixed and 3.95% 5-year variable are the median of the 17 lenders listed in Ratehub.ca’s rate tables updated 2 November 2026 (fixed range 4.44%–5.99%, variable 3.68%–4.70%). Insured (high-ratio) mortgages often get slightly lower rates than uninsured ones.
  • Fixed-rate payments use Canadian semi-annual compounding (Interest Act); variable-rate payments use monthly compounding, which most lenders apply to variable mortgages. Rates are assumed constant for the whole amortization.
  • CMHC homeowner premiums on the loan amount: 4.00% above 90% loan-to-value, 3.10% for 85.01–90%, 2.80% for 80.01–85%, plus 0.20% for amortizations over 25 years. Provincial sales tax on the premium in Ontario (8%), Quebec (9%) and Saskatchewan (6%) must be paid in cash at closing.
  • Minimum down payment (Department of Finance rules from 15 December 2024): 5% of the first $500,000, 10% of the portion from $500,000 to $1.5 million; 20% at $1.5 million or more, where default insurance is not available. 30-year insured amortizations: first-time buyers and buyers of new builds.
  • Stress test: qualify at the greater of 5.25% or the contract rate + 2 points (OSFI Guideline B-20; the same floor applies to insured loans). Income needed uses CMHC’s 39% GDS / 44% TDS limits, property tax of 1% of the price a year and $100/month heating. Take-home pay: 2026 Ontario, from our Canada salary after tax engine.
