What a $700K house costs at today’s mortgage rates

The Bank of Canada raised its policy rate by a quarter point to 2.50% on 28 October 2026 (up from 2.25%), citing inflation of around 3% driven by high energy prices, and the big banks lifted prime to 4.70% the next day. Variable-rate payments follow prime, so a typical variable mortgage at prime − 0.75 (3.95%) now costs $3,577 a month on this loan. Fixed rates track 5-year Government of Canada bond yields instead; the median 5-year fixed among lenders on Ratehub.ca was 4.94% on 2 November 2026, with the cheapest insured offer at 4.44% ($3,748 a month). Every 1 percentage point on the fixed rate changes the payment by about $395 a month.

Around the national average. CREA’s national average sale price was $666,538 in September 2026 (and it forecasts $681,681 for 2026 as a whole), so a $700,000 home is 5% above the typical Canadian sale. The minimum down payment is now blended: 5% of the first $500,000 ($25,000) plus 10% of the remaining $200,000 ($20,000), for $45,000 — 6.4% of the price.

What income do you need for a $700,000 house?

The income you need is set by the stress test, not by the payment you will actually make. Your gross debt service (GDS) — the stress-tested mortgage payment plus property tax and heating — must be no more than 39% of gross income, and total debt service (TDS), which adds car loans, credit cards and lines of credit, no more than 44%. On a $681,200 mortgage, qualifying at 6.94% means a paper payment of $4,746, so you need about $167,000 a year with no other debts (one earner, or two on about $84,000 each).

Down payment and CMHC insurance on a $700,000 house

The minimum down payment on a $700,000 house is $45,000 (6.4%). Anything below 20% means the mortgage must carry default insurance from CMHC, Sagen or Canada Guaranty; the premium is a percentage of the loan and is usually added to the mortgage. The stress test on a $700,000 mortgage

Every federally regulated lender must check that you could still pay if rates rose. The qualifying rate is the greater of 5.25% or your contract rate plus 2 points. For a 4.94% fixed that is 6.94%, turning a $3,939 payment into a $4,746 test payment; for a 3.95% variable it is 5.95% ($4,338), which is why a variable rate needs about $13,000 less income to qualify.

Monthly vs accelerated bi-weekly payments

Canadian lenders offer monthly, semi-monthly, bi-weekly and weekly payments. Plain bi-weekly ($1,818 × 26) costs the same per year as monthly. Accelerated bi-weekly is half the monthly payment — $1,969 — every two weeks; because there are 26 pay periods you make the equivalent of 13 monthly payments a year, and on this $681,200 mortgage that saves about $81,000 of interest and 3 yrs 6 mo.

After the first 5-year term

Canadian mortgages are renewed every few years, so the rate above is only locked for the term. Over the first five years you would pay $236,319, of which $157,463 is interest, and still owe about $602,000 at renewal. At a renewal rate 1 point higher, the remaining 20 years would cost $4,270 a month. At the 30-year amortization the balance after five years is higher, at $626,000.

Tips before you buy a $700K home

Compare insured rates. With less than 20% down your mortgage is insured, and insured mortgages are usually priced lower than uninsured ones. On a $681,200 mortgage, 0.25 points is worth about $97 a month.
Use the FHSA and Home Buyers’ Plan. A First Home Savings Account takes up to $8,000 a year ($40,000 lifetime) of tax-deductible contributions, and the RRSP Home Buyers’ Plan lets each first-time buyer withdraw up to $60,000. Together they can cover much of a down payment.
Budget for closing costs. Lenders want to see roughly 1.5% of the price in cash on top of the down payment for land transfer tax, legal fees, title insurance and adjustments. Provincial sales tax on the CMHC premium (Ontario, Quebec, Saskatchewan) is also due at closing.
Use your prepayment privileges. Most fixed mortgages let you prepay 10–20% of the original balance each year without penalty. An extra $200 a month on this loan at 4.94% cuts about $51,000 of interest over 25 years.
Plan for renewal, not just today. After the first 5-year term you will still owe about $602,000. Renewal rates could be higher or lower; check what your payment would be at +1 point ($4,334/month at today’s balance).

FAQ

What is the monthly mortgage payment on a $700,000 house in Canada?

About $3,939 a month with the minimum $45,000 down payment, a 4.94% 5-year fixed rate and a 25-year amortization (typical rate on Ratehub.ca, 2 November 2026). With 20% down it is $3,238. At a 3.95% variable rate the starting payment is $3,577.

What is the minimum down payment for a $700,000 house?

$45,000: 5% of the first $500,000 ($25,000) plus 10% of the remaining $200,000 ($20,000). That is 6.4% of the price.

How much income do I need for a $700,000 house in Canada?

About $167,000 of gross household income with no other debts, assuming the minimum down, a 4.94% 5-year fixed rate qualified at 6.94%, property tax of about $7,000 a year and $100 a month for heating, using the 39% GDS limit.

How much is CMHC insurance on a $700,000 house?

With the minimum $45,000 down the loan-to-value is 93.6%, so the premium is 4.00% of the $655,000 loan: $26,200, added to the mortgage. It is $19,530 with 10% down and nothing with 20% down. Ontario, Quebec and Saskatchewan also charge sales tax on the premium.

How much do accelerated bi-weekly payments save on a $700,000 house?

Paying $1,969 every two weeks instead of $3,939 monthly pays the mortgage off in about 21 yrs 6 mo instead of 25 years and saves about $81,000 of interest, if the 4.94% rate stayed the same.

Is a 30-year amortization available on a $700,000 house?

With less than 20% down, only if you are a first-time buyer or buying a newly built home (rules since 15 December 2024). The payment would be $3,618 instead of $3,939, including a 0.20% higher CMHC premium. With 20% down a 30-year amortization is widely available: $2,969.
