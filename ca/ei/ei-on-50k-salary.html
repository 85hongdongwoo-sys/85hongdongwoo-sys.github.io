How the $50K EI amount is worked out

The 2026 calculation is the same for everyone outside the fishing program: your insurable earnings, the number of “best weeks” for your region, and the 55% rate. Insurable earnings are your gross pay, including overtime, commissions and vacation pay, up to $68,900 a year — so your whole $50,000 salary counts. Divided over the year that is $961.54 a week, and 55% of it is $528.85, which rounds to $529. You would need $68,900 or more to reach the $729 maximum, $200 a week higher than your rate.

Over a month that is about $2,292 before tax, against $4,167 of gross salary — a big gap to plan for, with EI replacing 55% of gross pay and 62% of after-tax pay.

What if you only worked part of the year?

EI does not divide by 52. It adds up your earnings in your best weeks — 21 weeks in Toronto right now, from 14 in high-unemployment regions to 22 where unemployment is 6% or less — and divides by that number. With a steady $50,000 salary every week is the same, so any 21 weeks give $962. But if you had worked only 17 weeks (680 hours) before losing the job, Service Canada would still divide by 21: 17 × $962 ÷ 21 = $778, and 55% of that is $428 a week instead of $529. You would also qualify for only 15 weeks of benefits in Toronto.

2026 temporary EI rules that help a $50K earner

EI Pilot Project No. 25 took effect on 11 October 2026. For claims that start by 9 October 2027, the one-week waiting period is waived, so your first week of unemployment is paid (worth $529 to you), and severance or vacation pay on separation does not delay or reduce your EI. Long-tenured workers can get up to 20 extra weeks on claims starting by 12 June 2027 — in Toronto that could take a full-year claim from 38 to 58 weeks ($30,682). And only the reason you left your most recent job is now assessed. See the waiting period and 2026 measures.

Is EI taxed on a $50,000 salary?

Yes. EI benefits are taxable income and Service Canada deducts federal and provincial income tax from every payment, but no CPP contributions or EI premiums. Annualizing a $529 weekly benefit gives $27,508 a year; at 2026 federal and Ontario rates with basic personal amounts that suggests about $50 a week withheld, leaving roughly $479. In January you receive a T4E slip; because your job income for the year is taxed separately, the real tax on your EI depends on how many months you worked — a mid-year layoff can produce a refund or a balance owing.

Working part-time while on EI

You can take part-time or casual work and keep part of your EI. The rule since 2018: you keep 50 cents of benefits for every dollar you earn, until your earnings reach 90% of the weekly insurable earnings your claim is based on ($865 here); above that, benefits are cut dollar for dollar. Two days a week at your old pay ($380) would reduce your $529 benefit by $190 to $339, for a total of $719 that week.

Maternity, parental and sickness EI on $50,000

The same $529 weekly rate applies to EI maternity, standard parental and sickness benefits, because they use the same 55% formula and the same $729 ceiling. Extended parental benefits stretch the money over more weeks at 33%, which on $50,000 is $317 a week.

Will you have to repay EI on a $50,000 salary?

The EI “clawback” applies to regular benefits when your 2026 net income is above $86,125: you repay 30% of the lesser of your regular benefits and the excess. Take a layoff on 30 June with 12 weeks of severance ($11,538) and 26 weeks of EI for the rest of the year ($13,754 — severance is not deducted from EI for 2026 claims). Net income for the year would be about $50,292: $35,833 under the threshold, so nothing would be repaid. Maternity, parental and sickness benefits are never clawed back.

Tips if you are claiming EI on a $50K salary

Ask about severance timing. In 2026 separation money (severance, pay in lieu of notice, vacation pay on separation) is not deducted from EI for claims that start by 9 October 2027 — but it still counts as income for tax and for the repayment test.
Check your region every four weeks. Your hours requirement, best weeks and maximum weeks are fixed by the regional unemployment rate in force on the week your claim starts, and those rates are updated every four weeks.
Look at the 20 extra weeks. Long-tenured workers — people who paid at least 30% of the maximum premium in 7 of the last 10 years and drew fewer than 36 weeks of regular EI in the past 3 years — can get up to 20 extra weeks on claims starting by 12 June 2027.
Keep a job-search log. You must be ready, willing and capable of working each day and actively looking. Note employers contacted, applications and interviews; Service Canada can ask for them.
Apply even before your Record of Employment arrives. Apply within four weeks of your last day of work. If you wait longer you can lose benefits; Service Canada can get the ROE electronically from your employer after you apply.

FAQ

How much EI will I get on a $50,000 salary?

About $529 a week before tax in 2026: 55% of average insurable weekly earnings of $962 ($50,000 ÷ 52). After income tax is withheld, roughly $479 a week.

How much EI do I get every two weeks on $50,000 a year?

EI is paid every two weeks, so about $1,058 before tax, or about $959 after the estimated tax withheld.

How many weeks of EI can I get with a $50,000 salary?

It depends on your insurable hours and your region, not your salary. After a full year of full-time work: Toronto 38 weeks, Montréal 40 weeks, Vancouver 38 weeks, Calgary 38 weeks (rates for 11 October to 7 November 2026). The national range is 14 to 45 weeks.

Is EI taxable on a $50,000 salary?

Yes. Service Canada withholds income tax from each payment — about $50 a week on a $529 benefit by our Ontario estimate — and sends a T4E slip. No CPP or EI premiums are deducted from benefits.

How much maternity leave EI on $50,000?

$529 a week for 15 weeks of maternity benefits, then $529 a week for up to 35 weeks of standard parental benefits or $317 a week for up to 61 weeks of extended parental benefits (outside Quebec).

Will I have to pay back EI on a $50,000 salary?

Only if your 2026 net income exceeds $86,125, and then 30% of the lesser of your regular benefits or the excess. In our example (laid off mid-year with 12 weeks' severance) income is about $50,292, so nothing is repaid.
