You can get between 14 and 45 weeks of EI regular benefits. The number comes from the Table of Weeks of Benefits in the Employment Insurance Act: find the row for the insurable hours you worked in the last 52 weeks and the column for your region’s unemployment rate. After a full year of full-time work you get the maximum for your region — currently 38 weeks in Toronto, 38 in Calgary and 45 in high-unemployment regions.

Two inputs decide the length

Insurable hours in the qualifying period and the regional rate for the week your claim starts (rates for 11 October to 7 November 2026).

Maximum weeks of EI in major cities

The table rewards both long attachment to work and living where jobs are scarce. At 6% unemployment or less you need 700 hours and get 14 to 36 weeks; where unemployment is above 16% you need only 420 hours and get 32 to 45 weeks. Each percentage point of unemployment adds two weeks, and every extra 35 to 70 hours you worked adds roughly another week.

How to read your own number

Count your insurable hours on all your Records of Employment in the last 52 weeks (full time is about 35 to 40 hours a week, so six months is roughly 900 to 1,050 hours). Find your region by postal code on Service Canada’s site and read off the rate. For example, 1,050 hours in Toronto (6.9%) gives 21 weeks; the same hours in London (8.7%) give 25 weeks; in rural Newfoundland and Labrador (11.6%), 31.

Extra weeks in 2026–2027

  • Long-tenured workers: up to 20 additional weeks for claims starting by 12 June 2027 (EI Pilot Project No. 25), if you paid at least 30% of the maximum annual premium in 7 of the last 10 years and received fewer than 36 weeks of regular benefits in the last 156 weeks. Entitlement can then reach 65 weeks.
  • Seasonal workers in 13 designated regions can get up to 5 extra weeks under a separate pilot if they have a history of seasonal claims; check eligibility with Service Canada.
  • Sickness, maternity and parental weeks are fixed (26, 15, 35/61) and don’t use this table — but they can be combined with regular benefits on the same claim, up to set limits.

FAQ

How many weeks of EI can I get?

Between 14 and 45 weeks of regular benefits, depending on your insurable hours in the last 52 weeks and the unemployment rate in your EI economic region.

How many hours do I need to qualify for EI?

Between 420 and 700 insurable hours for regular benefits, depending on your regional unemployment rate; 600 hours for sickness, maternity, parental and caregiving benefits.

How many weeks of EI in Toronto?

With rates for 11 October to 7 November 2026 (6.9%), you need 665 hours and can get 15 to 38 weeks; a full year of full-time work gives 38 weeks.

Can I get more than 45 weeks of EI?

Temporarily, yes: long-tenured workers can get up to 20 extra weeks on claims starting by 12 June 2027.

How often do EI regional rates change?

Every four weeks. The rate that applies is the one in force for the week your benefit period begins.

Assumptions & sources

  • Benefit rate and maximums: Canada.ca “EI regular benefits: How much you could receive” and the maternity/parental and sickness benefit-amount pages — 55% of average insurable weekly earnings; maximum yearly insurable earnings $68,900 from 1 January 2026 ($65,700 in 2025); maximum $729 a week ($695 in 2025); extended parental 33%, maximum $437.
  • Best weeks (14 to 22) and the weeks you can be paid come from the regional unemployment rate: Employment Insurance Act, Schedule I, and Service Canada’s “EI Program Characteristics” for 11 October to 7 November 2026 (rates are updated every four weeks).
  • 2026 temporary measures: Canada Gazette Part II, SOR/2026-203 (EI Pilot Project No. 25, in force 11 October 2026) — waiting week waived and separation money not deducted for claims starting up to 9 October 2027; up to 20 extra weeks for long-tenured workers for claims starting up to 12 June 2027; only the most recent separation assessed.
  • Repayment (clawback): 30% of the lesser of regular benefits or net income above $86,125 (2026 = 1.25 × maximum insurable earnings). Premiums 2026: 1.63% of insurable earnings (Quebec 1.30%), maximum $1,123.07.
  • Tax withheld is our estimate: the weekly benefit annualized through 2026 federal and Ontario tax brackets with basic personal amounts (and the Ontario Health Premium).
