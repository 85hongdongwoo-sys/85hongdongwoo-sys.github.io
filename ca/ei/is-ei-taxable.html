Yes, all EI benefits are taxable income in Canada. Service Canada deducts federal and provincial income tax from each payment and sends you a T4E slip after the year ends. No CPP contributions or EI premiums are deducted. On the 2026 maximum of $729 a week, our estimate of the tax withheld in Ontario is about $90 a week, leaving roughly $639.

Why you might get a refund — or owe tax

Withholding treats EI as your only income, but most people also had a job earlier in the year. Example: a $64,000 earner works 8 months ($42,667), then gets 16 weeks of EI at $677 ($10,832). Their employer withheld tax as if they earned $64,000 for the full year, and Service Canada withheld about $1,245 on the EI. Because total income for the year is only $53,499, they will often get a refund in spring. The opposite happens when you go back to a well-paid job late in the year, or when severance pushes your income up — and if net income passes $86,125, the EI repayment also applies.

The T4E slip

  • Box 14 shows total benefits paid; box 22 the income tax deducted.
  • Box 7 shows the repayment rate (30%) when regular benefits may be subject to the clawback.
  • Benefits go on line 11900 of your return. T4Es are available in My Service Canada Account and CRA My Account by the end of February.

EI vs your pay stub

On a job, your pay stub shows income tax, CPP contributions and EI premiums. On EI only income tax comes off, so the gap between EI and your old take-home pay is smaller than the gap in gross pay. On a $64,000 salary, Ontario take-home is about $960 a week; EI after tax is about $599 — 62% of it.

FAQ

Is EI taxable in Canada?

Yes. All EI benefits — regular, sickness, maternity, parental and caregiving — are taxable income. Federal and provincial income tax is deducted from each payment.

How much tax is taken off EI?

It depends on the weekly amount and your province. Our Ontario estimate is about $90 a week on the $729 maximum and about $44 on a $500 benefit.

Are CPP and EI premiums deducted from EI benefits?

No. Only income tax is withheld from EI benefits.

What is a T4E?

The tax slip Service Canada issues for EI benefits, showing benefits paid, tax deducted and any repayment rate.

Will I owe tax on EI at tax time?

Possibly. Withholding assumes EI is your only income. If other income for the year is high, or your net income exceeds $86,125, you may owe more; after a mid-year layoff you often get a refund.

  • Benefit rate and maximums: Canada.ca “EI regular benefits: How much you could receive” and the maternity/parental and sickness benefit-amount pages — 55% of average insurable weekly earnings; maximum yearly insurable earnings $68,900 from 1 January 2026 ($65,700 in 2025); maximum $729 a week ($695 in 2025); extended parental 33%, maximum $437.

  • 2026 temporary measures: Canada Gazette Part II, SOR/2026-203 (EI Pilot Project No. 25, in force 11 October 2026) — waiting week waived and separation money not deducted for claims starting up to 9 October 2027; up to 20 extra weeks for long-tenured workers for claims starting up to 12 June 2027; only the most recent separation assessed.
