How these EI numbers are worked out

Every article uses one set of inputs checked against official sources on 3 November 2026: the 2026 maximum insurable earnings of $68,900 and maximum weekly benefit of $729 from Canada.ca, the Table of Weeks of Benefits in Schedule I of the Employment Insurance Act, the regional unemployment rates Service Canada publishes for 11 October to 7 November 2026, and the temporary measures in EI Pilot Project No. 25 (Canada Gazette, 7 October 2026). Take-home pay comparisons use our 2026 Ontario salary engine. EI is decided claim by claim, so treat every figure as an estimate and check your own claim in My Service Canada Account.

A quick rule of thumb: weekly EI ≈ salary ÷ 52 × 0.55, capped at $729. On $50,000 that is $529; on $60,000, $635. Above $68,900 the answer is always $729.

FAQ

How much EI will I get in 2026?

55% of your average insurable weekly earnings, up to a maximum of $729 a week. Insurable earnings are capped at $68,900 a year, so anyone earning that or more gets $729.

What is the maximum EI benefit in 2026?

$729 a week for regular, sickness, maternity and standard parental benefits (up from $695 in 2025), and $437 a week for extended parental benefits.

How many weeks of EI can I get?

Between 14 and 45 weeks of regular benefits, depending on your insurable hours in the last 52 weeks and the unemployment rate in your EI economic region. Sickness benefits last up to 26 weeks.

Is there still a one-week waiting period for EI?

Not for most claims right now: EI Pilot Project No. 25 waives the waiting week for claims that start between 11 October 2026 and 9 October 2027.

Do I have to pay tax on EI?

Yes, EI benefits are taxable and income tax is withheld from each payment. Regular benefits may also be partly repaid if your 2026 net income is over $86,125.

Assumptions & sources

  • Benefit rate and maximums: Canada.ca “EI regular benefits: How much you could receive” and the maternity/parental and sickness benefit-amount pages — 55% of average insurable weekly earnings; maximum yearly insurable earnings $68,900 from 1 January 2026 ($65,700 in 2025); maximum $729 a week ($695 in 2025); extended parental 33%, maximum $437.
  • Best weeks (14 to 22) and the weeks you can be paid come from the regional unemployment rate: Employment Insurance Act, Schedule I, and Service Canada’s “EI Program Characteristics” for 11 October to 7 November 2026 (rates are updated every four weeks).
  • 2026 temporary measures: Canada Gazette Part II, SOR/2026-203 (EI Pilot Project No. 25, in force 11 October 2026) — waiting week waived and separation money not deducted for claims starting up to 9 October 2027; up to 20 extra weeks for long-tenured workers for claims starting up to 12 June 2027; only the most recent separation assessed.
  • Repayment (clawback): 30% of the lesser of regular benefits or net income above $86,125 (2026 = 1.25 × maximum insurable earnings). Premiums 2026: 1.63% of insurable earnings (Quebec 1.30%), maximum $1,123.07.
  • Tax withheld is our estimate: the weekly benefit annualized through 2026 federal and Ontario tax brackets with basic personal amounts (and the Ontario Health Premium).
