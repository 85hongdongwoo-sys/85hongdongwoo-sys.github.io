EI repayment (clawback) threshold 2026

Net income over $86,125? You may repay 30% of your regular EI benefits at tax time.

If your 2026 net income is more than $86,125, you repay 30% of whichever is smaller: your regular EI benefits for the year, or the amount by which your net income exceeds $86,125. The threshold is 1.25 times the $68,900 maximum insurable earnings (it was $82,125 for 2025). Special benefits — maternity, parental, sickness, caregiving — are never repaid, and anyone who received less than one week of regular benefits in the previous 10 tax years is exempt.

Repayment in one line

Repay = 30% × lesser of (regular EI, net income − $86,125).

The repayment can never exceed 30% of your regular benefits, so the most anyone repays on $12,000 of EI is $3,600. Net income here is line 23400 of your tax return — after deductions such as RRSP contributions, union dues and child care expenses — so an RRSP contribution can reduce or eliminate the clawback.

Worked example: severance plus EI

A worker earns $75,000 by the end of August, is laid off with $15,000 of severance and collects $729 a week of EI for 16 weeks ($11,664). Because 2026 claims are not reduced by severance, all three amounts land in the same tax year: net income $101,664, which is $15,539 over the threshold. Repayment: 30% × $11,664 = $3,499. A $16,000 RRSP contribution would bring net income under the line and save it entirely.

Who is exempt

  • Infrequent claimants: if you received less than one week of regular benefits in the 10 tax years before 2026, you repay nothing on 2026 regular benefits. Note the test rolls forward: a claim that spans 2026 and 2027 can be exempt in 2026 but not in 2027.
  • Special benefits: maternity, parental, sickness, compassionate care and family caregiver benefits are excluded from the calculation.

How it is collected

Your T4E shows your benefits and, in box 7, the 30% repayment rate if your regular benefits may be repayable. You calculate the amount on the social benefits repayment chart when you file, and it is entered on line 42200 of your return; it is also deductible on line 23500 so it is not taxed twice. If you expect to owe it, you can ask Service Canada to deduct extra tax from your EI payments, or set the money aside.

FAQ

What is the EI clawback threshold for 2026?

$86,125 of net income (1.25 times the $68,900 maximum insurable earnings).

How much EI do I have to repay?

30% of the lesser of your regular EI benefits for the year and your net income above $86,125.

Do I have to repay maternity or parental EI?

No. Special benefits — maternity, parental, sickness and caregiving — are not subject to the repayment.

Who doesn’t have to repay EI?

People who received less than one week of regular benefits in the 10 tax years before the year in question.

Can RRSP contributions reduce the EI clawback?

Yes. The test uses net income after deductions, so RRSP contributions lower it, and with it the repayment.
