You can work part-time while on EI and keep 50 cents of your benefits for every dollar you earn, until your earnings reach 90% of the weekly earnings your claim is based on. Above that cap, every extra dollar earned is taken off your benefit. There is no fixed dollar limit: on a $52,000 salary ($1,000 a week) you could earn up to $900 in a week under the 50% rule.

How the deduction is calculated

EI paid = weekly benefit − 50% × earnings (up to 90% of previous weekly earnings) − 100% × earnings above that cap

“Previous weekly earnings” means the average insurable weekly earnings used to set your benefit rate. The rule has been permanent since August 2018 and applies to regular, sickness, parental, caregiving and fishing benefits. On maternity benefits, earnings are deducted dollar for dollar.

Reporting work and earnings

  • Report gross earnings (before deductions) for the week you worked, not the week you were paid.
  • Report every day of work in your bi-weekly report, even if you haven’t been paid yet; an estimate is fine and can be corrected later.
  • Self-employment and commission income count too, as do tips; for full-time self-employment, you may be considered not unemployed at all.
  • Undeclared earnings found later create an overpayment and can bring penalties and a higher hours requirement on future claims.

Does working part-time reduce your total EI?

Partly paid weeks still use up one week of your entitlement, while weeks with no EI paid do not. So a part-time job can stretch your claim over more calendar weeks. Severance and vacation pay on separation are separate: for claims starting by 9 October 2027 they are not deducted at all under the 2026 pilot. If you return to full-time work, stop claiming — and remember your remaining weeks may still be available within the benefit period if the job ends.

FAQ

How much can I earn while on EI?

There is no fixed dollar limit. You keep 50 cents of EI for every dollar you earn up to 90% of your previous weekly earnings; above that, benefits are reduced dollar for dollar.

Does working part-time while on EI reduce my benefits?

Yes, by 50 cents per dollar earned up to the cap. On a $550 benefit, earning $400 reduces EI by $200 to $350, for $750 in total.

Can I work while on maternity leave EI?

You can, but earnings while on maternity benefits are deducted dollar for dollar. The 50 cents rule applies to parental benefits.

Do I report earnings when I work or when I get paid?

Report gross earnings for the week in which you did the work, even if payday is later.

Does working while on EI extend my claim?

Weeks where earnings reduce your EI to zero are generally not counted against your weeks of entitlement, so they can be used later within the 52-week benefit period.
