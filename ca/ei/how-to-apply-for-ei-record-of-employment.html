Apply for EI online as soon as you stop working, and no later than four weeks after your last day of work, or you may lose benefits. You don’t need to wait for your Record of Employment (ROE): employers who file electronically send it to Service Canada directly. If you are eligible, the first payment usually arrives within 28 days of applying — and for claims starting by 9 October 2027 your first week is paid because the waiting period is waived.

Before you apply: what you need

  • Your Social Insurance Number (SIN), and your mother’s maiden name if applicable.
  • Your mailing and home address, including postal code (it sets your EI economic region).
  • Names, addresses and dates of employment for all employers in the last 52 weeks, and the reason each job ended.
  • Your banking information for direct deposit.
  • For sickness benefits: your doctor’s details; for maternity/parental: the due date or birth date and your partner’s SIN if sharing.
  • Details of any money you received or will receive on separation (severance, vacation pay, pay in lieu of notice).

Step by step

  1. Apply online on Canada.ca (about 60 minutes). You can apply for several benefit types on one application; you get a confirmation number at the end.
  2. Your employer issues the ROE. Employers filing electronically must do so within 5 calendar days after the end of the pay period in which your earnings stopped; Service Canada then gets it automatically. You can view it in My Service Canada Account.
  3. Get your access code. A letter with your 4-digit access code arrives by mail; you need it for your reports.
  4. Receive the decision. Service Canada may call you about the reason you left or your availability. Answer honestly and keep notes.
  5. Complete bi-weekly reports online or by phone, every two weeks, declaring work, earnings, training, travel and availability. You are paid only for weeks you report.
  6. Get paid every two weeks by direct deposit, with tax already withheld.

The Record of Employment explained

The ROE is the form that tells Service Canada how many insurable hours and how much insurable earnings you had, and why your job ended. Key blocks: insurable hours (block 15A), insurable earnings by pay period (block 15C), and the reason code (block 16) — for example A for shortage of work, D for illness, E for quit, F for maternity, M for dismissal. The code is the employer’s description, not a decision: if you disagree, say so in your application. If an employer won’t issue an ROE, apply anyway and Service Canada can use pay stubs and other evidence.

Common mistakes that delay or cost benefits

Waiting too long. If you apply more than four weeks after your last day, you may lose benefits for the weeks before you applied. You can ask for the claim to be backdated only with good cause.
  • Not reporting part-time earnings, or reporting them for the week you were paid rather than worked.
  • Leaving Canada without telling Service Canada (you generally can’t receive regular benefits while abroad).
  • Missing a bi-weekly report — payments stop until it’s done.
  • Forgetting to apply when a contract ends because “there may be more work” — you can always stop claiming if the job comes back.

FAQ

How do I apply for EI in 2026?

Apply online on Canada.ca as soon as you stop working, and within four weeks of your last day of work. You need your SIN, employment history for the last 52 weeks and banking details.

Do I need my Record of Employment to apply for EI?

No. Apply without it; employers who file electronically send the ROE to Service Canada directly.

When will I get my first EI payment?

Usually within 28 days of applying if you are eligible and your ROE is available. For claims starting by 9 October 2027, the waiting week is waived.

How long does an employer have to issue an ROE?

For electronic ROEs, 5 calendar days after the end of the pay period in which the interruption of earnings occurred.

How often do I report while on EI?

Every two weeks, online or by phone, using the access code mailed to you.
