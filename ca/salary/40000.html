How a $40,000 salary is taxed in Canada in 2026

Federal income tax. Outside Quebec your taxable income is $39,635 (salary minus the deductible enhanced part of CPP). The 2026 federal brackets are 14% up to $58,523, 20.5% to $117,045, 26% to $181,440, 29% to $258,482 and 33% above; the lowest rate fell from 15% to 14% from July 2025, so 2026 is the first full year at 14%. After the $16,452 basic personal amount, the Canada employment amount and CPP/EI credits, federal tax is $2,691 — the same in every province except Quebec ($2,228 after the abatement).

CPP and EI. CPP takes 5.95% of earnings above $3,500 — $2,172 at this salary. EI takes 1.63%, or $652. Quebec workers pay QPP ($2,300), EI at 1.30% ($520) and QPIP ($172) instead.

Province matters. Provincial tax on $40,000 runs from $701 to $2,751. Nunavut leaves the most take-home and Nova Scotia the least — a gap of $2,051 a year, or $171 a month.

A raise from here. Going from $40,000 to $45,000 in Ontario adds $3,741 of take-home — you keep about 75 cents of each extra dollar.

FAQ

How much is $40,000 after tax in Canada?

In 2026 a $40,000 salary leaves about $32,814 in Ontario, $33,333 in British Columbia, $33,332 in Alberta and $32,088 in Quebec after income tax, CPP/QPP and EI. Across all 13 provinces and territories take-home ranges from $31,734 in Nova Scotia to $33,784 in Nunavut.

How much is $40,000 a year per month after tax?

About $2,734 a month in Ontario, $2,778 in B.C., $2,778 in Alberta and $2,674 in Quebec. Gross monthly pay is $3,333.

$40,000 a year is how much an hour?

$40,000 is $19.23 an hour before tax at 40 hours a week for 52 weeks. After tax that is about $15.78 an hour in Ontario and $16.24 in Nunavut.

Which province has the highest take-home pay on $40,000?

Nunavut leaves the most ($33,784), followed by Northwest Territories ($33,365) and British Columbia ($33,333). Nova Scotia leaves the least ($31,734), a difference of $2,051 a year.

What is the marginal tax rate on $40,000 in Ontario?

Including CPP and EI, about 25.2% of the next dollar earned goes to deductions in Ontario. The federal bracket for this income is 14.0%.
