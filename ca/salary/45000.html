How a $45,000 salary is taxed in Canada in 2026

Federal income tax. Outside Quebec your taxable income is $44,585 (salary minus the deductible enhanced part of CPP). The 2026 federal brackets are 14% up to $58,523, 20.5% to $117,045, 26% to $181,440, 29% to $258,482 and 33% above; the lowest rate fell from 15% to 14% from July 2025, so 2026 is the first full year at 14%. After the $16,452 basic personal amount, the Canada employment amount and CPP/EI credits, federal tax is $3,338 — the same in every province except Quebec ($2,765 after the abatement).

CPP and EI. CPP takes 5.95% of earnings above $3,500 — $2,469 at this salary. EI takes 1.63%, or $733. Quebec workers pay QPP ($2,615), EI at 1.30% ($585) and QPIP ($194) instead.

Province matters. Provincial tax on $45,000 runs from $886 to $3,462. Nunavut leaves the most take-home and Nova Scotia the least — a gap of $2,577 a year, or $215 a month.

A raise from here. Going from $45,000 to $50,000 in Ontario adds $3,591 of take-home — you keep about 72 cents of each extra dollar.

FAQ

How much is $45,000 after tax in Canada?

In 2026 a $45,000 salary leaves about $36,554 in Ontario, $36,872 in British Columbia, $36,937 in Alberta and $35,456 in Quebec after income tax, CPP/QPP and EI. Across all 13 provinces and territories take-home ranges from $34,997 in Nova Scotia to $37,574 in Nunavut.

How much is $45,000 a year per month after tax?

About $3,046 a month in Ontario, $3,073 in B.C., $3,078 in Alberta and $2,955 in Quebec. Gross monthly pay is $3,750.

$45,000 a year is how much an hour?

$45,000 is $21.63 an hour before tax at 40 hours a week for 52 weeks. After tax that is about $17.57 an hour in Ontario and $18.06 in Nunavut.

Which province has the highest take-home pay on $45,000?

Nunavut leaves the most ($37,574), followed by Northwest Territories ($37,067) and Alberta ($36,937). Nova Scotia leaves the least ($34,997), a difference of $2,577 a year.

What is the marginal tax rate on $45,000 in Ontario?

Including CPP and EI, about 25.2% of the next dollar earned goes to deductions in Ontario. The federal bracket for this income is 14.0%.
