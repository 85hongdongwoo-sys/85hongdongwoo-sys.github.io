What these numbers include

  • Federal income tax — 2026 brackets of 14%, 20.5%, 26%, 29% and 33% (thresholds $58,523, $117,045, $181,440, $258,482), basic personal amount $16,452 (reduced to $14,829 above $181,440), Canada employment amount $1,501, and credits for CPP/EI.
  • Provincial and territorial tax — each jurisdiction's 2026 brackets and basic personal amount, plus the Ontario surtax and Health Premium, the Ontario and B.C. tax reductions, and Quebec's deduction for workers and 16.5% federal abatement.
  • CPP / QPP — 5.95% (QPP 6.3%) on earnings from $3,500 to $74,600 and 4% CPP2/QPP2 up to $85,000. The enhanced portion is deducted from income; the base portion earns a credit.
  • EI and QPIP — 1.63% to $68,900 (1.30% in Quebec) and QPIP 0.430% to $103,000 in Quebec.

Not included: RRSP and pension plan contributions, union dues, benefits, refundable credits and other low-income reductions. Data as of 2026-11-03.

FAQ

How is take-home pay calculated in Canada?

Take-home pay is salary minus federal income tax, provincial or territorial income tax, CPP (QPP in Quebec) and EI premiums (plus QPIP in Quebec). Each tax is calculated on brackets, then reduced by non-refundable credits such as the basic personal amount and the credits for CPP and EI.

Which province has the lowest taxes on salary in 2026?

At $60,000 Nunavut leaves the most take-home ($48,773) and Nova Scotia the least ($44,726). At $100,000 the highest is Nunavut ($76,654) and the lowest Nova Scotia ($68,867).

What changed in Canadian income tax for 2026?

The lowest federal rate is 14% for the full year (it was 15% until June 2025), brackets rose 2% for inflation, and the federal basic personal amount is $16,452. B.C. raised its lowest rate to 5.60%, P.E.I. added a 20% bracket over $200,000, and Newfoundland and Labrador raised its basic personal amount to $13,094.

What are the CPP and EI rates for 2026?

Employees pay CPP of 5.95% on earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 plus CPP2 of 4% up to $85,000 (maximum $4,646.45). EI is 1.63% on earnings up to $68,900 (maximum $1,123.07), or 1.30% in Quebec.
