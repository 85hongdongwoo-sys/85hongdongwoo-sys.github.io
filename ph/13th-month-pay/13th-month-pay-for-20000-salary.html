A ₱20,000 monthly basic salary earned for all twelve months means a ₱20,000 13th month pay. The amount drops only if you worked fewer months or had unpaid days — each unpaid day costs about ₱63.90 on a 6-day week.

Worked example

Paolo is a warehouse checker in Calamba, Laguna, on ₱20,000 a month with a 6-day week. His employer uses the 313-day factor, so his daily rate is ₱20,000 × 12 ÷ 313 = ₱766.77.

In April his father was hospitalised in Samar and Paolo took 4 days off without pay after using up his service incentive leave. His basic pay for the year is ₱240,000 − 4 × ₱766.77 = ₱236,932.91. His 13th month: ₱19,744.41, about ₱255.59 less than a coworker with perfect attendance. The paid SIL days themselves don't reduce anything — they were paid as salary.

How much do unpaid absences really cost?

Less than people fear. A day of leave without pay removes one day's pay from your basic salary, and the 13th month is one-twelfth of that — so each unpaid day costs you only about ₱63.90 of 13th month pay on ₱20,000. Tardiness and undertime deductions work the same way: they lower basic salary earned, and the 13th month follows.

What employers cannot do is withhold the whole 13th month as a penalty for absences, or make it conditional on “perfect attendance”. Attendance bonuses are separate and voluntary; the 13th month is mandatory.

Tax: the ₱90,000 ceiling at ₱20,000

A full-year 13th month of ₱20,000 is entirely exempt. You still have ₱70,000 of tax-free room for a Christmas bonus, productivity incentive or other benefits paid in 2026. A ₱10,000 Christmas bonus would still leave ₱60,000 of room.

The 13th month is never subject to SSS, PhilHealth or Pag-IBIG contributions. Tax estimates assume a full year at ₱20,000 with employee contributions of about ₱1,700 a month deducted.

Things worth knowing

Ask HR whether they use the 261-day (5-day week) or 313-day (6-day week) factor. It changes your daily rate and therefore how much an absence costs.
Money claims, including unpaid 13th month pay, prescribe after three years. If an old employer still owes you for 2024, act before the end of 2027.
Resigning in December? Your 13th month for January to your last day is still due — and if you leave before the 24th, it's part of your final pay.

FAQ

How much is 13th month pay for ₱20,000 salary?

₱20,000 for a full year with no unpaid absences. For partial years, multiply ₱1,666.67 by the months worked.

Can my employer deduct absences from my 13th month pay?

Only indirectly: unpaid days reduce the basic salary you earned, so the 13th month is computed on the lower total. They cannot cancel the 13th month or impose an attendance condition on it.

How is 13th month pay computed on a ₱20,000 salary?

Add up the basic salary you earned from January to December and divide by 12. For a full year at ₱20,000 that gives ₱20,000; each month worked adds ₱1,666.67.

Is 13th month pay subject to SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG?

No. Contributions are based on regular monthly compensation; the 13th month is not part of it.
