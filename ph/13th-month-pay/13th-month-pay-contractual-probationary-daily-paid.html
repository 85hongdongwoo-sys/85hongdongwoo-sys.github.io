13th month pay for contractual, probationary and daily-paid workers

Who PD 851 covers — and the short list of people it doesn’t.

Employment status doesn't matter. DOLE's advisories say all rank-and-file employees are entitled “regardless of their position, designation or employment status, and irrespective of the method by which their wages are paid”, if they worked at least one month in the year. That includes probationary, contractual, project, seasonal, part-time, agency-hired, piece-rate and daily-paid workers.

Covered vs not covered

Covered by PD 851

  • Regular and probationary employees
  • Contractual, project and seasonal workers
  • Part-time employees
  • Agency/contractor-hired workers (paid by the contractor)
  • Daily-paid and hourly-paid workers
  • Piece-rate workers
  • Employees with fixed pay plus commission (on the basic part, at least)
  • Those with several employers — each pays its share
  • Resigned or terminated employees (pro-rated)

Outside PD 851

  • Managerial employees
  • Government employees (year-end bonus system)
  • Workers paid purely on commission, boundary or task basis
  • Those who worked less than one month in the year
  • Household workers — now covered by the Kasambahay Law instead

The “not covered” list comes from the 1987 Revised Guidelines. Note that the guidelines also exempted employers already paying an equivalent benefit — a Christmas bonus of at least one month's basic salary counts as compliance, but a smaller one doesn't.

Three quick examples

Daily-paid: ₱650 a day, 5 days a week

Rey works Monday–Friday at an auto parts shop. He was paid for 245 working days in 2026 (holiday pay excluded, three unpaid days). Basic: 245 × ₱650 = ₱159,250. 13th month: ₱13,270.83.

Part-time: four hours a day

Celine is a part-time library assistant at a private college, paid ₱9,000 a month for half days. Twelve months gives ₱108,000 of basic pay and a 13th month of ₱9,000. Part-time status cuts the base, not the entitlement.

Agency worker, two assignments

Ronnie is employed by a manpower agency and deployed to a supermarket (January–June, ₱16,000) and then a warehouse (July–December, ₱17,000). His employer is the agency for the whole year, so it owes (6 × ₱16,000 + 6 × ₱17,000) ÷ 12 = ₱16,500. If the agency doesn't pay, the principals are jointly liable under DOLE's contracting rules.

“We only give 13th month to regulars”

That policy breaks the law. The one-month minimum is the only service requirement. A probationary employee hired on 1 November and still employed on 1 December has earned a 13th month for 2026.

Several employers at once

Someone who works mornings for one company and evenings for another gets a 13th month from each, computed on what each one pays. The ₱90,000 tax exemption, however, is per person per year — two concurrent employers means you'll file your own annual income tax return.

Supervisors are not managers

A frequent misunderstanding: the 13th month isn't only for the lowest-paid staff. “Rank-and-file” in PD 851 means everyone who isn't a managerial employee. Team leaders and supervisors who don't make management policy or hire and fire are covered.

FAQ

Are contractual employees entitled to 13th month pay?

Yes. Contractual, project and seasonal employees are covered if they are rank-and-file and worked at least one month in the calendar year.

Do part-time workers get 13th month pay?

Yes, computed on the basic pay they actually earned. A part-timer earning ₱9,000 a month all year gets ₱9,000.

Who pays 13th month pay for agency workers?

The contractor or agency, as the employer. Under DOLE rules on contracting, the principal is jointly and severally liable if the contractor fails to pay.

Who is not entitled to 13th month pay under PD 851?

Government employees (who receive a year-end bonus instead), managerial employees, and workers paid purely by commission, boundary or task without a fixed wage. Kasambahay are covered separately under RA 10361.

Are piece-rate workers covered?

Yes. Their 13th month is one-twelfth of total earnings for the year, excluding overtime and premium pay.
