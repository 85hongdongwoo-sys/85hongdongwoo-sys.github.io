₱35,000 a month for twelve months = a ₱35,000 13th month pay. This page looks at it from the employer's side too: what to accrue each month, how to handle a team with different start dates, and what goes in the DOLE report.

Worked example

Joy handles payroll for a 14-person architecture firm in Mandaluyong. Three people earn ₱35,000: one has been there for years, one was hired on 1 April, and one left on 15 July after 6½ months.

Her working: the long-timer gets ₱35,000. The April hire earned 9 × ₱35,000 = ₱315,000, so ₱26,250. The July leaver earned 6 months plus half of July — ₱210,000 + ₱17,500 = ₱227,500 — so ₱18,958.33, paid in her final pay. Joy accrues ₱2,916.67 per head per month in the books so December doesn't hit cash flow all at once.

For employers: accrual and the DOLE report

Accruing one-twelfth of basic payroll monthly is the cleanest way to budget. At ₱35,000, that's ₱2,916.67 per employee per month, or ₱40,833.33 a month for a team of 14 on the same rate.

After paying, employers file a compliance report through DOLE's online reporting portal by 15 January of the following year. It asks for the establishment's details, number of workers who benefited, the amount granted per employee and the total. The 13th month is deductible as a business expense and, up to the ₱90,000 ceiling, does not need income tax withheld.

Is it taxed?

A full-year 13th month of ₱35,000 is entirely exempt. You still have ₱55,000 of tax-free room for a Christmas bonus, productivity incentive or other benefits paid in 2026. A ₱10,000 Christmas bonus would still leave ₱45,000 of room.

The 13th month is never subject to SSS, PhilHealth or Pag-IBIG contributions. Tax estimates assume a full year at ₱35,000 with employee contributions of about ₱2,825 a month deducted.

Quick tips

Run the computation from your payroll register (basic pay column), not from the salary rate field — it automatically picks up unpaid leave and mid-month starts.
Money claims, including unpaid 13th month pay, prescribe after three years. If an old employer still owes you for 2024, act before the end of 2027.
Resigning in December? Your 13th month for January to your last day is still due — and if you leave before the 24th, it's part of your final pay.

FAQ

How much 13th month pay should an employer accrue per month for a ₱35,000 employee?

₱2,916.67 a month (₱35,000 ÷ 12). Over a full year that adds up to the ₱35,000 owed in December.

Do employers have to report 13th month pay to DOLE?

Yes. DOLE asks employers to submit a compliance report through its online reporting portal by 15 January of the following year.

Is 13th month pay subject to SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG?

No. Contributions are based on regular monthly compensation; the 13th month is not part of it.

When is the 13th month pay deadline in 2026?

On or before 24 December 2026 under PD 851. Employers may pay earlier, and may pay half before the school year opens and the rest by 24 December.

Sources

  • Presidential Decree No. 851 (1975) and the Revised Guidelines on the Implementation of the 13th Month Pay Law (1987): one-twelfth of basic salary earned in the calendar year, rank-and-file employees, at least one month of service, pay on or before 24 December.
  • DOLE Labor Advisory No. 13, Series of 2024 and No. 16, Series of 2025 (reported by PNA/SunStar, 29 Nov 2025): no exemption or deferment, “basic salary” definition and exclusions, compliance report through the DOLE online portal by 15 January.
  • National Internal Revenue Code Sec. 32(B)(7)(e) as amended by RA 10963 (TRAIN): 13th month pay and other benefits exempt up to ₱90,000 a year; graduated income tax rates in force since 1 January 2023. House Bill 7661 (₱150,000 cap) was still pending in November 2026.
  • Employee contributions used for tax estimates: SSS 5% of the monthly salary credit (₱5,000–₱35,000), PhilHealth 2.5% (5% shared, ₱10,000–₱100,000), Pag-IBIG ₱200.
