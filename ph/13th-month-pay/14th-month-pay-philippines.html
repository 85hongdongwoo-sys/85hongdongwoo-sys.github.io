Is there a 14th month pay in the Philippines?

Every December, the rumour comes back. Here’s where things stand.

No law requires a 14th month pay for private-sector employees as of November 2026. Only the 13th month is mandatory. Bills creating a 14th month have been filed in almost every Congress since the 2000s — including in the current 20th Congress — but none has been enacted. Some employees still receive one, by contract, CBA or company practice.

Myth vs fact

Myth

  • “A 14th month law was passed — employers must pay it this year.”
  • “If the company gave a 14th month once, it must always give it.”
  • “The 14th month is automatically tax-free.”

Fact

  • No 14th month law exists for the private sector as of November 2026.
  • A one-off bonus doesn't bind the employer. A deliberate, consistent practice over a long period, or a contract/CBA clause, does.
  • It shares the ₱90,000 ceiling with the 13th month and other benefits.

Where the bills stand

Proposals for a mandatory 14th month go back at least to the mid-2000s. Versions have typically required the 14th month to be paid by 31 May — partly to help families with school expenses — and some would move the 13th month deadline earlier too. In 2025 bills were refiled in both chambers of the 20th Congress. Business groups have argued that many small employers couldn't afford it, and DOLE has said in the past that it was studying feasibility. None of the bills has reached the President's desk.

When you are owed a 14th month

Contract or CBA

If your offer letter says “14 months' pay” or your CBA grants a mid-year bonus, it's enforceable like any other contract term.

Company practice

The Supreme Court has held that a benefit given voluntarily, deliberately and consistently over a long period becomes part of the employment terms. Employers can't withdraw it unilaterally (Article 100, non-diminution of benefits). A bonus that depended on profits or was clearly discretionary each year usually doesn't ripen.

Example. Ben works for a family-owned hardware chain in Bulacan on ₱30,000 a month. Every June for nine years, the company has paid a “14th month” equal to one month's basic salary, described in memos as an annual benefit. If it suddenly stops in 2026, Ben and his coworkers have a strong argument that it is a company practice — and a SEnA request is the place to start.

The government “14th month”

Government personnel receive a mid-year bonus of one month's basic pay in May and a year-end bonus plus ₱5,000 cash gift in November. News reports often call the May bonus a 14th month pay. It comes from budget laws and DBM circulars, not from PD 851, and doesn't apply to private companies.

Tax if you get both

On ₱50,000 a month, a 13th and a 14th month add up to ₱100,000 — ₱10,000 over the ceiling. At that salary your top tax rate is about 20%, so roughly ₱2,000 goes to tax.

FAQ

Is 14th month pay mandatory in the Philippines?

No. As of November 2026 no law requires private employers to pay a 14th month. Only the 13th month under PD 851 is mandatory.

Has the 14th month pay bill been passed?

No. Bills have been filed repeatedly in the Senate and House, including in the 20th Congress (2025–2028), but none has become law.

Can a 14th month pay become mandatory for my company?

Yes, if it is in your contract or CBA, or if your employer has given it deliberately and consistently for a long time, making it a company practice that cannot be withdrawn under Article 100.

Do government employees get a 14th month pay?

Government personnel receive a mid-year bonus of one month’s basic pay in May, which is often called a “14th month”, plus the year-end bonus and ₱5,000 cash gift.

Is a 14th month pay taxable?

It shares the ₱90,000 tax-free ceiling with the 13th month and other bonuses. Whatever exceeds ₱90,000 in total is taxable.
