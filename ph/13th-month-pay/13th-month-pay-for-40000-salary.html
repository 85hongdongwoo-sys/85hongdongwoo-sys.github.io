An example from payroll

Mark does IT support for a bank's back office in Ortigas, on ₱40,000 a month. In December he gets his 13th month of ₱40,000, a ₱10,000 Christmas bonus, and in June he received a ₱5,000 anniversary bonus.

All three go into the same ₱90,000 bucket: ₱40,000 + ₱10,000 + ₱5,000 = ₱55,000. That's ₱35,000 below the ceiling, so none of it is taxed and his December payslip shows no extra withholding. He could receive up to ₱35,000 more in bonuses this year before a single peso becomes taxable.

What else shares the ₱90,000 ceiling

The exemption isn't just for the 13th month. Under the tax code it covers the 13th month pay and other benefits — Christmas bonuses, productivity incentives, loyalty or anniversary bonuses, and the part of de minimis benefits above their own limits. Regular allowances that are part of your monthly compensation are not in the bucket; they're taxed with your salary.

At ₱40,000 a month, your taxable pay is roughly ₱444,600 a year after contributions, which puts you in the 20% bracket. So if your bonuses ever went over the ceiling, each extra ₱1,000 would cost you about ₱200 in tax.

Where ₱40,000 sits against the tax-free limit

A full-year 13th month of ₱40,000 is entirely exempt. You still have ₱50,000 of tax-free room for a Christmas bonus, productivity incentive or other benefits paid in 2026. A ₱10,000 Christmas bonus would still leave ₱40,000 of room.

The 13th month is never subject to SSS, PhilHealth or Pag-IBIG contributions. Tax estimates assume a full year at ₱40,000 with employee contributions of about ₱2,950 a month deducted.

Practical tips

Keep a running total of bonuses received during the year — if you change jobs, the ₱90,000 ceiling applies to you, not to each employer.
Keep the employer's computation. If you were paid less than one-twelfth of your basic salary for the year, a written query to HR settles most cases before DOLE is needed.
Payday falls on a weekend or holiday? 24 December 2026 is a Thursday, so there's no excuse for “we'll pay after Christmas”. Employers may — and many do — pay earlier.

FAQ

Is ₱40,000 13th month pay taxable?

No. It is below the ₱90,000 ceiling. Only if your 13th month plus other bonuses for the year exceed ₱90,000 is the excess taxed.

Does my Christmas bonus count toward the ₱90,000 limit?

Yes. The ceiling is shared by the 13th month and other benefits such as Christmas, productivity and anniversary bonuses.

When is the 13th month pay deadline in 2026?

On or before 24 December 2026 under PD 851. Employers may pay earlier, and may pay half before the school year opens and the rest by 24 December.

Do I need to work a minimum period to get it?

Yes — at least one month during the calendar year. Below that, there is no entitlement under PD 851.
